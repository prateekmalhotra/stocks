### 2. Critical Blind Spots & Strategic Risks to Address

#### A. Moat Durability & The "Women-First" Dilemma
* **The "First Move" Pivot:** The thesis highlights Bumble's "women-first" mechanic as a durable moat. However, the introduction of features like **"Opening Moves"** (allowing men to initiate responses to pre-set prompts) was driven by user feedback regarding "first-move fatigue" among women. 
* **The Dilemma:** By diluting the core structural differentiator to reduce friction, Bumble risks becoming another commoditized swiping app. The thesis should explicitly address whether Bumble still possesses a pricing/retention moat compared to Match Group’s portfolio (Hinge, Tinder) or newer entrants.

#### B. Secular Dating App Burnout vs. "Intentional Reset"
* The thesis attributes payer declines largely to a deliberate "quality reset" (purging low-intent accounts). 
* **Counter-Hypothesis:** The industry is experiencing structural **"Dating App Burnout"** among Gen Z and Millennials, who are increasingly fatigued by subscription paywalls and algorithmic matching, turning instead to organic social channels or real-life meetups. 
* *Recommendation:* Frame payer declines not just as an internal management choice, but assess the structural ceiling on freemium conversion across the online dating industry.

#### C. Regulatory & App Store Fee Assumptions
* The upside scenario models app store fee compression from ~21% down to ~19%. While the EU Digital Markets Act (DMA) and US antitrust scrutiny are ongoing, Apple and Google have largely offset direct commission reductions with alternative processing fees and core technology fees. 
* Assuming material fee compression adds unneeded beta risk to the Bull case; it is safer to keep commission friction static at ~21%–22%.
