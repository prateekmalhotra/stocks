Section 1: Business Model Reality & Post-Regulatory Pivot

Following China's 2021 "Double Reduction" (Shuang Jian) regulatory overhaul that prohibited for-profit academic tutoring for K-9 students, New Oriental Education & Technology Group Inc. (NYSE: EDU) executed one of the most successful corporate pivot strategies in modern emerging market history. Rather than winding down operations, New Oriental repurposed its physical real estate, digital assets, and brand goodwill into a diversified business model anchored by compliance-cleared educational offerings and high-engagement live-streaming e-commerce.

Core Revenue Engines

EDU's current operational structure generates cash flow across five distinct, complementary pillars:

  • Non-Academic Youth Enrichment & Tutoring: Focuses on non-subject-matter skill development, including coding/programming, robotics, scientific literacy, arts, public speaking, and physical sports. Designed to fully comply with regulatory guidelines, this segment has expanded across 60+ tier-1 and tier-2 cities, surpassing 1.0 million active student enrollments per quarter.
  • Intelligent Learning Systems & Devices: An AI-powered hardware-plus-SaaS model offering adaptive learning tablets and customized software platforms. Deployed in approximately 60 cities, this engine generates high-margin hardware sales and recurring software subscription revenue, with the top 10 market cities driving over 50% of segment revenue.
  • Overseas Test Preparation & Study Consulting: The legacy core of New Oriental, catering to high school and university students preparing for international standardized tests (TOEFL, IELTS, GRE, SAT) and overseas university placements. In Q4 FY2026, overseas test prep revenue expanded 6% YoY, while consulting rose ~1% YoY, demonstrating steady cash generation despite macroeconomic complexities.
  • Adult & University Student Services: Comprises post-secondary exam prep, domestic graduate entrance exam tutoring, civil service exam training, and professional licensing courses. This segment delivered a standout performance in Q4 FY2026, with revenue surging 29% YoY as young adults sought upskilling in a tight job market.
  • East Buy (01797.HK) Live-Streaming E-Commerce: A 57%-owned subsidiary that converted former star tutors into cultural, content-driven live-stream hosts selling private-label agricultural goods, food items, and lifestyle products.

East Buy Talent Restructuring & Segment Stabilization

The live-streaming e-commerce landscape has experienced notable executive and creator turnover. Following the late-2023 departure of top host Dong Yuhui (who launched "Time with Yuhui"), July 2026 saw former East Buy CEO Sun Dongxu, along with prominent live-stream hosts Shi Ming ("Mingming") and Guo Tianquan ("Tianquan"), officially leave to launch a competing livestreaming venture named "Beautiful Tomorrow" (Beautiful Tomorrow Technology Co., Ltd.).

In response to market concerns regarding talent attrition, New Oriental's leadership, led by Founder Yu Minhong, outlined a strategic framework to stabilize and future-proof East Buy:

  1. Transition from Key-Person Reliance to Institutional Brand Equity: East Buy is actively de-risking its reliance on individual "superstar" anchors by scaling an 18-channel Matrix account strategy on Douyin (TikTok China) and driving traffic toward its proprietary East Buy Mobile App (which features paid VIP membership perks).
  2. Private-Label SKU & Supply Chain Dominance: Management is shifting East Buy’s economic driver from talent-driven traffic to product excellence, expanding private-label inventory across high-frequency consumer goods, agricultural products, and cultural products to capture higher gross margins.
  3. Synergy with Offline Cultural Tourism: Integrating East Buy's e-commerce footprint with New Oriental’s expanding cultural tourism, study tours, and senior wellness travel ventures across 30 Chinese provinces and select international destinations.

Section 2: Competitive Moat & Unit Economics

Moat Architecture & Pricing Power

New Oriental holds a durable moat in China’s consumer education sector, underpinned by three key structural pillars:

  • Institutional Brand Equity & Regulatory Safety: In a post-regulatory market, parents prioritize compliance safety and financial stability when placing upfront tuition deposits. Having navigated decades of policy shifts, EDU's brand carries trust, allowing it to command premium pricing for its non-academic enrichment and intelligent learning device bundles without suffering high price elasticity.
  • Offline Network Scale & Capacity Utilization: With learning centers operating in over 60 cities, EDU enjoys structural cost advantages. As non-academic class fill rates exceed pre-pivot levels, operating leverage accelerates, spreading fixed center lease and administration overhead across higher student volumes.
  • Dramatically Lower Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC): Unlike pure-play digital competitors that rely on expensive performance marketing on social media platforms, EDU leverages organic multi-stage cross-selling. A child entering non-academic programming at age 7 naturally transitions into high school prep, university service, or overseas test prep, yielding higher Lifetime Value (LTV) relative to CAC.

Section 6: Thesis Confidence, Execution Risk & Pre-Mortem Falsification

Our overall thesis confidence in New Oriental Education is Moderate-High, supported by the company's strong brand equity, market leadership in non-academic enrichment, and net cash buffer. However, institutional investors must systematically monitor key operational and macro execution risks that could impair returns:

1. Operational Execution & Capacity Over-Expansion Risk

To capture non-academic demand, management has aggressively expanded physical learning center capacity. If student enrollment growth fails to keep pace with new lease commitments, elevated fixed operating overhead will compress operating margins. Management must maintain strict utilization hurdles (>65% seat capacity within 6 months of center opening) to prevent operational deleverage.

2. East Buy Livestreaming E-Commerce Fragility

The East Buy segment remains vulnerable to key-man risk, creator churn, and shifting algorithms on Chinese short-video platforms. While e-commerce provides top-line diversification, its lower gross margin profile and higher inventory/logistics commitments can dilute EDU's consolidated margin quality if e-commerce losses expand.

3. Regulatory Policy & Macro Environment

China's private education sector remains subject to administrative oversight. Although non-academic tutoring, artistic enrichment, and adult test preparation operate in clear regulatory alignment, localized policy interpretations or licensing freezes present unpredictable tail risks that require an elevated discount rate.

Margin of Safety Assessment

EDU trades at an NTM P/E of ~18.9x, but adjusting for its ~$3.0B+ in net cash, short-term investments, and long-term bank deposits, the core education business trades at an effective ex-cash NTM P/E of under 13x. This valuation discount provides a fundamental margin of safety, ensuring that even under moderate earnings hiccups, downside equity risk is muted.

Pre-Mortem Falsification Triggers

The fundamental investment thesis will be considered broken, triggering an immediate downgrade or position exit, if any of the following explicit conditions materialize:

  • Core Education Revenue Deceleration: Consolidated net revenue growth falling below 8% YoY for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural saturation in non-academic tutoring or market share loss to competitors.
  • East Buy Operating Loss Expansion: East Buy segment operating margin remaining negative or operating losses exceeding $30M annually, proving that e-commerce diversification is permanently value-destructive.
  • Regulatory Policy Reversal: Introduction of municipal or national restrictions targeting non-academic enrichment, smart learning hardware sales, or licensing freezes for physical learning centers.
  • Capital Allocation Degradation: Cancellation or significant curtailment of the share repurchase program accompanied by capital deployment into low-yielding non-core venture assets.
