Section 1: The Premise of the Company

New Oriental Education & Technology Group Inc. (NYSE: EDU / 9901.SEHK) represents China’s premier private educational and learning services enterprise. Founded in 1993 by Executive Chairman Michael Minhong Yu, the business has successfully completed one of the most substantial corporate transformations in modern consumer history. Following the Chinese government’s 2021 "Double Reduction" regulatory directive—which eliminated mandatory K-9 academic curriculum tutoring—New Oriental completely restructured its operating model, pivoting from traditional compulsory academic tutoring toward non-academic educational initiatives, intelligent hardware systems, overseas test preparation, high school academic tutoring, cultural tourism, and e-commerce livestreaming via its 57%-owned listed subsidiary, East Buy Holding Limited.

Core Business Model & Unit Monetization Engine

New Oriental generates cash flows across four interconnected operational pillars, operating through an extensive nationwide footprint of learning centers, self-operated digital platforms, and livestreaming channels:

  • New Educational Initiatives & Core Tutoring: Encompasses non-academic tutoring (artistic, scientific, sports, and coding programs aimed at well-rounded student development), high school academic tutoring, and intelligent learning systems/hardware. Monetization occurs via prepaid tuition fees collected prior to course commencement. This generates a massive working capital buffer reflected in $2,242.9 million of deferred revenue as of May 31, 2026 (+14.8% YoY). Unit economics are anchored to learning center capacity utilization, student retention per cohort, and price realization per course hour.
  • Overseas Test Preparation & Study Abroad Consulting: Provides test preparation for international standardized exams (IELTS, TOEFL, SAT, GRE) alongside end-to-end university application consulting. Customers pay upfront consulting retainers and course packages. Volume drivers depend on international student mobility, foreign study visas, and household discretionary spend among middle-and-upper-class families.
  • Private Label Products & Livestreaming E-Commerce (East Buy): Operates live-streaming social commerce across Douyin, WeChat, and proprietary mobile applications. Monetization stems from direct merchandise sales of private-label agricultural goods, food, daily necessities, and third-party commission fees. East Buy leverages New Oriental’s brand trust and teacher-anchored livestreaming personalities to minimize customer acquisition costs (CAC).
  • Educational Materials, Distribution & Cultural Tourism: Synthesizes book publishing, digital learning software distribution, and customized cultural travel experiences for students, family units, and elderly demographics. Revenue is realized via book sales, software licensing, and per-head tour package pricing.

Sector-Specific Maintenance CapEx & Owner Earnings Derivation (Buffett 1986 Standard)

Under the Buffett 1986 Owner Earnings standard, capital expenditure must be rigorously bifurcated into maintenance CapEx and growth CapEx. For an enterprise operating a vast physical network of learning centers (over 800 locations) alongside technology infrastructure, maintenance CapEx represents the recurring cost required to maintain physical classroom standards, refresh leasehold improvements (5-year depreciation cycle), update IT hardware, and replace baseline server capacity. In accordance with empirical standards for retail learning networks, true statutory Maintenance CapEx is anchored to statutory Depreciation & Amortization (~60% of total CapEx, or ~$148.14 million), while the remaining 40% ($98.76 million) represents discretionary expansion CapEx spent on launching new learning centers.

Crucially, Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) is treated as a 100% real cash charge, as it causes permanent equity dilution.

Governance, Executive Control & C-Suite Audit

  • Executive Control & Key Personnel: Founder and Executive Chairman Michael Minhong Yu retains ultimate strategic authority. Yu successfully navigated the 2021 restructuring and personally spearheaded the incubation of East Buy. CEO Chenggang Zhou and CFO Stephen Zhihui Yang have served in executive leadership for over a decade, providing exceptional executive stability and operational continuity.
  • Dual-Class & Listing Structure: Ordinary shares are traded on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (9901.HK) and listed as ADSs on the NYSE (EDU), where 1 ADS represents 10 ordinary shares. Voting control remains concentrated with founder Michael Yu and aligned long-term management.
  • Activist Campaigns & Ownership Stability: SEC 13D/13G filings confirm zero hostile activist campaigns (Elliott, Starboard, or Third Point). The institutional shareholder base is anchored by long-term fundamental asset managers including Invesco, Orbis Allan Gray, First Beijing Investment, and UBS Asset Management.
  • Capital Return Policy: Management operates under a formal board policy allocating no less than 50% of annual GAAP net income to shareholder distributions via cash dividends and share repurchases. In FY2026, EDU executed $274 million in share buybacks and declared $300 million in cumulative dividends, reducing diluted ADS count by ~2.2% YoY.

Economic Moat Classification: Narrow Moat

Under strict Graham/Buffett/Munger valuation parameters, New Oriental is classified as a Narrow Moat enterprise:

  • Brand Equity & Trust: Operating in China for over 30 years, "New Oriental" is the single most recognized consumer brand in Chinese private education. This trusted reputation drives organic student recruitment and provides pricing power, keeping customer acquisition costs low compared to digital-only peers.
  • Scale Density & Dual-Engine Infrastructure: Possesses an institutional footprint of over 800 physical learning centers across 100+ cities integrated with proprietary AI-driven learning systems and East Buy’s social commerce ecosystem.
  • Regulatory Bounding Constraint: Bounded at Narrow Moat rather than Wide Moat due to low contract switching costs for individual students and structural exposure to Chinese regulatory policy shifts.

Section 2: The Probable Future Paths

Based on the company's audited statutory filings, segment dynamics, 4-quarter earnings call commentary, and institutional counter-theses, here are the distinct, realistic operational paths covering 90%–95% of the fundamental probability space over the next 3–5 years:

Path 1: Steady Post-Restructuring Expansion & Core Learning Platform Monetization

Archetype: Central Baseline Trend & Operational Continuation

New Oriental leverages its non-academic tutoring (literary, artistic, and scientific enrichment), high school academic coaching, study abroad consulting, and East Buy e-commerce ecosystem to drive a steady 11.3% revenue CAGR. Capacity expansion in learning centers is scaled prudently at 10%–15% annually to prevent idle lease drag. Fixed cost leverage across newly established learning spaces unlocks operating margin expansion to 13.5%. Supported by consistent share repurchases and balance sheet interest income, Owner Earnings per share grow at a +16.4% CAGR from $4.89 to $10.44 by Year 5.

Path 2: E-Commerce Compression, Learning Center Saturation & Margin Drag

Archetype: Downside Friction, Competitive Headwinds & Margin Drag

Deceleration in East Buy livestreaming sales—driven by host turnover, platform algorithm shifts, and intense price competition across douyin/e-commerce channels—combines with capacity saturation across physical non-academic learning centers. Rapid network expansion leads to local cannibalization and lower utilization rates, squeezing operating margins from 11.4% down to 8.0%. Top-line revenue growth slows to a 5.2% CAGR, while higher maintenance CapEx and capitalized software costs compress Year-5 Owner Earnings to $4.44 per share (-1.9% per-share OE CAGR).

Path 3: Enterprise Platform Acceleration & Digital-Physical Operating Leverage Expansion

Archetype: Upside Acceleration, Operating Leverage & TAM Expansion

New Oriental accelerates revenue growth to a peak of 21.8% CAGR via surging demand for smart learning devices (AI learning tablets), rapid penetration of non-academic enrichment centers into Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, and monetization of East Buy's private-label product lines. Fixed learning center lease dilution and high-margin digital hardware scaling unlock 610 bps of operating margin expansion to 17.5%. ROIC expands from 13.4% to 22.8%, driving Year-5 Owner Earnings per share to $21.70 (+34.7% per-share OE CAGR).
