01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market has aggressively re-rated Cummins to a premium multiple of 25.6x Owner Earnings, treating it as a high-growth AI and hyperscale data center infrastructure play. This valuation is driven by surging demand for backup power generators in the Power Systems segment. However, this optimistic narrative overlooks a stark operational disconnect: approximately 75% of Cummins' revenue base remains tied to highly cyclical, low-margin industrial and automotive segments. These core segments are currently experiencing structural margin erosion, rising cost headwinds, and a failure of operating leverage, as evidenced by the recent 90 basis point compression in consolidated EBITDA margins despite record top-line revenues.

The market is pricing in unsustainable peak earnings for the data center business while ignoring the cyclical vulnerabilities of the broader enterprise. Skeptics point to a negative mix shift in the Distribution segment toward lower-margin equipment, volume downgrades in mining engines due to elevated customer inventories, and a heavy reliance on lower-margin international sales to prop up engine volumes. Furthermore, the indefinite pause of the Amplify battery joint venture and ongoing cash burn at Accelera highlight that Cummins' transition to zero-emission technologies is proving to be a capital-intensive drag rather than a near-term growth driver.

How Things Are Going Now

Operationally, Cummins is delivering record-level top-line performance, driven by robust demand in its Power Systems and Distribution segments. In the latest quarter, the company achieved an annualized revenue run-rate of $37,828.0M, supported by 161,200 engine shipments, representing a 9.7% year-over-year increase. This volume growth was led by a 17.3% surge in medium-duty engines to 86,100 units, while heavy-duty engines grew a modest 1.7% to 30,100 units. The Engine segment maintained solid unit monetization, averaging $19,132 in revenue per engine shipped.

Despite this volume momentum, profitability is facing near-term friction. The latest quarter's gross margin stood at 26.1%, and consolidated EBITDA margins compressed to 17.5% due to higher variable incentive compensation accruals and elevated SG&A expenses, which grew 15% YoY to $893M. However, cash generation remains exceptionally strong, with operating cash flow reaching $1,808M in the first half of 2026. This cash flow supported $438M in capital expenditures, leaving substantial free cash flow to fund a 10% dividend increase to $2.20 per share and $468M in share repurchases.

To optimize its portfolio, management has actively pruned underperforming assets, taking a $199M charge to divest its low-pressure fuel cell business and restructuring its Accelera zero-emissions segment. While the Accelera segment continues to operate at a loss ($69M EBITDA loss in the latest quarter), the core Power Systems segment is thriving, with EBITDA margins expanding to 24.5% on the back of insatiable demand for standby power generators from hyperscale AI data centers.
