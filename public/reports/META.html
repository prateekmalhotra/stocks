Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Meta Platforms, Inc. (NASDAQ: META), under the active leadership of Founder & Chief Executive Officer Mark Zuckerberg and Chief Financial Officer Susan Li, entered the second half of 2026 navigating two distinct financial realities. On the commercial front, Meta's core advertising engine is executing with unprecedented efficiency, propelled by the deep integration of generative AI ranking, recommendation, and targeted delivery architectures across its Family of Apps (FoA). In Q2 2026 (ended June 30, 2026), consolidated net revenue expanded 28.0% year-over-year (+27% constant currency) to $60.80 billion. Top-line acceleration was driven by a 14% YoY increase in ad impressions served across Facebook and Instagram—where Instagram reached 2.0 billion daily active users—and a 12% increase in average price per ad. Consequently, GAAP operating income fell 8.2% YoY to $18.78 billion, with GAAP operating margin contracting by ~1,200 basis points to 30.9%. Excluding legal and severance charges, operating income would have increased 9% YoY.

Management remains unyielding in its commitment to build out proprietary compute capacity ahead of demand through Meta Superintelligence Labs. CFO Susan Li narrowed full-year 2026 CapEx guidance to $130.0–$145.0 billion (up from $125.0–$145.0 billion) and raised full-year expense guidance to $165.0–$169.0 billion. While near-term cash conversion is severely suppressed by this CapEx wave, monetization vectors beyond display advertising are beginning to scale: Advantage+ AI ad tools crossed a $75 billion annual revenue run rate, Family of Apps "Other" revenue crossed $1.0 billion (+73% YoY) driven by WhatsApp paid messaging, and over 1 million businesses actively utilize Meta Business Agents weekly. Supported by a balance sheet fortress containing $90.30 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities against $83.70 billion in total debt, Meta possesses the balance-sheet liquidity required to absorb near-term cash-flow drag while locking in structural, physical, and algorithmic competitive moats.

How Meta Makes Money: Business Mechanics, Switching Costs & Pricing Power

1. Non-GAAP Add-Back Audit & 100% SBC Cash Expense Treatment

Standard Wall Street research routinely adds back Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) to cash flows under the premise that equity grants are non-cash items. Under the 7 First Principles of Business Valuation, this practice is rejected. SBC represents real economic dilution that reduces the owner's fractional share of the enterprise. If key engineers, research scientists, and executives were not compensated in restricted stock units (RSUs), Meta would be forced to pay equivalent compensation in cash.

In Q2 2026, Meta's Stock-Based Compensation reached $7.66 billion (up from $4.83 billion in Q2 2025), representing 12.6% of quarterly revenue. On a TTM basis ending June 30, 2026, total SBC expense swelled to $25.14 billion. To maintain denominator integrity and protect existing shareholders from equity dilution friction, 100% of SBC is deducted directly from GAAP Operating Cash Flow when deriving true Buffett Owner Earnings. Furthermore, because share repurchases have been significantly curtailed to fund the AI CapEx ramp ($0 common stock buybacks in recent quarters vs. $26.26 billion repurchased in FY 2025), SBC is no longer being neutralized by opportunistic share cancellations, resulting in net per-share dilution.
