Section 1: Company Overview & Audited Financial Baseline

Investment Recommendation Headline: AVOID / TRIM

Celsius Holdings, Inc. (CELH) operates as a rapidly scaled functional beverage platform that develops, processes, markets, and distributes calorie-burning, performance-focused energy drinks, liquid hydration packets, and functional powders. Under the executive leadership of Chief Executive Officer John Fieldly and newly appointed Chief Commercial Officer Tyler Bohannon, Celsius has transitioned from an emergent single-brand disruptor into a multi-brand beverage platform encompassing the flagship CELSIUS® brand, the female-focused lifestyle energy brand Alani Nu® (acquired in April 2025), and Rockstar Energy® (integrated via a finished-goods commercial model in mid-2025). The core operational engine centers on an asset-light co-packing model coupled with a national Direct-to-Store Delivery (DSD) distribution agreement with PepsiCo, Inc., which serves as the primary distribution partner and holds a strategic equity stake in the enterprise. Operating fundamentals are underwritten as of the latest statutory reporting period covering LTM Q2 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) and full fiscal year FY 2025.

1. The Core Business Machine, Moat & Unit Economics

Primary Economic Moat Rating: Narrow Moat

The economic moat of Celsius Holdings is classified as Narrow Moat, anchored by brand equity in functional health/fitness energy and tollbridge distribution scale through PepsiCo's DSD network. Unlike legacy energy drinks that relied on high-sugar or taurine profiles, Celsius carved out a functional market segment by positioning its Proprietary MetaPlus® blend as a thermogenic metabolic booster. This positioning created high consumer brand affinity within gym, retail, and club channels. The acquisition of Alani Nu expanded this brand power into high-margin female lifestyle demographics, building a combined U.S. RTD energy category market share of approximately 20.0% by early 2026.

However, the moat is constrained by zero customer switching costs at the retail point-of-sale, intense shelf-space competition from category titans (Monster Beverage, Red Bull) and emerging challenger brands (Ghost, C4, Keurig Dr Pepper), and structural dependence on PepsiCo for route-to-market execution. Furthermore, packaging concentration risk is acute: greater than 90% of total product volume is packaged in standard aluminum cans, exposing the supply chain and gross margins to aluminum tariff fluctuations, smelting cost surges, and co-packer conversion fee spikes. Unit economics reflect an asset-light framework: Celsius does not own capital-intensive canning facilities, relying instead on third-party co-packers to fill cans based on rolling purchase commitments. This structure keeps capital expenditures disciplined—locking in baseline Maintenance CapEx at exactly $29.6M (1.2% of net revenue)—while allowing gross margins to fluctuate near 48.0%–50.0%.

Buffett & Munger Pricing Power & Inflation Resistance Audit

Pricing Power Classification: Inflation Pass-Through

Celsius exhibits Inflation Pass-Through capacity, constrained by retail trade promotions and distributor allowance dynamics. Under the "Prayer Session" test, management cannot set wholesale price increases unilaterally without risk of shelf-space loss or volume destruction. When raw material costs (aluminum cans, sucralose, citric acid, freight) spike, Celsius can pass through cost increases over a 6-to-12-month lag via list price adjustments, but must cushion retail velocity through gross-to-net promotional allowances. In Q2 2026, gross margins compressed from 51.5% to 48.1% year-over-year, driven by elevated trade promotional spend required to clear inventory during retail space optimizations and rising co-packing commodity charges.

Additional dollars generated from price adjustments drop straight through to cash flow without being absorbed by maintenance CapEx capital traps.

Buffett & Munger Cash Flow Predictability & "Too Hard" Pile Audit

Cash Flow Predictability Classification: Moderate Predictability

Celsius is categorized as Moderate Predictability due to inventory adjustments within the PepsiCo distribution network, integration steps following multi-brand acquisitions, and shifting shelf-space reset timing among mass merchants. In the 10-Year Visibility Test, while functional energy drinks demonstrate strong habitual repeat consumer purchase behavior, channel inventory swings by DSD partners can create multi-quarter operational misalignments between consumer sell-through (scan data) and reported factory sell-in revenue.

The enterprise avoids the "Red Queen" technology trap of hardware obsolescence. Reinvestment friction is concentrated in SG&A brand-building and trade marketing rather than physical asset replacement. Approximately 85% of revenue represents repeat, impulse-driven consumer transactions across grocery, convenience, mass, and e-commerce channels.

Executive turnover added leadership friction following C-suite changes, including the transition out of COO Eric Hanson and the appointment of Tyler Bohannon as Chief Commercial Officer.

Section 2: The Three Forward-Looking Operating Stories

To establish a rigorous equity valuation framework for Celsius Holdings, Inc. (CELH), we model three distinct forward-looking operational trajectories over a 5-year forecast horizon. All three scenarios are strictly anchored to verified baseline fundamentals: Starting Normalized Owner Earnings (OE₀) of $309.4M ($1.21 per share) across 255.6 million diluted shares, baseline Maintenance CapEx of exactly $29.6M (1.2% of net revenue), and statutory Net Debt of -$36.7M (-$0.14 per share) reflecting $631.2M in cash and cash equivalents offset by $667.9M in long-term debt and term liabilities. The operational baseline incorporates recent headwinds including a -11.7% YoY organic contraction in the flagship CELSIUS® brand during Q2 2026, gross margin compression to 48.1%, C-suite instability marked by the departure of COO Eric Hanson, and critical packaging risk with >90% of total product volume concentrated in aluminum cans.

Top-line visibility remains obscured by inorganic M&A additions—with Alani Nu® contributing $364.4M and Rockstar Energy® contributing $66.5M in baseline revenue—as well as acute structural drag from PepsiCo DSD destocking friction, self-inflicted SKU over-cutting errors acknowledged by management, expanding trade promotional billbacks, and exposure to aluminum tariff volatility. Based on these compounding operational vulnerabilities, our overall investment stance is set strictly to AVOID / TRIM.

📖 Story 1: Operational Rebalancing, DSD Friction & Margin Compression (Base Case — 50% Probability)

In this primary baseline scenario, Celsius Holdings faces a protracted operational rebalancing period rather than a swift V-shaped recovery. The core flagship CELSIUS brand slowly rebounds from its Q2 2026 organic contraction (-11.7% YoY) to modest low-single-digit growth as retail inventory across PepsiCo's Direct-to-Store Delivery (DSD) network normalizes. However, lingering DSD destocking friction, retailer pushback on trade promotional billback schedules, and self-inflicted SKU over-cutting errors—acknowledged by executive management following C-suite reshuffling and the departure of COO Eric Hanson—delay velocity recovery across core retail channels.

Gross margins settle near 48.1% to 48.5% as trade promotional allowances remain elevated to defend shelf space against category incumbents, while unhedged aluminum tariff risks weigh on production costs given the company's >90% aluminum packaging concentration. Although inorganic contributions from Alani Nu ($364.4M baseline) and Rockstar ($66.5M baseline) stabilize overall top-line volume, consolidated revenue growth decelerates to a 5-year CAGR of +6.5%.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Consolidated net revenue grows from $3,047.2M to $4,175.2M by Year 5 (+6.5% CAGR). Flagship CELSIUS organic growth stabilizes at +2.5% annually, while Alani Nu expands at +6.0% annually. U.S. RTD energy market share plateaus at ~18.5%.

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Gross margins average 48.2% under persistent promotional billback friction and aluminum input cost inflation. Operating margins level off at 13.5% as SG&A deleveraging offsets overhead cuts. Maintenance CapEx remains tied to baseline trends at 1.2% of net revenue ($29.6M baseline scaling to $50.1M in Year 5). Starting from OE₀ of $309.4M ($1.21/share), Normalized Owner Earnings reach $476.1M ($1.86 per share) by Year 5.

DCF Intrinsic Value Output: Discounted at a 9.5% WACC with a 2.5% terminal growth rate, Story 1 yields an intrinsic fair value of $21.80 per share (incorporating the -$0.14/share Net Debt deduction based on -$36.7M Net Debt across 255.6M shares).

📖 Story 2: Multi-Brand DSD Synergy Acceleration & Global Scalability (Bull Case — 15% Probability)

This upside trajectory models a rapid resolution of PepsiCo DSD alignment friction, allowing Celsius to fully rectify past SKU over-cutting errors and scale its combined brand portfolio across non-tracked food service, hospitality, and international channels. The flagship CELSIUS brand rebounds aggressively from its Q2 2026 contraction (-11.7% YoY) back to double-digit organic growth (+11.5% CAGR), while leadership transition following COO Eric Hanson's exit yields operational improvements in supply chain efficiency.

Scale efficiencies provide purchasing power over aluminum suppliers, mitigating aluminum tariff headwinds despite the >90% packaging concentration. Trade promotional billbacks normalize as organic consumer pull accelerates, driving gross margins up toward 51.5%. Fixed-cost leverage across PepsiCo's logistics network significantly reduces freight expenses, driving operating margins toward 18.5%.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Consolidated net revenue expands at a 5-year CAGR of +13.5%, reaching $5,677.2M by Year 5. Flagship CELSIUS organic growth rebounds to +11.5%, while Alani Nu surges at a +15.0% CAGR. U.S. RTD energy market share reaches 23.5%, and international revenue expands to 8.5% of sales mix.

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Gross margins expand to 51.5%. Operating margins reach 18.5% due to SG&A absorption. Maintenance CapEx scales with top-line growth at 1.2% of revenue ($68.1M in Year 5). Starting from OE₀ of $309.4M ($1.21/share), Normalized Owner Earnings scale to $786.1M ($3.08 per share) by Year 5.

DCF Intrinsic Value Output: Discounted at a 9.0% WACC with a 3.0% terminal growth rate, Story 2 yields an intrinsic fair value of $43.11 per share (incorporating the -$0.14/share Net Debt deduction).

📖 Story 3: Category Saturation, Tariff Pressure & Promotional Erosion (Bear Case — 35% Probability)

This downside stress scenario models severe structural saturation in functional energy beverages, compounded by worsening PepsiCo DSD destocking friction and irreversible market share loss resulting from SKU over-cutting errors. The flagship CELSIUS brand's organic decline deepens past Q2 2026 levels (-11.7% YoY) into sustained structural contraction (-5.0% annually) as key retail distributors reallocate shelf space to challenger brands. C-suite turnover further impairs operational execution.

To defend distribution points and avoid store de-listings, Celsius escalates trade promotional billbacks and slotting allowances. Unhedged aluminum tariffs penalize cost of goods sold due to the company's extreme >90% aluminum can concentration. Gross margins collapse to 42.5%, while fixed overhead deleveraging pushes operating margins down to 7.0%.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Consolidated net revenue contracts at a 5-year CAGR of -3.5%, dropping to $2,548.8M by Year 5. Flagship CELSIUS revenue contracts -5.0% annually, while acquired brands Alani Nu ($364.4M baseline) and Rockstar ($66.5M baseline) stagnate. U.S. RTD energy market share drops to 12.5%.

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Gross margins fall to 42.5% under billback escalation and aluminum tariff shocks. Operating margins compress to 7.0%. DCF Intrinsic Value Output: Discounted at a 10.5% WACC with a 1.0% terminal growth rate, Story 3 yields an intrinsic fair value of $5.54 per share (incorporating the -$0.14/share Net Debt deduction).
