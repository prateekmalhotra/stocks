Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

1. Business Model Reality & Core Moat Analysis

StoneCo Ltd. (NASDAQ: STNE) has completed a structural strategic transformation, divesting non-core software assets to refine its operational focus strictly on its high-return financial technology flywheel. The company operates a fully integrated ecosystem designed specifically for Brazilian Micro, Small, and Medium Businesses (MSMBs), generating recurring cash flows across three interconnected pillars: Merchant Acquiring & Payments, Digital Banking, and MSMB Credit Solutions.

Strategic Refocusing & Capital Allocation Event: On February 20, 2026, StoneCo formally closed the sale of Linx to TOTVS for an enterprise value of R$3.05 billion. Retaining roughly R$3.8 billion in amortizable fiscal goodwill, management promptly returned the net proceeds via a landmark extraordinary dividend of $2.53 per share (~R$3.08 billion total) on May 4, 2026. Former CEO and Board Chairman Pedro Zinner summarized the rationale: "Our competitive advantages live at a very specific intersection: payments, banking, and credit... Linx was a good business sitting outside that intersection."

Revenue Generation & Ecosystem Economics

StoneCo’s fundamental thesis relies on deep monetization of merchant cash flows through proprietary distribution channels (Hubs) and end-to-end technology:

  • Merchant Acquiring (Payments): Stone earns transaction processing fees (take rate) on Total Payment Volume (TPV), augmented by pre-payment fees (receivables discounting/antecipação). While card TPV expansion has moderated to single digits due to macroeconomic tightness, PIX QR code payment volumes have surged, offering lower headline take rates but significantly higher customer engagement and lower funding costs.
  • Digital Banking Services: StoneCo functions as the primary operational bank for over 2 million merchants. Revenue is generated via interest income on retail deposits (R$10.1 billion balance), account maintenance, wire fees, and interchange on business debit/credit cards. Banking deposits serve as a low-cost internal funding base for credit operations.
  • MSMB Credit Solutions: Leveraging real-time POS transaction data, Stone underwrites working capital loans and credit cards. Credit generates elevated gross yields (credit revenue reached R$297 million in Q1 2026), with repayments automatically deducted directly from daily merchant acquiring settlements.

Pricing Power, Take-Rate Trends & Customer Retention

StoneCo's structural competitive moat—historically anchored by its hyper-local sales Hubs and superior customer service—faced friction following aggressive pricing adjustments implemented during 2024–2025 to offset elevated domestic interest rates (Selic). While these repricing initiatives initially protected net interest margins, they triggered client churn among fee-sensitive 2025 merchant cohorts and caused a temporary contraction in active clients. Management is currently shifting focus from pure client volume to maximizing Average Revenue Per Active Client (ARPAC) through bundled payments, banking, and credit integration.

Credit Deterioration & Operational Execution Stress

The primary earnings drag in Q1 2026 stemmed from accelerating credit losses within micro and small business portfolios, driven by persistent high Selic interest rates (~10.5%–12.0%) in Brazil:

  • Surging Provision Costs: Adjusted gross profit remained flat at R$1.50 billion as top-line gains were fully offset by a >50% QoQ surge in expected credit loss provisions. Cost of Risk escalated sharply to 21.9%.

Management Strategy to Remediate Churn & Credit Stress:

Heading into the Q2 2026 reporting period (scheduled for August 13, 2026), executive management has reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance, projecting gross profit and net income acceleration in 2H 2026. To mitigate churn and control credit risk, StoneCo is executing a three-part turnaround strategy:

  1. Pricing Simplification & Transparent Bundling: Unifying payments and banking fees into transparent, fixed-tier packages to reduce merchant billing friction.
  2. Dedicated Account Management Desks: Re-engaging high-ARPAC merchants via localized Hub incentives and specialized retention desks.
  3. Pivot to Secured Credit Underwriting: Pivoting new credit origination away from uncollateralized working capital loans toward secured, collateralized lending products and credit card solutions to structurally curb NPL formation.

Warren Buffett Owner Earnings & SBC Adjustments

In accordance with fundamental owner-earnings principles, Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) is treated as a 100% real economic expense and a direct cause of shareholder dilution. While accounting standards add back SBC to operating cash flows as a non-cash item, issuing shares to executives dilutes existing owners' equity claims just as real cash wages do. Additionally, total capital expenditures are separated into Maintenance CapEx (the capital needed to replace aging POS terminals and preserve software infrastructure) and Growth CapEx (investments to expand into credit and banking solutions).

Section 6: Thesis Confidence, Execution Risk & Pre-Mortem Falsification

Primary Thesis Conviction: Moderate Conviction
While StoneCo trades at a significant discount to its Base Case intrinsic value ($16.85) and provides a 23.0% starting Owner Earnings yield, overall conviction is capped at 'Moderate' due to macroeconomic sensitivity in Brazil, elevated credit loss provisions in the micro-merchant portfolio, and persistent double-digit sovereign funding costs.

Key Execution Risks & Vulnerabilities

The path to realizing intrinsic fair value faces three structural execution risks that require ongoing monitoring:

  • 1. Micro-Merchant Credit Defaults & Provision Escalation: As StoneCo expands its working capital loan book to MSMEs, credit default rates remain the primary tail risk. In Q1 2026, increased credit loss provisions compressed gross profit margins from 44.4% to 41.6%. Higher provisions could offset payment processing gains if underwriting models miscalculate default risk during economic slowdowns.
  • 2. High SELIC Persistence & Funding Cost Compression: With the Central Bank of Brazil (Copom) maintaining the benchmark SELIC rate at 14.0%–14.25%, high interest rates increase funding costs on liquidity lines used to finance prepayment transactions (FIDC). Prolonged double-digit interest rates limit net interest margin (NIM) expansion on merchant banking deposits.
  • 3. Price Competition & Take-Rate Compression: Aggressive pricing from incumbent acquiring networks (Itaú/Rede, Bradesco/Cielo) and fintech competitors (PagSeguro, Mercado Pago) poses an ongoing risk to merchant take-rates. Furthermore, the rapid adoption of Brazil's central bank instant payment system (Pix) reduces reliance on traditional debit cards.

Pre-Mortem Falsification Triggers

The following pre-defined quantitative and qualitative triggers would invalidate the investment thesis, requiring an immediate thesis downgrade or position exit:

Trigger 1: Non-Performing Loans (NPL 90+) Exceeding 6.0% of Credit Portfolio
If 90+ day non-performing loan ratios in the credit portfolio climb above 6.0% for two consecutive quarters, indicating systemic underwriting failures similar to the 2021 credit pause, the investment thesis is falsified.
Trigger 2: Normalized Gross Profit Margin Compression Below 38.0%
If gross profit margins drop below 38.0% (down from 41.6% in Q1 2026) due to take-rate erosion or elevated funding costs, it would signal structural margin degradation and invalidation of the capital-light moat hypothesis.
Trigger 3: Abandonment or Dilution of Share Buyback Execution
If management halts share repurchases despite the stock trading below book value ($9.65/share) or issues accretive stock-based compensation exceeding 3.5% of total share count annually, the cannibal compounding thesis is invalidated.
