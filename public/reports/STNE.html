01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market is pricing StoneCo (STNE) at $9.36 per share—a distressed 2.7x P/OE multiple and EV/OE of 4.6x—reflecting deep institutional skepticism and widespread sell-side downgrades. Equity markets, alongside analysts from Morgan Stanley (Underweight) and Goldman Sachs (Neutral), are pricing in a structural margin decay driven by two relentless forces: the rapid disintermediation of debit card processing by Brazil's Central Bank Pix payment rails, and market share losses to peers like PagSeguro (PAGS). With net balance sheet debt sitting at $-6.52 per share ($-1,586M total net debt), investors treat StoneCo as a spread business caught in a higher-for-longer Selic interest rate environment where wholesale funding costs remaining at 85%–100% of CDI constantly squeeze prepayment yields.

Simultaneously, the market fears that management's rapid expansion of its working capital credit book (reaching R$3,752.0 million) represents a dangerous re-run of its failed 2021 underwriting playbook. With 90+ day non-performing loans (NPLs) elevated at 8.60% and loan loss coverage dropping to 203.6%, sell-side commentary highlights that management is steering forward guidance toward the bottom of gross profit targets. Investors are discounting current normalized owner cash flow ($3.42/share, or $832.1M) as peak, non-repeatable earnings that will inevitably be eroded by credit default write-downs, elevated cost of risk, and non-recurring allowances.

How Things Are Going Now

Despite intense equity market pessimism, StoneCo's underlying operating engine continues to process massive payment volumes while generating substantial cash flow. Total Payment Volume (TPV) grew +4.3% YoY to R$142.2 billion in the latest quarter, while the active client base expanded +6.4% YoY to reach 4.8 million merchants. The real-time annualized revenue run-rate stands at $2,562.4M, supported by a 43.6% gross margin. While monthly ARPAC contracted -6.6% YoY to R$251.1 due to Pix mix shift, StoneCo partially offset transaction fee compression by expanding its credit portfolio +107.5% YoY to R$3,752.0M, capturing higher net interest income from MSMB working capital loans.

Operational cash generation remains solid, with audited Owner ROIC at 26.0% and normalized annual Owner Earnings running at $832.1M ($3.42 per share) after treating $26.8M in stock-based compensation as real cash dilution and deducting $178.6M in maintenance POS CapEx. However, our Moat Audit confirms a Weak Moat: StoneCo possesses zero pricing power over Pix payment rails, faces relentless price wars from incumbent bank acquirers, and must continually spend CapEx on POS hardware to retain merchant relationships.
