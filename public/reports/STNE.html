StoneCo Ltd. (NASDAQ: STNE) operates an integrated financial technology and software platform targeting Micro, Small, and Medium-Sized Businesses (MSMEs) across Brazil. Historically recognized primarily as an aggressive card acquirer, StoneCo has engineered a multi-pronged monetization architecture that combines transaction acquiring, digital banking, working capital lending, and integrated software.

  • Total Payment Volume (TPV) & Merchant Acquiring: Payments remain the primary customer acquisition funnel. Revenue is derived from Merchant Discount Rates (MDR) charged per transaction, POS terminal rentals, and financial volume processing (including PIX QR code integration). While top-line payment growth faced macroeconomic deceleration in early 2026—with MSMB TPV reaching R$ 137.0 billion (+3.0% YoY)—acquiring yields remain stable as StoneCo balances price adjustments against competitive pressures.
  • Software Strategy & Linx Realignment: StoneCo executed a definitive strategic pivot away from non-core enterprise software. Capital proceeds from these divestments were returned directly to equity holders, refocusing management bandwidth on high-return financial verticals.
  • Digital Banking Franchise: StoneCo’s banking ecosystem serves as an operational hub and critical low-cost funding mechanism. Retail deposits stood at R$ 10.1 billion (+22% YoY, average daily deposits +26% YoY). By capturing merchant settlement flows directly within Stone accounts, StoneCo systematically reduced its blended cost of funding from 100% of CDI in early 2025 to approximately 87% of CDI. In Brazil’s high Selic rate climate (~14.5%), this structural funding edge expands net interest margins across both transaction prepayment and credit lending.
  • Working Capital Credit Loans: Credit represents StoneCo’s highest-margin, highest-volatility growth vector. The total loan portfolio expanded 14% sequentially to R$ 3.22 billion (comprising R$ 2.9 billion in working capital merchant solutions and R$ 0.4 billion in credit cards). Portfolio yield accreted to 3.3% per month (up from 3.1% in Q4 2025), driving credit segment revenues to R$ 297 million (+25% QoQ).
Strategic Realignment: The monetization flywheel has shifted from pure TPV land-grab to deep wallet penetration. StoneCo captures payment float, monetizes merchant balances through digital banking services, and utilizes transactional visibility to underwrite high-yield working capital loans.

2. Merchant Retention, Churn Dynamics & MSME Execution

Sustaining merchant retention while expanding wallet monetization has proven to be an operational double-edged sword. During 2025 and early 2026, StoneCo experienced elevated churn within micro-merchant cohorts (primarily onboarded via the Ton self-serve channel) and specific 2025 SMB cohorts.

  • MSME Cohort Friction: High prevailing interest rates and inflationary pressures squeezed micro-merchant operating cash flows, leading to business closures and increased price sensitivity. Aggressive pricing updates on inactive accounts and automated fee structures triggered elevated client attrition.
  • Corrective Operational Initiatives: Management responded by simplifying product tiers, refining credit-scoring algorithms to prevent over-leverage, and re-engaging localized sales "Hubs" to manage relationship-driven SMB accounts. Additionally, StoneCo introduced the "RPAC" (Revenue Per Active Client) operating metric to track monetization efficiency across retained core merchants, shifting compensation incentives from raw account additions to high-retention, multi-product client relationships.

3. Pricing Power & Unit Economics

In standalone card acquiring, pricing power is constrained by intense industry rivalry. Pure-play MDR rates face ongoing pressure from bank-backed acquirers and instant digital payment alternatives like PIX. However, StoneCo’s bundled delivery—combining transaction settlement, working capital credit, and zero-fee banking—allows the firm to sustain premium effective take rates.

By expanding its credit portfolio yield to 3.3% per month and maintaining strict funding costs through its R$ 10.1 billion deposit base, StoneCo protects overall unit economics even when headline card processing fees adjust downward.
