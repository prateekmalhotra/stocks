Section 1: Business Model, Hub Strategy, Customer Retention & Competitive Dynamics

1. Core Architecture: The "Stone Hub" Hyper-Local Operating Model

StoneCo Ltd. (NASDAQ: STNE) operates a differentiated distribution and service model in the Brazilian small-and-medium business (SMB) merchant acquiring market. While legacy bank acquirers (Rede/Itaú, Cielo/Bradesco) rely on traditional bank branches and call centers, and pure-play fintechs (PagSeguro/PagBank) rely heavily on digital self-serve onboarding, Stone’s growth core is its proprietary "Hub Strategy."

A Stone Hub is an autonomous, localized unit comprising dedicated direct sales personnel, operational logistics agents, and customer service teams ("Green Angels") operating within a specific zip code or city footprint. This decentralized structure delivers two critical competitive advantages:

  • SLA Superiority: Field agents provide fast on-site terminal setup, hardware replacement, and technical troubleshooting within hours, alongside immediate phone responses.
  • Commercial Proximity: Local sales representatives build direct relationships with local business owners, navigating local economic nuances and cross-selling financial services far more effectively than remote tele-sales reps or generic digital ads.

2. Customer Retention & Switching Costs

Payment processing in Brazil is fundamentally commoditized on price alone. However, Stone mitigates churn and creates stickiness by transitioning from a single-product acquirer into a deeply embedded financial operating platform:

  • Triple-Play Integration (Payments + Banking + Credit): Stone embeds transaction clearing, business digital accounts (PIX, bill payments, wire transfers), and working capital loans into a single interface. Once an SMB migrates its primary cash flow management and payroll into Stone’s banking ecosystem (which exceeded R$10 billion in client deposits), switching costs increase significantly.
  • Automatic Receivables Prepayment (ARV): In Brazil, credit card sales natively settle in 30 days. Stone’s automated prepayment features grant merchants instant liquidity, turning processing into a daily treasury tool.
  • Net Promoter Score (NPS) Advantage: By maintaining an industry-leading NPS relative to legacy acquirers, Stone reduces structural merchant churn—allowing it to preserve pricing power even during aggressive price wars.

Section 2: Linx Divestment & Software Strategy, ARPAC Trends, & Macro Resilience

1. The Strategic Pivot: Linx Divestment & Embedded Partnerships

In 2021, Stone acquired Linx for ~R$6.7 billion to pair enterprise enterprise resource planning (ERP) / retail software directly with payment processing. However, managing complex enterprise retail software proved capital-intensive and culturally divergent from Stone’s lean SMB financial platform.

Following a thorough strategic review, Stone executed a major structural pivot:

  • Software Business Divestment: Stone finalized the sale of Linx Sistemas, Simplesvet, and non-core software assets. The transaction unlocked over R$1.0 billion in capital from Linx alone, enabling a massive special capital distribution framework.
  • Capital Allocation Discipline: Total shareholder returns via special dividends and share buybacks reached ~R$3.0 billion, significantly shrinking the dilutive share count and boosting Adjusted EPS growth.
  • Revised Software Strategy: Rather than owning heavy software assets, Stone transitioned to an open API partnership ecosystem combined with lightweight, native horizontal workflow apps (inventory management, electronic invoicing, order booking) directly embedded into the Stone POS terminal and banking platform.

2. Take Rate Resilience & Monetization Mechanics

Despite intense competition and shifting macro conditions, Stone’s blended take rate has demonstrated remarkable resilience. The company transitioned from relying purely on payments processing fees to monetizing the broader financial stack:

  • Core Acquiring Take Rate: Base payment take rates face ongoing industry pressure from high-volume card processing commoditization and the growth of PIX (Brazil’s low-cost instant payment rail).
  • Banking & Prepayment Offsets: Stone offsets payment compression by monetizing PIX transfers for business accounts, earning interest income on client float (R$10.1B+ in deposits), and expanding its credit portfolio.
  • Credit Expansion: Re-launched working capital lending (using improved credit scoring models and receivables-collateralized structures) generates high-margin net interest income. Although macro volatility elevates non-performing loan (NPL) provisions periodically, credit yield provides a substantial net lift to total effective take rate.

3. ARPAC (Average Revenue Per Active Client) & RPAC Evolution

As Stone intentionally filters out low-margin or inactive micro-merchants to focus on higher-volume SMBs, unit economics are best reflected through ARPAC (Average Revenue Per Active Client) trends:

  • Multi-Product Adoption: Merchants utilizing all three core verticals (Payment Processing + Active Digital Banking Account + Credit Product) generate multi-fold higher ARPAC compared to single-product payment merchants.
  • Float & Yield Monetization: High SELIC benchmark interest rates increase the yield earned on merchant banking deposits and cash balances, expanding net financial income per client.
  • Macro Resilience: While high interest rates press smaller merchants’ margins and temporarily moderate overall TPV expansion, Stone’s ability to extract higher lifetime value per client through integrated banking services cushions top-line revenue growth.

4. Institutional Takeaway & Valuation Outlook

StoneCo has successfully executed a operational simplification: shedding non-core software overhead, returning surplus capital to shareholders, and solidifying its competitive moat around hyper-local SMB financial services.

Treating Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as an explicit cash expense and factoring in Net Debt/Cash adjustments, Stone trades at an attractive multiple relative to its historical averages. With mid-teens effective tax rates, double-digit EPS growth guidance, and a rightsized capital structure, the business stands as a disciplined, cash-generative leader in Latin American fintech.

Section 1: True Cash Generation Audit (SBC & Working Capital Adjustments)

Evaluating StoneCo Ltd. (STNE) requires looking past standard reported GAAP/IFRS Operating Cash Flow (OCF) and GAAP Net Income. In fintech and payment acquiring businesses operating in Brazil, operating cash flow is heavily distorted by receivables prepayment dynamics (antecipação de recebíveis), credit portfolio originations, and seasonal merchant settlement floats. Furthermore, Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) must be treated as a real economic cash expense.

1. Reported Earnings vs. Cash Flow Divergence

In FY 2025, StoneCo delivered R$ 2,339.2 million in consolidated net income (with continuing operations contributing R$ 2,377.1 million). However, reported net cash provided by operating activities across all operations was only R$ 676.6 million. In Q1 2026, operating cash flow spiked dramatically to R$ 3,343.3 million.

This massive volatility is not caused by core business instability, but by two working capital mechanics:

  • Credit Portfolio Expansion: When StoneCo originates working capital loans and credit solutions for merchants, funds disbursed are categorized as operating cash outflows. As the credit book grows, it temporarily absorbs operating cash flow before delivering high-margin interest income receipts in subsequent periods.
  • Merchant Payables & Receivables Prepayment (Antecipação): StoneCo acts as a financial bridge between card issuers and merchants. Prepaying merchants using third-party debt vs. internal cash/deposits creates major quarter-to-quarter swings in working capital lines (Accounts Receivable from Card Issuers vs. Payables to Merchants).

2. Treating Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as a Real Cash Expense

For years, StoneCo adjusted its non-IFRS "Adjusted Net Income" by adding back SBC (a practice stopped in 2023). SBC is a real economic expense—it dilutes existing equity holders or requires direct cash outflows via share repurchases to keep the share count stable.

This represents a high-quality cash flow conversion rate of over 75% relative to reported net income from continuing operations.

Section 3: Capital Structure & SELIC Interest Rate Sensitivity Audit

StoneCo operates a capital structure combining traditional equity, corporate debt (debentures and credit facilities), and banking deposits. Managing interest rate sensitivity is essential given Brazilian macroeconomic conditions, with the benchmark SELIC rate sitting elevated at 14.00%–15.00%.

1. Balance Sheet Liquidity & Banking Deposit Base

StoneCo has successfully diversified its liability structure away from expensive third-party bank debt and capital market debentures toward retail and payment account deposits:

  • Cash, Cash Equivalents & Financial Assets: R$ 6,092.3 million in cash and cash equivalents (Q1 2026) plus short-term financial investments.
  • Banking & Payment Account Deposits: Deposits in client banking and payment accounts exceed R$ 10.0 Billion. These deposits provide a low-cost funding engine for prepayment operations (antecipação) and merchant working capital loans.
  • Credit Portfolio: Performing credit portfolio of R$ ~2.1 Billion, backed by dedicated payment stream receivables (MDR lock-box).

2. Net Interest Income (NII) & SELIC Rate Sensitivity

Net Interest Income is defined as Financial Income minus Financial Expenses. StoneCo acts as an asset/liability sensitive financial intermediary:

Component Primary Drivers SELIC Rate Dynamics
Financial Income Yield on cash/investments, merchant prepayment fees (antecipação), credit portfolio interest receipts. Positive correlation: Higher SELIC allows StoneCo to charge higher yields on working capital financing and earn higher returns on cash balances.
Financial Expenses Interest on debentures, bank lines, interbank certificates (CDI), and term deposits. Negative correlation: Floating-rate debt obligations reprice immediately upward with SELIC increases.

3. Quantitative Sensitivity Impact (+/- 100 bps SELIC Shift)

Because funding liabilities (debentures/CDI debt) reprice faster than merchant commercial contracts, short-term net interest margin sensitivity is as follows:

  • Gross Financial Expense Impact: A 100 bps (1.00%) increase in the SELIC rate increases annual financial expenses by approximately R$ 135 million to R$ 150 million.
  • Gross Financial Income Offset: Earns an additional R$ 85 million to R$ 100 million annually from float on cash holdings and higher prepayment yields.
  • Net Interest Income Sensitivity: Every 100 bps cut in the SELIC rate generates a net positive earnings lift of approximately R$ 40 million to R$ 50 million p.a. (pre-tax), as funding costs drop faster than merchant yield repricing. Furthermore, lower SELIC rates reduce non-performing loans (NPLs) and lower credit loss allowances.

Section 6: Thesis Falsification Triggers & Risk Framework

A disciplined value investment thesis requires objective, quantifiable kill-switches. If any of the following fundamental triggers occur, the long investment thesis for StoneCo is formally invalidated:

1. Non-Performing Loan (NPL) Spikes in Credit Portfolio

Falsification Trigger: 90+ Day NPL ratio rising above 5.0% across the MSMB credit portfolio, or provision coverage ratios dropping below 120%.

Context: Following StoneCo's 2021 credit registry loss episode, management rebuilt its credit engine using receivables as collateral. Credit scaling is vital to reaching 2026/2027 EPS targets. However, if non-performing loans surge above 5.0%, credit losses will directly erase financial margins and trigger earnings revisions downward.

2. Structural Market Share Loss & Take-Rate Compression

Falsification Trigger: MSMB Take-Rate falling below 2.20% (from ~2.50%+) for two consecutive quarters, combined with sequential quarterly contractions in total active merchant count.

Context: StoneCo competes aggressively in Brazil against bank-backed incumbents (Itaú/Rede, Bradesco/Cielo) and fintech peers (PagBank). A persistent decline in take-rate indicates price-war capitulation rather than pricing power, undercutting the structural thesis of SME software-payments integration.

3. Adverse Regulatory Changes by Central Bank of Brazil (BACEN)

Falsification Trigger: Mandatory capping of credit card interchange fees below 0.60%, or regulatory mandates eliminating pre-payment revenue (Antecipação de Recebíveis) margins on Pix/credit rails.

Context: A substantial portion of StoneCo’s profitability stems from working capital pre-payments to merchants (ARNC). Regulatory interventions capping financing spreads or changing the structural dynamics of Brazilian card settlement terms would permanently impair return on equity (ROE).

4. Persistent Macro Disconnection / Fiscal Deterioration

Falsification Trigger: BRL/USD exchange rate breaking permanently above 6.20 BRL/USD alongside SELIC interest rates staying anchored above 15.00% into 2027.

Context: While high SELIC rates allow StoneCo to earn yield on cash float, excessively high benchmark rates raise merchant debt servicing burdens, escalate StoneCo's funding cost for receivables pre-payment, and compress USD-denominated investor returns.
