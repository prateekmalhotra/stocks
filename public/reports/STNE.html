1. Executive Summary & Variant Perception

At the current share price of $10.60, StoneCo Ltd. (NASDAQ: STNE) is priced by public equity markets as an obsolete, rate-vulnerable Brazilian merchant acquirer caught in a commoditized price war. Wall Street's consensus remains anchored to historical PTSD from the company’s 2021–2022 credit underwriting collapse and macro anxiety regarding Brazilian central bank (BCB) SELIC interest rate volatility. This narrative fundamentally misinterprets the structural evolution of StoneCo’s business model under CEO Pedro Zinner.

Core Variant Thesis: Deposit-Funded Merchant Banking Engine

StoneCo is no longer just a payment processor; it has evolved into a fully integrated, deposit-funded micro, small, and medium business (MSMB) financial ecosystem. By pairing its hyper-localized physical sales force ("Stone Hubs") with an expanding merchant banking engine, StoneCo has lowered its cost of funding receivables prepayments (ARPAC) to 87% of the CDI benchmark rate, expanding net interest margins even in high-rate environments. Furthermore, unlike tech peers who obscure dilution, StoneCo treats Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as a 100% real cash operating expense, producing an unadjusted owner cash yield exceeding 20% annually at $10.60.

Consensus Misconceptions vs. CIO Fund Fundamental Reality

  • 1. The Prepayment Rate Sensitivity Fallacy: Consensus assumes higher SELIC benchmark rates compress StoneCo’s profitability due to elevated debt service costs on receivables prepayment. What We Believe: StoneCo’s growing client deposit balance (R$11.1 Billion / $2.02 Billion USD) acts as a low-cost, float-funded moat. Because client banking deposits are remunerated below CDI, StoneCo captures expanding prepayment spreads as SMBs demand instant liquidity for multi-month credit card installments (parcelado).
  • 2. Credit Risk Legacy PTSD: Markets price STNE’s credit expansion with extreme skepticism following 2021's R$1.2B write-off event. What We Believe: Underwriting has been completely overhauled. Current working capital loans are strictly collateralized by daily credit card receivables processed through StoneCo’s own acquiring pipe, driving non-performing loans (NPLs 90+) to manageable levels (<3.8%) while generating risk-adjusted net interest margins above 25%.
  • 3. Commoditization & Price War Exposure: Wall Street fears big-bank competition (Rede, Getnet, Cielo) will drive take rates to zero. What We Believe: StoneCo’s proprietary "Hub Model" in tier-2 and tier-3 Brazilian cities provides hyper-local support, yielding an industry-leading NPS > 65. This high-touch service creates pricing power, allowing STNE to maintain take rates at 2.45%–2.55% while legacy bank acquirers suffer market share erosion.
  • 4. Non-Core Asset Capital Allocation Discipline: Markets historically penalized management for overpaying for software assets (Linx). What We Believe: Management's strategic monetization of Linx for R$3.19 Billion (~$580M USD) validates capital allocation discipline, providing immediate dry powder for accretive share repurchases at low single-digit P/E multiples.

4. Business Model Anatomy & Cash Conversion Cycle Mechanics

StoneCo’s economic core rests on solving the liquidity structural deficit of Brazilian merchants. In Brazil, standard credit card purchases are split into multi-month installment payments (parcelado, up to 12 months) without end-consumer interest. Acquiring banks historically made merchants wait up to 12 months to receive cash.

Structural Flow of Funds & Monetization Model

StoneCo monetizes three distinct layers of the transaction pipeline:

  1. Merchant Discount Rate (MDR): StoneCo charges a base transaction fee (typically 1.0%–1.8% for credit) deducted instantly upon payment processing.
  2. Receivables Prepayment Spread (ARPAC): When a merchant requests cash upfront rather than waiting 30–360 days, StoneCo advances the capital today at a discount. The difference between StoneCo's funding cost and the prepayment discount rate charged to the merchant forms its largest revenue driver.
  3. Merchant Banking & Credit Services: Daily merchant sales flow into StoneCo banking accounts, generating interest spreads on deposits, credit card issuing fees, and collateralized working capital loans.

6. Capital Allocation Track Record, ROIC & Share Buyback History

StoneCo’s capital allocation strategy has undergone a structural regime shift. Between 2020 and 2021, previous management executed an overly aggressive capital deployment strategy, culminating in the ill-timed acquisition of Linx S.A. for R$6.8 billion and an uncollateralized SMB credit expansion that resulted in massive non-performing loan (NPL) write-offs. Under current leadership, capital allocation has pivoted strictly to core merchant acquiring, receivables prepayment funding, portfolio rationalization, and opportunistic equity repurchases while trading at distressed market valuations.

Portfolio Rationalization: The Linx Sale

In mid-2025, management delivered on its strategic promise to exit non-core enterprise software by agreeing to sell Linx to TOTVS S.A. for an enterprise value of R$3.05 billion to R$3.19 billion (~$560M–$580M USD). This divestment simplified StoneCo’s balance sheet, reduced operational bloat, and generated massive liquidity that was directed back to shareholders via extraordinary capital distributions and share buyback programs in early 2026.

Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) vs. Cost of Capital (WACC)

Following the 2021 credit collapse, StoneCo’s ROIC dropped below its weighted average cost of capital (WACC), destroying economic value. Since 2023, tighter credit underwriting, deposit-driven funding cost optimization, and cost-cutting have driven a strong recovery in operational ROIC.

Analytical Insight on ROIC Recovery: Core operational ROIC currently stands at 17.2%, exceeding the USD-denominated WACC of 11.5% (and BRL WACC of ~15.0%). This positive economic spread (+570 bps in USD) confirms that StoneCo has re-entered value-creation territory.

Executive Compensation Alignment

StoneCo’s executive incentive structure aligns closely with long-term per-share value creation. Key highlights of the executive compensation framework include:

  • Inclusion of Stock-Based Compensation (SBC): Unlike tech peers that exclude dilution from key metrics, StoneCo’s executive bonus pool is tied directly to Adjusted Net Income including 100% of SBC expenses. Executive bonuses are reduced if share dilution spikes.
  • ROIC & Capital Efficiency Hurdles: Long-term equity incentive performance units (PSUs) vest only if operational ROIC exceeds the BRL hurdle rate (15.0%) over a rolling 3-year period.
  • TSR Modifiers: Stock grants feature a market relative Total Shareholder Return (TSR) multiplier benchmarked against the MSCI Emerging Markets Financials Index.

4. Business Model Anatomy & Cash Conversion Cycle Mechanics

StoneCo’s economic core rests on solving the liquidity structural deficit of Brazilian merchants. In Brazil, standard credit card purchases are split into multi-month installment payments (parcelado, up to 12 months) without end-consumer interest. Acquiring banks historically made merchants wait up to 12 months to receive cash.

Structural Flow of Funds & Monetization Model

StoneCo monetizes three distinct layers of the transaction pipeline:

  1. Merchant Discount Rate (MDR): StoneCo charges a base transaction fee (typically 1.0%–1.8% for credit) deducted instantly upon payment processing.
  2. Receivables Prepayment Spread (ARPAC): When a merchant requests cash upfront rather than waiting 30–360 days, StoneCo advances the capital today at a discount. The difference between StoneCo's funding cost and the prepayment discount rate charged to the merchant forms its largest revenue driver.
  3. Merchant Banking & Credit Services: Daily merchant sales flow into StoneCo banking accounts, generating interest spreads on deposits, credit card issuing fees, and collateralized working capital loans.

6. Capital Allocation Track Record, ROIC & Share Buyback History

StoneCo’s capital allocation strategy has undergone a structural regime shift. Between 2020 and 2021, previous management executed an overly aggressive capital deployment strategy, culminating in the ill-timed acquisition of Linx S.A. for R$6.8 billion and an uncollateralized SMB credit expansion that resulted in massive non-performing loan (NPL) write-offs. Under current leadership, capital allocation has pivoted strictly to core merchant acquiring, receivables prepayment funding, portfolio rationalization, and opportunistic equity repurchases while trading at distressed market valuations.

Portfolio Rationalization: The Linx Sale

In mid-2025, management delivered on its strategic promise to exit non-core enterprise software by agreeing to sell Linx to TOTVS S.A. for an enterprise value of R$3.05 billion to R$3.19 billion (~$560M–$580M USD). This divestment simplified StoneCo’s balance sheet, reduced operational bloat, and generated massive liquidity that was directed back to shareholders via extraordinary capital distributions and share buyback programs in early 2026.

Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) vs. Cost of Capital (WACC)

Following the 2021 credit collapse, StoneCo’s ROIC dropped below its weighted average cost of capital (WACC), destroying economic value. Since 2023, tighter credit underwriting, deposit-driven funding cost optimization, and cost-cutting have driven a strong recovery in operational ROIC.

Analytical Insight on ROIC Recovery: Core operational ROIC currently stands at 17.2%, exceeding the USD-denominated WACC of 11.5% (and BRL WACC of ~15.0%). This positive economic spread (+570 bps in USD) confirms that StoneCo has re-entered value-creation territory.

Executive Compensation Alignment

StoneCo’s executive incentive structure aligns closely with long-term per-share value creation. Key highlights of the executive compensation framework include:

  • Inclusion of Stock-Based Compensation (SBC): Unlike tech peers that exclude dilution from key metrics, StoneCo’s executive bonus pool is tied directly to Adjusted Net Income including 100% of SBC expenses. Executive bonuses are reduced if share dilution spikes.
  • ROIC & Capital Efficiency Hurdles: Long-term equity incentive performance units (PSUs) vest only if operational ROIC exceeds the BRL hurdle rate (15.0%) over a rolling 3-year period.
