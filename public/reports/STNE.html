Section 1: The Premise of the Company

StoneCo Ltd. (NASDAQ: STNE) operates as a leading financial technology disruptor in Brazil, delivering an integrated financial ecosystem tailored for Micro, Small, and Medium Businesses (MSMBs). The company’s core business engine combines point-of-sale (POS) card payment processing, real-time Pix instant payment acceptance, digital merchant banking accounts, digital commerce gateway software (Pagar.me), and specialized working capital credit solutions. Following its strategic decision in 2025 to divest non-core enterprise software assets (Linx and Simplesvet), StoneCo returned to its primary operating premise under a unified brand identity: "Stone, the bank for entrepreneurs."

The unit economics of the StoneCo business model rest on three interconnected monetization engines:

  • Payments Acceptance Take Rate: Generating transaction fees on Total Payment Volume (TPV), maintaining a net take rate of 1.0% to 1.2% across card processing and rapidly growing Pix QR-code volume. In Q2 2026, TPV reached R\(142.2 billion ($27.30 billion USD, converted at an exchange rate of R\) 5.21 / $1.00 USD), representing a 4.3% year-over-year reacceleration.
  • Digital Banking & Deposit Float: Merchant clients utilize StoneCo banking accounts as their primary cash hub, driving retail deposits to R$ 10.8 billion ($2.07 billion USD) in Q2 2026 (+20% YoY). This expanding deposit base acts as a low-cost internal funding mechanism, reducing StoneCo's overall cost of capital down to approximately 87% of Brazil's benchmark CDI rate.
  • Working Capital Credit Solutions: Monetizing merchant transaction flows by underwriting working capital loans and revolving credit. The credit portfolio expanded 107.5% YoY to R$ 3.8 billion ($729.4 million USD) in Q2 2026. However, credit quality faced pressure with non-performing loans over 90 days (NPLs >90d) reaching 8.60% and annualized cost of risk touching 21.5%, forcing management to pivot toward lower-ticket, government-backed facilities (e.g., BNDES, FGI Pix).

The primary operational metrics monitored by executive management include: Active Merchant Client Base (4.82 million active clients in Q2 2026), Average Revenue Per Active Client (ARPAC) (R$ 251.1 / month), Total Payment Volume (TPV), Retail Deposits, and Credit Portfolio Scale vs. NPL Ratios.

Corporate Governance, VIE Structure & Capital Allocation Controls

StoneCo Ltd. is an exempted company incorporated under the laws of the Cayman Islands, serving as a holding entity for its operating subsidiaries in Brazil (primarily Stone Pagamentos S.A. and Stone Sociedade de Crédito Direto S.A.). Unlike Chinese ADRs, StoneCo does not operate via a Variable Interest Entity (VIE) contract structure; it exercises direct legal equity ownership over its Brazilian operating subsidiaries, regulated under the oversight of the Central Bank of Brazil (BACEN).

The company maintains a dual-class share structure consisting of Class A common shares (one vote per share) traded on NASDAQ under ticker STNE, and Class B common shares (ten votes per share) held primarily by co-founders André Street and Eduardo Pontes. As of Q2 2026, total basic shares outstanding stood at approximately 243.2 million shares/ADSs following aggressive share buyback execution. Upstream cash flows from Brazilian operating subsidiaries to the Cayman holding company for offshore distributions or ADS buybacks are subject to Brazilian foreign exchange controls and a 10% dividend withholding tax friction. Compliance under the Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act (HFCAA) remains fully satisfied, with principal audit operations inspected by PCAOB-registered international audit firms.

StoneCo stands at an operational inflection point. Over the past 12 months, management executed a major capital reallocation strategy: divesting non-core software assets, distributing an extraordinary cash dividend of $2.53 per ADS (~R\(3.08 billion / $591.2 million USD), and repurchasing ~R\) 3.0 billion ($575.8 million USD) in shares. While payments TPV and deposit growth remain robust, the rapid expansion of the working capital loan book introduced elevated credit loss provisions (R$ 187.6 million / $36.0 million USD in Q2 2026 alone), creating an earnings drag. Management's capital allocation priority has shifted toward tight underwriting discipline, leveraging BNDES-backed facilities, and channeling core Owner Earnings into high-ROIC internal product execution and opportunistic share repurchases below intrinsic value.

Section 2: 3 Probable Business Stories

Based on the company's core premise, audited financial baseline, primary operational metrics, and recent 4-quarter trajectory, here are 3 distinct, probable fundamental paths that cover approximately 90%–95% of probable business outcomes for StoneCo over the next 3 to 5 years:

📖 Story 1: Base Case — The Disciplined Merchant Bank: Banking Float Monetization & Credit Underwriting Normalization

In this baseline path, StoneCo successfully consolidates its strategic repositioning as "Stone, the bank for entrepreneurs," capitalizing on its low-cost deposit engine while disciplining its working capital credit underwriting. Facing a prolonged high-interest rate regime in Brazil (Selic benchmark rate near 12%–14%), management deliberately reins in aggressive high-ticket direct credit origination and pivots toward lower-ticket facilities, government-backed credit programs (such as BNDES and FGI Pix), and payroll-collateralized products. While Pix QR-code volume continues to displace traditional debit cards—exerting minor take-rate pressure—StoneCo offsets this by deepening merchant account engagement and increasing retail deposit float.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: The active merchant client base expands at a steady 4.0% CAGR from 4.82 million to 5.65 million by 2029. Average Revenue Per Active Client (ARPAC) expands moderately from R\(251.1/month ($48.20 USD/month) to R\) 295.0/month ($56.62 USD/month), driven by higher banking solution adoption and cross-selling Pagar.me online/offline payment tools. Annualized TPV grows at a 5.2% CAGR from $109.2B USD (R\(568.8B) to $133.8B USD (R\) 697.1B). The working capital credit portfolio scales conservatively from $729.4M USD (R\(3.8B) to $1.05B USD (R\) 5.47B), prioritizing lower risk profiles. Net revenue grows from the baseline $2.73B USD (R\(14.23B) to $3.38B USD (R\) 17.61B) by 2029 (a 4.4% annualized revenue CAGR).

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Operating margins recover from Q2 2026 lows as non-performing loans over 90 days (NPLs >90d) decline from 8.60% toward 5.50%, driving annualized cost of risk down from 21.5% to 12.0%. Quarterly credit loss provisions fall from $36.0M USD (R\(187.6M) to ~$18M–$20M USD. Retail deposit growth to $3.10B USD (R\) 16.1B) maintains internal funding costs at ~85% of CDI, preserving strong Net Interest Margins (NIM). Maintenance CapEx remains controlled at 3.5% of revenue (~$118M USD/year), while Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) is capped at ~$45M USD/year. Core Owner Earnings compound from the baseline $441.8M USD (R\(2.30B) to $615.0M USD (R\) 3.20B) annually. Management channels cash generation and surplus liquidity into disciplined share repurchases, shrinking shares outstanding from 243.2 million to ~195.0 million, resulting in per-share Owner Earnings compounding at an 11.5% CAGR.

Key Milestones to Watch: NPLs >90d falling below 6.0%; retail deposits surpassing R\(14.0B ($2.69B USD); monthly ARPAC rising above R\) 280; and annual share count reduction exceeding 3.5% per year.

📖 Story 2: Bull Case — High-Yield MSMB Ecosystem Engine: Monetary Easing & Full Cross-Sell Acceleration

In this upside path, Brazil enters a favorable macroeconomic cycle as inflation cools, allowing the Central Bank of Brazil (BACEN) to reduce the Selic rate down toward 9.0%–10.0%. This macro tailwind slashes debt-servicing stress across Micro, Small, and Medium Businesses (MSMBs), driving a surge in entrepreneurial activity and transaction volume. StoneCo leverages its unified Pagar.me and Stone infrastructure to capture substantial market share from legacy Brazilian banks. Advanced AI-driven transaction underwriting allows StoneCo to safely scale its working capital loan book while maintaining pristine credit quality, turning its digital bank into the undisputed monetary hub for Brazilian entrepreneurs.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Active merchant clients expand at an 8.2% CAGR from 4.82 million to 6.60 million by 2029. ARPAC surges from R\(251.1/month ($48.20 USD/month) to R\) 375.0/month ($71.98 USD/month) as merchants utilize StoneCo for 100% of their operational needs—including payments, working capital, payroll, and invoice management. Annualized TPV reaccelerates at a 10.5% CAGR, reaching $160.5B USD (R\(836.2B). The credit portfolio expands rapidly and responsibly to $1.65B USD (R\) 8.60B), fully backed by retail deposits which surge to $4.20B USD (R$ 21.88B). Driven by strong credit income and payment volumes, top-line revenue expands from $2.73B USD to $4.25B USD by 2029 (an 11.7% annualized revenue CAGR).

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Operating leverage takes full effect across fixed software and distribution overhead. Declining defaults lower NPLs >90d to <4.20% and cost of risk down to 9.5%, dramatically reducing expected credit loss provisions. Operating margins expand from 21.0% to 32.5%. Owner Earnings accelerate from the baseline $441.8M USD to $925.0M USD annually by 2029. Because deposit float ($4.20B USD) exceeds the entire credit portfolio ($1.65B USD) by more than 2.5x, StoneCo eliminates high-cost third-party debt funding entirely. Surplus Owner Earnings and net cash allow management to execute $400M+ USD in annual share repurchases alongside dividend payouts, compounding per-share intrinsic value rapidly.

Key Milestones to Watch: BACEN Selic benchmark cuts below 10.5%; credit portfolio growth exceeding 30% YoY with NPLs >90d under 4.5%; retail deposits topping R\(18.0B ($3.45B USD); and monthly ARPAC breaking past R\) 330.

📖 Story 3: Bear Case — Macro Stagflation, High Selic Drag & Credit Delinquency Write-Down Spiral

In this downside path, persistent fiscal instability and inflation force Brazilian monetary authorities to maintain high benchmark interest rates (Selic at 14.0%+ or higher) through 2028. Severe macro pressure severely strains MSMB balance sheets, triggering widespread business liquidations and corporate bankruptcy protection filings across Brazil. StoneCo's rapid credit expansion in late 2025 and early 2026 proves to be toxic, leading to severe delinquency spillovers. Concurrently, rapid merchant adoption of zero-cost Pix QR code payments cannibalizes high-margin debit card processing revenues faster than expected, compressing acquiring take rates while merchant churn rises.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Active merchant clients stagnate and contract from 4.82 million down to 4.40 million by 2029 as small business failures outpace new merchant additions. ARPAC declines from R\(251.1/month ($48.20 USD/month) to R\) 210.0/month ($40.31 USD/month) as financially distressed merchants curtail borrowing and transaction volume. Annualized TPV stagnates at $105.0B USD (R\(547.0B). Management is forced to halt credit origination and aggressively write off distressed accounts, contracting the working capital credit portfolio from $729.4M USD (R\) 3.8B) down to $420.0M USD (R$ 2.19B). Net revenue contracts from the baseline $2.73B USD to $2.20B USD by 2029 (-4.2% annualized CAGR).

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Bad debt losses spike, with NPLs >90d breaching 10.5% and annualized cost of risk staying elevated above 22.0%. Sustained credit loss provisions ($180M+ USD per year) and operational deleverage crush operating margins down from 21.0% to 10.5%. Annual GAAP Operating Cash Flow falls to $320M USD, and core Owner Earnings drop from the baseline $441.8M USD to $165.0M USD by 2029. To satisfy Brazilian Central Bank (BACEN) capital adequacy standards (Managerial Capital Ratio) against non-performing assets, management freezes share buybacks and dividend distributions, retaining all cash offshore and in capital reserves to absorb credit write-downs.

Key Milestones to Watch: NPLs >90d remaining above 9.0%; cost of risk exceeding 20.0%; net quarterly active client losses exceeding 50,000; and total revenue contracting YoY for two consecutive quarters.
