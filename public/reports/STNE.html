Filing Reference: Q1 2026 Form 6-K & Earnings Call (May 14, 2026) | Executive Leadership: CEO Mateus Scherer Schwening & CFO Diego Ventura Salgado

EXECUTION HEAVY Credit Relaunch Capital Return

StoneCo Ltd. has transitioned into a pivotal strategic phase marked by C-suite continuity, non-core asset rationalization (divestiture of Linx), aggressive share capital repurchases, and an expanded focus on Brazilian Micro, Small, and Medium Businesses (MSMBs). Under new Chief Executive Officer Mateus Scherer Schwening and CFO Diego Salgado, StoneCo is repositioning its core acquiring franchise from a pure point-of-sale (POS) processing volume engine into an integrated ecosystem spanning payments, merchant digital banking deposits, and cash-flow-underwritten working capital credit.

First-quarter 2026 results demonstrate resilient operational monetization alongside tight credit quality constraints. While top-line revenue expanded 6.5% YoY to R$ 3.58 billion, gross margin compressed to 41.6% due to elevated credit loss provisioning. Management is actively optimizing its 4.7 million active client base by winnowing unmonetized micro-merchants to protect return on capital in an environment where Selic policy rates remain elevated at 14.00%.

Deep-Dive Business Model & Core Revenue Engines

StoneCo operates a three-pronged monetization flywheel tailored to Brazilian entrepreneurs. The platform creates ecosystem lock-in by capturing daily transaction flows, housing merchant liquidity, and leveraging real-time transaction data to automate credit underwriting.

Monetization Engine Primary Operational Drivers Current Economic Contribution Competitive & Moat Dynamics
1. MSMB Merchant Acquiring & Prepayment Card TPV (R$ 137B), Merchant Discount Rates (MDR), and Advanced Receivables Settlement (Prepayment / PIX QR cash advance). Contributes ~75% of total net revenue. Average Revenue Per Active Client (ARPAC) at R$ 247/month. Proprietary "Stone Hubs" provide direct, hyper-local service teams. Prepayment spreads remain high, but are pressured by Instant Payment (PIX) adoption.
2. Merchant Digital Banking & Deposits Checking accounts, billing tools (Boleto), cash management, retail deposits (R$ 10.1B). Reduces consolidated cost of funds to 87% of CDI. Replaces expensive bank debt funding. High sticky balance ecosystem. Merchant balances serve as internal liability funding for the credit portfolio, creating self-sustaining capital.
3. Cash-Flow Working Capital Credit Working capital loans (R$ 2.9B) and corporate credit cards (R$ 400M). Credit portfolio reached R$ 3.2B. Generated R$ 297M in credit revenue in Q1 2026. High risk-adjusted yield. Underwritten using real-time merchant POS velocity. Principal repayments are automatically deducted from daily credit/debit card receivables, lowering collection friction.

SECTION 3: Cash Flow Quality, Stock-Based Compensation & Capital Allocation Audit

An audit of StoneCo Ltd.'s (NASDAQ: STNE) SEC filings through Q1 2026 (Form 6-K and Form 20-F) reveals a structural transformation. The company has executed a corporate simplification strategy by divesting non-core software assets (Linx and Simplesvet) to re-concentrate entirely on its high-margin financial services ecosystem—comprising payments, merchant banking, and SMB credit products. However, analyzing true underlying cash generation requires stripping out one-off tax adjustments, accounting for merchant prepayment liabilities, and treating Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as an operational cash outflow.

3.1 True Cash Flow Generation & SBC Neutralization

Reported GAAP net income in Q1 2026 reached R$ 1,711.3 million. However, this figure was heavily distorted by a non-recurring deferred tax asset (DTA) recognition tied to goodwill write-offs from the Linx acquisition and software divestiture. Excluding non-operational items, Adjusted Net Income from Continuing Operations stood at R$ 549.1 million (+3.5% YoY).

From an audit perspective, treating Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as a standard non-cash add-back in Free Cash Flow (FCF) calculations creates a false sense of capital efficiency. SBC represents real equity dilution. When a company actively repurchases shares to offset this dilution, SBC transforms into an active operational cash expense. In Q1 2026, StoneCo recorded SBC expenses of R$ 115.0 million ($21.14M USD), bringing trailing twelve-month (TTM) SBC to R$ 321.2 million.

3.2 Share Repurchases vs. Executive Dilution Audit

StoneCo has pursued one of the most aggressive share cannibalization strategies in Latin American fintech. In late 2025, the company completed a massive R$ 1.95 billion repurchasing program, buying back 21,872,021 Class A shares (retiring 8.27% of its total share base) at an average price of R$ 89.15 ($15.80 USD equivalent).

Rather than slowing down, management accelerated open-market repurchases throughout H1 2026 under an active R$ 2.00 billion authorization:

  • March 2026: Repurchased 6,350,579 Class A shares at an average price of $13.74.
  • April 2026: Repurchased 314,401 Class A shares at an average price of $11.47.
  • May 2026: Repurchased 5,420,634 Class A shares at an average price of $10.44.
  • June 2026: Repurchased 7,856,666 Class A shares at an average price of $10.79.

In total, between Q4 2025 and Q2 2026, StoneCo repurchased over 41.8 million Class A shares, successfully driving total outstanding shares down to 243.23 million (comprising 229.18M Class A and 14.05M Class B shares) as of March 31, 2026. This represents a net share count reduction exceeding 14% in 18 months, providing structural EPS support (+15.4% YoY basic EPS growth in Q1 2026 despite modest top-line growth).

Auditor's Dilution Warning: Long-Term Incentive Plan (LTIP) Expansion

Concurrently with approving the extraordinary dividend in April 2026, the Board authorized an expansion of the Long-Term Incentive Plan (LTIP) pool by approximately 3.8 million shares (~1.56% of outstanding shares). While buybacks are significantly outpacing LTIP grants, investors must track LTIP vesting schedules in future Form 6-K filings to verify that executive share grants do not erode buyback accretion.

3.3 Linx Divestiture & $2.53 Extraordinary Dividend Audit

On February 27, 2026, StoneCo officially closed the sale of its Linx software business. On April 14, 2026, the Board approved a landmark one-off extraordinary cash dividend of $2.53 per share (for both Class A and Class B shareholders), totaling approximately R$ 3.08 billion ($570M USD). The dividend was paid on May 4, 2026, to shareholders of record as of April 24, 2026.

Governance & Capital Strategy Evaluation: Management explicitly declared that this payout is a direct, non-recurring pass-through of divestiture net proceeds. It does not establish a formal dividend policy. By returning software cash proceeds directly to shareholders while maintaining buybacks out of operational cash flow, StoneCo demonstrated capital allocation discipline. However, this cash outflow permanently reduces the cash buffer on the balance sheet, elevating the importance of underlying credit portfolio quality.

Section 6: Thesis Confidence, Execution Risk & Surveillance Triggers

Thesis confidence is strictly bounded by StoneCo's transition from a pure-play payment acquirer into an integrated banking and credit provider. While core payment acquiring remains cash-generative, the credit portfolio introduces structural non-performing loan (NPL) risk.

Primary Execution Risks

1. Credit Default Losses & NPL Trajectory: Non-performing loans over 90 days increased to 6.98% in Q1 2026 (up from 5.21% in Q4 2025 and 4.57% in Q1 2025), pushing the Cost of Risk to 21.9%. Unsecured or working-capital credit extended to fragile Brazilian small businesses exposes StoneCo to credit loss spikes if economic activity slows.

2. High SELIC & Macro Volatility: With Brazil's central bank basic interest rate at 14.25%, elevated financial expenses constrain gross profit growth. High rates squeeze merchant operating margins, indirectly impacting credit health and payment volume expansion.

3. Competitive Take-Rate Pressures: Competition from established incumbent banks (Rede, Getnet) and fintech rivals (PagBank, Mercado Pago) restricts pricing power in payment processing fees, placing the burden of margin expansion entirely on software integration and credit cross-selling.
