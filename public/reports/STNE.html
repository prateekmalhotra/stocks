Executive Summary & Thesis Overview

Having successfully shed its capital-intensive software division (Linx) to TOTVS for over R$3.0 billion in early 2026, StoneCo has returned to its pure-play core: the tight triangular convergence of payments processing, banking deposit capture, and merchant credit underwriting. Trading at an ultra-depressed multiple of ~4.4x trailing earnings and holding R$4.94 billion in adjusted net cash (boosted by Linx proceeds prior to the R$3.08 billion extraordinary dividend payout in May 2026), StoneCo presents a classic high-yield, execution-heavy re-rating opportunity. The ultimate thesis success hinges on management's ability to migrate its merchant credit book toward collateralized/secured structures (including BNDES-backed and central bank receivable-registered lines) while re-accelerating Total Payment Volume (TPV) growth.

1. Core Revenue Drivers & Financial Engine

StoneCo’s top-line model relies on monetizing merchant transactions across three primary monetization vectors:

A. Payments Volume & Pix Infrastructure Dynamics

In Q1 2026, StoneCo processed R$137.2 billion in Total Payment Volume (TPV) across Micro, Small, and Medium Businesses (MSMBs). While overall payment volume grew modestly at 2.7% YoY, the structural composition of TPV experienced a significant shift: Pix QR Code transactions grew by nearly 50% YoY, steadily displacing traditional debit cards. Although Pix carries lower nominal take rates than credit card installments, StoneCo successfully offsets volume-based yield compression by bundling Pix with integrated instant settlement and automated working capital sweeps within its banking accounts.

B. Banking Deposit Capture (R$10.1B Foundation)

Retail banking deposits reached R$10.1 billion in Q1 2026 (up 22% YoY, with daily average deposits up 26% YoY) across 3.7 million active banking clients. This low-cost deposit balance is StoneCo’s most critical strategic asset. It provides cheap, sticky local-currency funding that directly lowers StoneCo's cost of capital when financing merchant prepayment receivables (prepaids) and working capital loans, insulating net interest margins (NIM) against high domestic Selic benchmark interest rates.

C. Post-Linx Strategy & Partnership Model

Following the closing of the Linx divestiture to TOTVS in February 2026, StoneCo eliminated the fixed-cost overhead and distraction of managing legacy software systems. Rather than owning the ERP software stack, StoneCo shifted to an API-first marketplace partnership model and native lightweight horizontal business management applications directly embedded in the Stone app. This allows StoneCo to capture software integration value and cross-sell payments and credit to enterprise software users without bearing capital expenditure burden.

D. Credit Portfolio Monetization

Credit revenues reached R$297.1 million in Q1 2026, tripling YoY as the total gross loan portfolio expanded to R$3.22 billion (+14% QoQ). The credit mix is comprised of R$2.9 billion in core merchant solutions (working capital and revolving credit) and R$400 million in credit card solutions.

2. Economic Moat & Competitive Dynamics

StoneCo operates in a competitive oligopoly alongside legacy bank-owned incumbents (Cielo [Bradesco/BB], Rede [Itaú], Getnet [Santander]) and independent fintech challengers (PagBank). StoneCo's durable competitive advantage is built around three core pillars:

Management Commentary on Moat Execution (Q1 2026 Earnings Call):

"Our Stone Hub strategy remains unmatched in tier-2 and tier-3 Brazilian cities. While legacy bank acquirers attempt to sell payments through branch managers, our green-jacket salesforce offers hyper-local, white-glove setup within 12 hours. When a merchant uses Stone for daily card processing, keeps their working capital in Stone Banking, and uses automated receivable deductions for working capital loans, annual churn drops below single digits."

A. The "Stone Hub" Hyper-Local Distribution Ecosystem

Unlike traditional acquiring banks that rely on passive bank branch cross-selling, StoneCo operates over 500+ proprietary "Stone Hubs" across Brazil. Each Hub acts as a localized franchise containing dedicated sales representatives, account managers, and logistics personnel ("green jackets"). This physical proximity allows same-day hardware replacement, local relationship management, and customized point-of-sale setup, driving market share gains in fragmented interior cities.

B. Switching Costs & Ecosystem Lock-In

Switching costs rise exponentially as merchants adopt multiple products across StoneCo’s ecosystem. In Q1 2026, 41% of active clients were classified as "heavy users" (utilizing payments, banking, and credit solutions simultaneously). When a merchant links their point-of-sale terminal directly to their Stone bank account, receivables automatically settle instantly, providing immediate working capital liquidity that no traditional bank can match without extra fee friction.

C. Pricing Power vs. Legacy Incumbents

Against incumbents like Cielo and Rede, StoneCo holds superior pricing power due to bundled software-payments offerings and faster settlement terms. While incumbents often engage in transactional price wars on card processing rates (take rates), StoneCo defends its gross margin profile by monetizing the full client relationship through interest income on float, banking service fees, and credit margins.

3. Operational Friction, Credit Deterioration & Strategic Pivot

The primary overhang on STNE stock is the sharp deterioration in credit quality during late 2025 and Q1 2026. Management is actively responding with underwriting changes and collateralized lending pivots.

A. Analysis of NPL Spike (NPL >90d at 6.98%)

The consolidated credit portfolio saw 90+ day NPLs jump from 4.57% in Q1 2025 to 6.98% in Q1 2026. Consequently, the Cost of Risk reached 21.9%, driving R$166.3 million in expected credit loss provisions. This provision spike compressed Adjusted Gross Margin to 41.6% (down from 44.4% YoY).

  • Macro Compression: Brazil’s central bank (BACEN) maintained the Selic benchmark interest rate higher for longer (~14.0% vs guidance assumptions of ~12.5%), squeezing cash flows for smaller MSMBs.
  • Vintage Seasoning: Credit expansion accelerated sharply throughout 2025 (+100%+ YoY book growth). As these uncollateralized working capital loan vintages aged, default rates naturally hit peak seasoning in Q1 2026.
  • Dedicated Desk Migration: Select larger merchant accounts managed under dedicated coverage desks experienced business distress and migrated into delinquent buckets.

B. Client Churn in 2025 Cohorts

Total unified active clients declined by 5% sequentially in Q1 2026 (from 4.95M to 4.70M). Management intentionally purged low-engagement, single-product micro-merchants onboarded during aggressive 2025 customer acquisition campaigns. These 2025 cohorts exhibited higher price sensitivity and elevated churn when faced with macro headwinds. The strategic shift prioritizes high-ARPAC, multi-product heavy users over raw account counts.

C. Strategic Transition to Secured & BNDES-Backed Credit

To permanently de-risk the loan book, management is executing a rapid structural pivot away from uncollateralized working capital toward guaranteed and secured credit lines:

  • BNDES Guarantee Programs: Partnering with state development banks (BNDES) to utilize credit guarantee funds (FGM/FGO) that absorb up to 80% of first-loss credit exposure.
  • Receivable Registry Lock: Enforcing automated receivables locks via central bank-approved trade repositories (CERC/R3). Loan repayments are withheld at source directly from card settlement streams across all acquirers, dramatically reducing borrower default risk.
  • Tighter Underwriting Vintages: Early data from late-Q1 2026 vintages indicates that First Payment Defaults (FPD) have already converged back toward historical low baselines.

4. Capital Allocation, Cash Position & Shareholder Returns

StoneCo maintains an exceptionally robust balance sheet and aggressive capital return policy:

  • Linx Windfall & Special Dividend: Following the Linx sale, StoneCo paid a monumental one-time extraordinary cash dividend of US$2.53 per share (totaling R$3.08 billion) on May 4, 2026.
  • Share Buyback Cannibal: StoneCo repurchased R$0.6 billion of ordinary shares YTD in 2026, with at least R$1.4 billion remaining under current repurchase authorizations. Total share count has dropped from over 300 million to ~244 million shares.
  • Net Cash & Solvency Buffer: Adjusted Net Cash stood at R$4.94 billion at quarter-end. The managerial Tier 1 Capital Ratio closed at a fortress-like 44.3% (or ~29% excluding temporary Linx dividend proceeds), well above the internal regulatory hurdle of 17%.

4. Execution Risk & Pre-Mortem Falsification Framework

While StoneCo’s valuation offers a high margin of safety, the thesis carries real operational and credit execution risks. The transition of executive leadership to CEO Mateus Scherer Schwening occurs precisely as StoneCo expands its working capital and credit lending portfolio (reaching R$2.8 billion).

Pre-Mortem Thesis Invalidation Triggers (Watchlist Alerts):
  • Trigger 1: Credit Quality Deterioration (NPL Spike): Non-performing loans (NPL >90 days) rising above 6.20% (from 5.21% in recent quarters) or cost of risk exceeding 20% of credit revenue. Impact: Forces severe loan-loss provisioning, wiping out guided gross margins.
  • Trigger 2: Macro Interest Rate Friction (High Selic Hurdle): Persistent Brazilian inflation holding the Selic rate above 11.5% into late 2026/2027. Impact: Compresses MSMB merchant profit margins, increasing churn and elevating financial funding expenses.
  • Trigger 3: Market Share Erosion to Competitors: Gross Payment Volume (GPV) growth slowing below market growth rate due to aggressive pricing pressure from Nu Holdings (Nu), Rede, or PagBank.
  • Trigger 4: Guidance Invalidation / Lower Threshold Test: Failure to reach the lower bound of 2026 adjusted basic EPS guidance (BRL 10.8), triggering a break below the $8.50 lower alert corridor.
