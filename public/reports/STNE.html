1. Executive Summary & Core Variant Perception

StoneCo Ltd. (NASDAQ: STNE) presents one of the most compelling value dislocations in global fintech today. At a current share price of $10.60, the market prices StoneCo like a structural melting ice cube at under 5.0x forward earnings. This extreme valuation discount stems from market memory anchored to the company's 2021 credit platform breakdown, lingering fears over a recent uptick in non-performing loans (NPL 90+ at 7.00% in Q1 2026), persistent high Brazilian benchmark interest rates (Selic ~14%), and the total liquidation of Warren Buffett’s Berkshire Hathaway position. However, these market concerns obscure significant underlying transformation.

The Core Variant Perception: Wall Street views StoneCo through the rearview mirror as a vulnerable, single-product credit card processor facing payment fee compression and credit risk. In reality, StoneCo has successfully transformed into a full-stack digital bank for Brazilian Micro, Small, and Medium Businesses (MSMBs). The company holds R$ 10.1 billion in retail deposits that fund working capital loans at a fraction of competitors' costs, has fully shed its non-core software unit (Linx) for R$ 3.05 billion (returning over $500 million in cash dividends in May 2026), and has retired >20% of its total outstanding shares since 2022.

Our research indicates that current credit provisioning expenses are a manageable consequence of loan book seasoning rather than a systemic repeat of 2021. Under modern Brazilian Central Bank infrastructure (Cerc/Registrato), StoneCo locks merchant credit card cash flows at the settlement layer, eliminating uncollateralized double-pledging risk. With normalized owner cash generation running at R$ 1.6B–R$ 2.0B annually, an Adjusted Net Cash surplus of ~R$ 2.4B, and aggressive share buybacks, StoneCo offers a strong margin of safety with a 3-year Base Case target of $20.80 per share (~25.2% annualized IRR).

2. Business Model Reality & Moat in Plain English

A. How StoneCo Makes Money: The Three-Engine Platform

  • MSMB Payments & Prepayment Spreads (Take Rate ~2.54%): StoneCo processes merchant payments and advances cash for installment sales (antecipação). While pure credit card processing faces modest fee compression, financial income derived from advancing cash to merchants at an attractive spread remains the primary revenue driver.
  • Retail Banking Deposits (The Funding Advantage): Active digital bank accounts hold R$ 10.1 billion in merchant deposits, carrying a low average cost of ~87% of the CDI rate. These organic deposits replace expensive wholesale institutional debt, widening Net Interest Margins (NIM) and expanding Return on Equity (ROE).
  • Working Capital Credit & Credit Cards: Rebuilt on top of central bank receivables registration, StoneCo’s loan book reached R$ 3.2 billion in Q1 2026. Charging monthly yields of 3.1% to 3.3% (~37%–40% annualized), credit generates high-margin revenue (R$ 297M in Q1 2026, up 186% YoY).

C. Addressing Strategic Misconceptions: PIX & Linx

The PIX "Threat" is an Onboarding Advantage: Brazil’s instant payment system (PIX) carries a lower direct payment take rate than traditional credit cards. However, PIX QR code volumes spiked >40% YoY. PIX acts as a zero-CAC customer acquisition engine, driving merchants into Stone’s digital bank accounts where they utilize higher-margin working capital loans and treasury services.

The Linx Divestiture: Completed in February 2026 for R$ 3.05 billion (plus R$ 360M net cash), selling the legacy software division simplified the business, unlocked a one-off $2.53/share special dividend paid in May 2026, and retained ~R$ 3.8 billion in tax goodwill to shelter future operating profits through 2034.

3. Honest Cash Flow, SBC Dilution & Capital Structure

A. Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Audit

Stock-Based Compensation is treated as a 100% real economic expense in our owner earnings analysis. StoneCo recognized R$ 297.0 million in SBC in 2025 (~2.1% of revenue and ~11.4% of reported Adjusted Net Income). Excluding SBC overstates non-GAAP profitability. Deducting SBC and maintenance Capex (~R$ 1.1B/year) from normalized operational cash flow yields a true annual Owner Cash Flow of R$ 1.6B to R$ 2.0B.

B. Share Buybacks vs. Dilution Pace

Management's share repurchase execution has been exceptionally aggressive and disciplined, completely offsetting SBC dilution:

  • Share Count Reduction: Outstanding Class A/B shares dropped from 312.0 million at year-end 2022 to 243.2 million in mid-2026, representing a net retired float of 20.6%.
  • Buyback Efficiency: Capital allocations under the Nov 2024 and May 2025 programs retired ~51 million shares at average prices between $9.51 and $16.34 USD. Buybacks have outpaced SBC share issuance by more than 15 to 1, driving a 33.6% growth in Adjusted Basic EPS in 2025 despite net income growing by a lower 18.6%.
