Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Aflac Incorporated (NYSE: AFL) delivered a resilient operating performance in Q2 2026, demonstrating strong underlying underwriting profitability and aggressive capital deployment despite ongoing foreign exchange headwinds. Total revenues for the second quarter reached $4.12 billion, representing a slight 1.0% year-over-year decline. This revenue compression was primarily driven by the continuous weakening of the Japanese Yen, which depreciated 9.3% against the U.S. Dollar year-over-year (averaging 159.45 JPY/USD in Q2 2026 compared to 144.60 JPY/USD in Q2 2025). The currency depreciation imposed a direct $0.05 per share drag on reported operating results. GAAP net earnings surged 37.7% year-over-year to $825 million ($1.63 per diluted share), up from $599 million ($1.11 per diluted share) in Q2 2025, largely supported by a narrowing of net GAAP investment losses to $153 million compared to $421 million in the prior-year period. Adjusted operating earnings totaled $883 million ($1.75 per diluted share), down 1.7% on a reported basis, but up 1.1% to $1.80 per share on a currency-neutral basis, proving that the business engine remains intact.

Segment-level execution displayed stark regional operational divergence. Aflac Japan, which generates roughly 60% of consolidated earnings, delivered exceptional underwriting margins, expanding its pretax adjusted margin by 230 basis points year-over-year to 34.3%. This expansion was propelled by a 250 basis point improvement in the Japanese benefit ratio to 64.0%, alongside disciplined expense management (expense ratio down 40 bps to 20.2%). Although Japan sales contracted 5.6% YoY in Q2 to ¥11 billion due to a tough comparison against the prior year's launch of Miraito cancer insurance, first-half 2026 Japan sales expanded 7.0% YoY, driven by strong market adoption of the Tsumitasu savings-type life product and Anshin Palette medical insurance. Conversely, Aflac U.S. generated $1.5 billion in net earned premiums (+2.3% YoY) with total sales rising 2.6% to $349 million, but experienced margin compression as its pretax operating margin fell 160 basis points to 20.9%. The U.S. margin decline was caused by a 220 basis point spike in the total benefit ratio to 49.5%, driven by elevated group disability claim severity during the quarter.

Management maintained its disciplined capital allocation philosophy, aggressively cannibalizing its outstanding share count while rewarding long-term shareholders. During Q2 2026, Aflac returned $1.292 billion to owners, comprising $983 million in open-market share repurchases and $309 million in cash dividends ($0.61 per share). This brings year-to-date capital deployment to $2.6 billion. On the investment front, Global Chief Investment Officer Brad Dyslin executed $4.8 billion in targeted portfolio repositioning trades in Q2, shifting low-yielding legacy assets into higher-yielding, high-quality fixed income securities. This repositioning is projected to generate an annualized run-rate lift of over $50 million in Net Investment Income (NII) with minimal capital impact. Furthermore, management expanded its internal reinsurance strategy, raising its target to cede 30% of Japan FSA reserves to offshore internal entities, optimizing balance sheet capital efficiency and supporting future Return on Equity (ROE) expansion. Forward guidance was updated to reflect full-year expectations: U.S. net earned premium growth is now anticipated to settle "just below" the 3.0%–6.0% target CAGR, while the Japan benefit ratio is expected to land near the high end of its 60%–63% full-year target range.

Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

Aflac operates an exceptionally cash-generative, capital-light business model centered on supplemental health, cancer, and voluntary life insurance across two primary geographic markets: Japan (~60% of earnings) and the United States (~40% of earnings). Unlike traditional health maintenance organizations (HMOs) or major medical insurers that reimburse medical providers for healthcare expenses, Aflac's fundamental value proposition is paying direct cash benefits straight to policyholders upon a qualifying medical event (e.g., cancer diagnosis, intensive care hospitalization, disability, or accident). Policyholders utilize these tax-free cash payouts at their own discretion to cover non-medical out-of-pocket expenses, living costs, deductibles, and wage replacement during recovery.

The unit economics of Aflac's underwriting engine are anchored in high-margin, low-ticket monthly premiums (typically $20 to $60 per month per policy) collected primarily via voluntary payroll deductions. In the United States, Aflac maintains access to over 500,000 corporate payroll accounts, distributed through a hybrid network of career commission-only agents and independent benefits brokers. In Japan, where Aflac pioneered cancer insurance in 1974, distribution is fortified through exclusive long-term alliances with Japan Post (spanning 18,000+ post office branches nationwide), major Japanese banks, and thousands of corporate agencies. Because premiums are automatically deducted from employee paychecks or bank accounts, Aflac enjoys extraordinary customer retention. Premium persistency rates stand at an industry-leading 92.7% in Japan and 79.4% in the U.S., establishing an extremely durable subscription-like cash flow stream.

Aflac's competitive moat is constructed on three economic pillars: switching costs embedded in payroll integration, massive scale and brand equity, and a self-funding float mechanism. Once Aflac's benefits platform is integrated into an employer's payroll administration software (e.g., ADP, Workday, Paychex), switching to a rival carrier introduces administrative friction and requires costly employee re-enrollment campaigns. Furthermore, Aflac holds an unrivaled position in Japanese cancer insurance, insuring roughly one out of every four Japanese households. This scale generates a massive investment float—exceeding $110 billion in portfolio assets—which management invests in conservative fixed income, high-grade corporate debt, and private credit assets, generating billions in predictable annual investment yield. Guaranteed-renewable policy structures provide Aflac with long-term pricing power on newly issued policy vintages, allowing the firm to adjust pricing to reflect long-term morbidity trends and medical inflation.

An audit of Aflac's long-term competitive dynamics reveals robust structural demand tailwinds offset by targeted macro and operational risks:

  • Structural Demand Tailwinds:
  • Product Innovation & Youth Penetration: New product launches like Tsumitasu (savings-type life) and Anshin Palette in Japan are successfully expanding Aflac's customer acquisition among younger demographics.
  • Higher Reinvestment Yield Environment: Higher global interest rates allow Aflac to deploy portfolio cash flows into higher-yielding investment-grade corporate bonds and private debt, as demonstrated by the $4.8 billion Q2 portfolio repositioning yielding an immediate $50M+ NII run-rate expansion.
  • Shift to Voluntary Workplace Benefits: U.S. employers increasingly utilize voluntary supplemental benefits to attract talent while shifting higher healthcare deductibles onto employees, expanding Aflac's addressable payroll account market.
  • Demographic Aging in Japan and the U.S.: Japan's super-aging society and the aging U.S. workforce drive relentless demand for supplemental cancer, medical, and long-term care financial protection.
  • Competitive Disruption & Operational Threats:
  • Digital Disruption in Voluntary Benefits: Insurtech platforms and traditional group carriers (e.g., Guardian, Sun Life, Unum) are investing heavily in automated payroll integration platforms, increasing competition for top-tier broker distribution channels.
  • Distribution Partner Concentration: Reliance on Japan Post for Japanese distribution exposes Aflac to potential structural shifts in partner commissions or regulatory distribution mandates.
  • U.S. Disability Claims Inflation: Rising claim severity in U.S. group disability insurance (reflected in the 220 bps benefit ratio spike in Q2 2026 to 49.5%) represents an ongoing underwriting margin pressure point.
  • Japanese Yen FX Volatility: Prolonged weakness in the JPY/USD exchange rate penalizes consolidated U.S. Dollar revenue and earnings reporting, masking local-currency earnings growth.

Section 3: Real Cash Flow, SBC Dilution & Owner Earnings Audit

A rigorous audit of Aflac Incorporated's cash flow statement requires stripping away non-cash accounting adjustments, mark-to-market revaluations, and deferred acquisition cost (DAC) adjustments. In the supplemental insurance business, GAAP operating cash flow can be obscured by net investment losses, expected credit loss (ECL) provisions, and derivative hedging noise. For the six months ended June 30, 2026, Aflac generated $1,080 million in operating cash flow, impacted by foreign currency translation as the Japanese Yen averaged 159.45 per U.S. Dollar compared to 144.60 in the prior-year period.

From a first-principles perspective, Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) is never an "accounting add-back." SBC represents real value transferred from existing equity owners to corporate managers. While tech companies routinely abuse SBC, Aflac maintains commendable discipline, incurring $50 million to $55 million annually in SBC. Every dollar of SBC is deducted 100% as a true cash expense, representing the cost required to neutralize share dilution through open-market repurchases.

Unlike asset-heavy industrial or retail models, Aflac's maintenance capital expenditures are minimal—consisting primarily of IT infrastructure, digital policy management software, and security enhancements across its Columbus, Georgia and Tokyo operating hubs. Annual maintenance CapEx averages $70 million to $80 million. Subtracting both SBC and maintenance CapEx from GAAP operating cash flow yields Warren Buffett Owner Earnings—the true discretionary cash available to distribute to shareholders without impairing the enterprise's underlying competitive moat.

Buffett Owner Earnings Discipline: Insurance cash flows are driven by upfront premium collections and deferred claim payouts. Operating cash flow in 2024–2026 reflects foreign exchange translation headwinds due to JPY weakness. However, because Aflac matches JPY liabilities with JPY assets in Japan, local currency cash generation remains exceptionally stable, throwing off $2.2B+ in true annual Owner Earnings to the parent company.

Working Capital Float & Customer Prepayment Dynamics

Aflac's economic engine operates on a negative working capital cycle, commonly referred to as "insurance float." Policyholders pay premiums upfront for supplemental health, cancer, and accident coverage, while benefit claims and policy payouts occur months or years into the future. As of June 30, 2026, Aflac held $64,348 million ($64.35 billion) in total policy liabilities, representing future policy benefits and unearned premiums.

This policyholder float acts as zero-cost capital that Aflac invests across its $108.4 billion investment portfolio (comprising fixed maturity securities, middle-market commercial loans, and infrastructure assets). In Japan—which generates roughly 51% of adjusted revenues and accounts for 75% of total company assets—policy premiums are received in Yen and held locally to back long-dated Yen benefit liabilities. This creates an organic operational hedge against long-term currency fluctuations, even though quarterly USD reporting undergoes translation volatility.

To evaluate corporate financial risk, parent debt obligations must be weighed against liquid holding company assets. Section 5: Warren Buffett Owner Earnings Intrinsic Valuation Matrix

To value Aflac Incorporated (NYSE: AFL) through the lens of Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger, we evaluate the firm as an unquoted private operating business producing cash flows across two primary sovereign jurisdictions: Japan and the United States. We reject arbitrary Wall Street exit multiples, which rely on the premise of selling to a "greater fool" in Year 5. Instead, valuation is rooted in normalized Owner Earnings—defined as reported operating earnings adjusted for non-cash foreign currency remeasurements, net investment gains/losses, reserve adjustments, and maintenance capital expenditures.

For an insurer like Aflac, Owner Earnings represent the cash generated that can be extracted from the enterprise or redeployed into per-share compounding without harming its regulatory solvency or underwriting capacity. In the first half of 2026, Aflac generated $1.78B in adjusted operating earnings ($883M in Q2 2026 and $901M in Q1 2026), translating to a normalized run-rate Owner Earnings baseline of approximately $3.58B per annum, or $7.15 per diluted share on 502.3M shares outstanding.

Rather than applying a generic U.S. discount rate, Buffett's "PetroChina / Iscar Rule" dictates that an international business must be discounted using a localized sovereign risk baseline that reflects the geographical distribution of its cash generation:

  • Japan Segment (~60% of Economic Earnings): Baseline anchored in the 10-Year Japanese Government Bond (JGB) yield (~1.10%).
  • U.S. Segment (~40% of Economic Earnings): Baseline anchored in the 10-Year U.S. Treasury yield (~4.25%).
  • Blended Risk-Free Rate: (0.60 × 1.10%) + (0.40 × 4.25%) = 2.36%.

Section 6: Thesis Invalidation Pre-Mortem & Falsification Triggers

A rigorous fundamental evaluation requires defining the exact conditions under which the bullish investment thesis breaks. Aflac operates a high-margin supplemental health business with exceptionally sticky customer persistency (92.7% in Japan). However, several operational, credit, and foreign exchange risks could permanently impair its Owner Earnings power.

Pre-Mortem Risk Analysis:

  • Uncontrolled Yen Depreciation & Currency Mismatch: While Aflac hedges much of its capital matching in Japan, an unmitigated spike in USD/JPY beyond 170.00 severely dilutes dollar-reported Owner Earnings and reduces parent company dividend transfer capacity from Aflac Japan to Aflac Incorporated.
  • Credit Losses in $4.8B Repositioned Investment Portfolio: To offset low Japanese yields, Aflac completed a record $4.8B investment portfolio pivot toward middle-market private credit, direct lending, and commercial real estate debt. An adverse macroeconomic credit cycle resulting in default spikes or double-digit markdowns would erode regulatory capital (RBC) and force capital diversion away from buybacks to reserve reinforcement.
  • Underwriting & Claims Inflation Spike: In the U.S. segment, rising medical cost inflation and utilization spikes could drive benefit ratios above targeted ranges, eroding U.S. pretax margins (which declined 160 bps YoY to 20.9% in Q2 2026). In Japan, aging demographics could increase cancer and medical rider claim frequency.
  • Reinsurance Counterparty Risk: Expanding internal and external reinsurance targets (e.g., Aflac Re Bermuda entering transactions with Japan Post Insurance for up to 30% of FSA reserves) introduces third-party credit and structural execution risk if reinsurers fail to meet liability obligations.
Mandatory Thesis Falsification Triggers: Immediate downgrade to AVOID / CAUTION and recommendation of capital exit will occur if any of the following explicit numerical thresholds are breached over two consecutive quarters:
  1. USD/JPY Foreign Exchange Floor: USD/JPY exchange rate sustaining above 170.00 without local product price increases, driving dollar-denominated Japan adjusted earnings down by >15%.
  2. Japan Total Benefit Ratio Ceiling: Japan Total Benefit Ratio rising above 67.5% (compared to 64.0% in Q2 2026).
  3. Portfolio Impairment Threshold: Credit defaults or realized impairments on the $4.8B repositioned portfolio exceeding 120 basis points per annum ($57M+ annual credit losses).
  4. Capital Return & Buyback Curtailment: Annual share repurchase allocation falling below $1.80B (a 45% reduction from the current ~$3.5B+ annual pace) without a clear, accretive capital deployment alternative.
  5. Adjusted Return on Equity Floor: Adjusted ROE excluding foreign currency remeasurement dropping below 13.0% (down from 16.6% in Q2 2026).

Execution Risk Rating & Margin of Safety Corridor:

Aflac maintains a Low-to-Moderate Execution Risk Rating due to its fortress balance sheet, regulatory solvency buffers (Combined RBC > 600%, Economic Solvency Ratio at 226%), and dominant market share in Japanese supplemental health. At the current price of $121.45, the stock trades at a 25.9% discount to Base Case Intrinsic Value ($164.00), offering an adequate Margin of Safety for disciplined value investors seeking steady per-share cash flow compounding.
