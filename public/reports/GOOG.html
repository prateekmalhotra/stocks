Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Alphabet Inc. (NASDAQ: GOOG, GOOGL) is undergoing an unprecedented structural transition from a high-margin, ad-driven Search monopoly into an integrated enterprise AI compute and cloud utility. In Q2 2026, Alphabet delivered consolidated revenues of $119.80 billion, representing a 24.0% YoY increase (23% in constant currency) and marking its 12th consecutive quarter of double-digit top-line growth. Consolidated operating income surged 30% YoY to $40.80 billion, driving a 160 basis point expansion in operating margin to 34.0%. However, the defining headline of the quarter was the financial manifestation of Alphabet's massive infrastructure buildout: quarterly capital expenditures doubled YoY to $44.90 billion, outpacing $39.10 billion in operating cash flow and driving quarterly Free Cash Flow into negative territory (-$5.86 billion) for the first time in company history.

The core engine behind Alphabet's top-line acceleration is Google Cloud, which saw revenue skyrocket 82.0% YoY to $24.80 billion. Cloud operating income tripled to $8.80 billion as operating margins expanded from 20.7% in Q2 2025 to 35.6% in Q2 2026, demonstrating substantial operating leverage on incremental enterprise AI workloads. Google Cloud's contract backlog expanded to $514 billion. Crucially, fears of AI-driven cannibalization of legacy Search proved unfounded: Search & Other advertising revenues grew 17.0% YoY to $63.30 billion, driven by higher query volumes from global rollouts of AI Overviews and AI Mode, which have boosted user engagement and commercial intent density. Paid consumer subscriptions across YouTube Premium, YouTube Music, YouTube TV, and Google One reached 350 million.

To support enterprise AI demand that continues to exceed available server and data center capacity, management raised its FY2026 CapEx guidance from $180B–$190B to $195B–$205B and signaled further spending increases in FY2027. To fund this generational infrastructure land-grab without impairing liquidity, Alphabet executed an upsized ~$85 billion equity capital raise in June 2026—comprising a $10 billion direct strategic investment from Berkshire Hathaway and a $35 billion allocated underwritten equity offering—alongside $20.3 billion in senior unsecured notes and a newly authorized $40 billion At-The-Market (ATM) equity program. While this equity issuance introduces near-term share dilution, management views it as an essential step to secure foundational compute power and cement Alphabet's full-stack AI competitive advantage.

Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

Alphabet generates revenue through three primary operational pillars: Google Services (Search advertising, YouTube ads, Google Network, and consumer subscriptions), Google Cloud Platform (GCP enterprise infrastructure, Vertex AI, and Gemini SaaS licenses), and Other Bets (including Waymo autonomous driving). The core economic flywheel relies on high-margin advertising cash flows from Search ($63.30B in Q2 2026) and YouTube ($9.9B+ in Q1/Q2 2026) funding the massive capital spending required to transform GCP into the dominant enterprise AI utility. Unit economics in Search remain extremely favorable, benefiting from near-zero incremental marginal costs on additional user queries, while GCP unit economics are rapidly scaling as capacity utilization rises, lifting GCP operating margins from negative territory three years ago to 35.6%.

Alphabet's competitive moat is shifting from a pure network-effect ad ecosystem to a vertically integrated, full-stack enterprise compute platform. High customer switching costs are established through GCP's Vertex AI ecosystem, where enterprise databases, custom fine-tuned Gemini models, security protocols, and workflow agent integrations are deeply embedded into corporate IT infrastructure. Furthermore, Alphabet possesses structural cost advantages derived from its proprietary hardware stack: its custom Tensor Processing Units (TPU v5p and Ironwood TPU v6) co-optimized with Jax/TensorFlow frameworks allow Alphabet to serve Gemini 3.5 inference at a 30%+ lower cost per token compared to competitors relying exclusively on third-party commercial GPUs. This hardware efficiency translates directly into enterprise pricing power and expanding cloud operating margins.

1. Deconstructing Buffett Owner Earnings & Capital Allocation Reality

In his 1986 letter, Buffett defined Owner Earnings as:

Owner Earnings = Reported Operating Cash Flow - Maintenance CapEx - Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) +/- Working Capital Adjustments

Applying this formula to Alphabet's current financial profile reveals the critical dichotomy between peak GAAP cash flow distortion and underlying normalized earning power:

  • Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Discipline: Stock-based compensation ($26.0 billion TTM) is treated as a 100% real cash expense. Issuing equity to employees dilutes existing shareholders or consumes cash via stock buybacks required to sterilize dilution. It must be fully deducted from cash from operations.
  • Maintenance CapEx vs. Discretionary Growth CapEx: Alphabet's updated 2026 CapEx guidance of $195.0B–$205.0B (up from $91.5B in 2025) represents an unprecedented infrastructure expansion. Management disclosures indicate that 40% of CapEx is directed toward long-duration land, data center shells, and power interconnects, while 60% funds shorter-cycle technical infrastructure (NVIDIA H100/B200 clusters and custom Trillium TPUs). We calculate baseline Maintenance CapEx—the capital strictly required to maintain Google Search's unit query volume, YouTube infrastructure, and core legacy Cloud operations—at $38.0 billion. The remaining ~$162.0 billion represents hyper-aggressive discretionary Growth CapEx designed to capture the enterprise AI cloud land grab and service Google Cloud's $460+ billion commercial backlog.
  • Normalized Owner Earnings Calculation: Deducting $38.0B in Maintenance CapEx and $26.0B in SBC from $185.0B in Operating Cash Flow yields $121.0 billion in Normalized Mid-Cycle Owner Earnings ($9.85 per diluted share across 12.28 billion shares). At the $343.00 stock price, Alphabet offers an initial normalized Owner Earnings yield of 2.87%.
