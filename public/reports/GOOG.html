Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

Alphabet generates economic profit through a multi-layered monetization ecosystem anchored by two core engines: high-margin digital advertising attention monopolies and hyper-scaling enterprise cloud compute infrastructure. Google Services ($94.5B revenue in Q2 '26, +15% YoY) captures high-margin rents by matching user intent across Google Search & Other ($63.8B), YouTube Advertising ($9.8B), and Subscriptions/Platforms ($20.9B). Search unit economics are characterized by incremental gross margins exceeding 75%, where query monetization relies on dynamic real-time ad bidding. Google Cloud ($24.8B revenue, +82% YoY) monetizes raw platform compute (GCP), custom TPU system capacity, and enterprise AI SaaS through Gemini Enterprise seats across nearly 90% of Fortune 100 clients.

Customer switching costs and network effects form a deep, highly defensible moat around core Search and Cloud platforms. In consumer Search, user habituation across 2.0+ billion Android operating devices, Chrome browser dominance, and default search distribution partnerships create near-zero customer churn and unparalleled intent data. In enterprise Cloud, switching costs are switching-friction heavy; migrating multi-petabyte datasets, custom enterprise LLM fine-tuning weights, and proprietary security workflows away from GCP creates massive technical inertia. Furthermore, Google's proprietary vertical integration—designing in-house custom Silicon (TPU v5p and TPU v6 Trillium) connected over its proprietary optical circuit switched (OCS) Virgo networks—yields a 30%–40% total cost of ownership (TCO) advantage over competitors reliant exclusively on merchant GPUs.

Treating Stock-Based Compensation as a Real Cash Expense

Following Warren Buffett’s principle that "if stock options aren't compensation, what are they? And if compensation isn't an expense, what is it?", 100% of SBC is treated as a direct cash expense and shareholder dilution factor. TTM SBC expense reached $28.15 billion ($14.71 billion in 1H 2026 alone). When SBC is excluded from cash flow add-backs, it directly reduces distributable cash flow to owners. Furthermore, with Alphabet pausing its open-market share buyback program during 1H 2026 to prioritize AI compute infrastructure, SBC dilution is no longer being actively sterilized by share repurchases, making it a direct economic cost borne by existing equity holders.

Maintenance CapEx vs. Discretionary Growth CapEx Disaggregation

To compute true Buffett Owner Earnings, total CapEx must be separated into maintenance CapEx (capital required to protect unit volume, maintain legacy Google Search/YouTube infrastructure, and refresh routine server capacity) and growth CapEx (discretionary multi-year expansion of Custom TPU clusters, Blackwell GPU nodes, and fiber networks for external Google Cloud enterprise customers).

Out of the $132.40 billion in TTM CapEx and the projected $200.00 billion FY 2026 CapEx, approximately 60% is directed toward technical compute servers and 40% toward long-life data center facilities and fiber real estate. Crucially, maintenance CapEx required to preserve the legacy Search and YouTube advertising moat is estimated at $32.00 billion TTM ($38.00 billion FY 2026 baseline). The remaining ~$162 billion in FY 2026 CapEx represents growth capital earmarked to service Google Cloud's $514 billion backlog and Gemini enterprise compute demand.

Section 5: Warren Buffett Owner Earnings Intrinsic Valuation Matrix

In his 1986 Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letter, Warren Buffett outlined the foundational definition of intrinsic value: "If we think through these questions, we can gain some insight into what owner earnings are... these represent (a) reported earnings plus (b) depreciation, depletion, amortization, and certain other non-cash charges... less (c) the average annual amount of capitalized expenditures for plant and equipment, etc. that the business requires to fully maintain its long-term competitive position and its unit volume."

To value Alphabet Inc. (GOOG) accurately at the current stock price of $343.00, we reject Wall Street's reliance on arbitrary Year-5 exit multiples—which Charlie Munger famously dismissed as "relying on a greater fool." Instead, we derive intrinsic value strictly through normalized Owner Earnings cash flows, adjusted for the capital intensity of the AI supercycle, stock-based compensation, and localized sovereign discount rates.
