01

What the Market is Pricing In

At $125.20 per share (13.1x normalized Owner Earnings and 15.5x EV/OE), the market is pricing Autoliv as a premier Tier-1 auto supplier operating at the upper boundary of its mid-cycle earning power. While the headline multiple implies moderate valuation relative to the broader market, it reflects deep-seated institutional skepticism regarding the sustainability of adjusted operating margins (targeted at 10.5%–11.0%) against flat-to-contracting global light vehicle production (LVP). Investors fear that previous commercial inflation compensation agreements negotiated with OEMs have run their course, leaving Autoliv exposed to fresh tariff friction, sticky wage inflation, and OEM pushback on non-recurring engineering reimbursements.

Furthermore, the market heavily discounts Autoliv’s customer mix transition toward domestic Chinese original equipment manufacturers (now over 55% of Autoliv’s China revenue base). While order-intake win-rates with domestic Chinese OEMs exceed 45% (supporting triple-digit launch growth with BYD, Geely, and XPENG), investors view this as structurally margin-dilutive. The aggressive domestic EV price war, lower average safety content per vehicle in budget platforms, and extended customer payment terms in the Chinese supply chain threaten to cap incremental returns on capital.

Business Cyclicality & Cycle Stance
Howard Marks & Warren Buffett mental models for cycle positioning and earnings durability.
1. Cyclicality Profile (The Nature of the Business)
Secular Compounder
Mission-critical software, tolls, or subscriptions (e.g. Microsoft, Visa, Apple). Revenue and cash flows compound steadily through economic downturns.
Moderate Cyclical
Consumer retail, merchant payments, or advertising (e.g. StoneCo, Amazon, Lululemon). Earnings fluctuate with interest rates and consumer spending, but long-term secular growth continues.
Deep Cyclical
Commodities, homebuilders, freight shipping, or manufacturing (e.g. NVR, Vale, GigaCloud). Earnings swing between extreme peaks and valleys. Never extrapolate peak earnings.
2. Cycle Stance (Where the Business Sits Today)
Downcycle Contraction / Trough
Operating under high interest rates, credit contractions, or inventory destocking. Starting earnings are depressed, offering asymmetric upside when conditions normalize.
Mid-Cycle Run-Rate
Operating near sustainable historical margins and normal unit economics. Reliable baseline for 3-year compounding calculations.
Peak / Over-Earning Risk
Generating temporary peak margins due to shortages or bubble pricing. Downside risk if margins mean-revert to historical levels.
