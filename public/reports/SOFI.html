01

What the Market is Pricing In

At the current share price of $18.91, SoFi Technologies trades at a demanding multiple of 65.0x normalized Owner Earnings ($0.29 per share) and 54.7x EV/OE. Mr. Market is pricing in a severe operational disconnect between SoFi’s headline volume growth and its true underlying economic earnings power. Skeptics view SoFi not as a high-margin enterprise fintech software platform, but as a balance-sheet-heavy consumer lender masquerading as a tech company. The market fears that reported earnings are artificially elevated due to mark-to-model fair value accounting (ASC 820) on personal loan originations, where gain-on-sale margins are recognized upfront while credit risk remains on the balance sheet.

Furthermore, the narrative surrounding SoFi's B2B Technology Platform (Galileo and Technisys) has weakened following a 23% YoY revenue drop in early 2026 and a 16% contraction in total accounts. Short-sellers and forensic analysts point to elevated true net charge-offs—arguing that delinquent loan sales mask an all-in annualized charge-off rate of 3.7% to 6.1%—and continuous dilution from $280 million in annual stock-based compensation. Consequently, Mr. Market is treating current valuation multiples as vulnerable to severe contraction if credit impairments tick up or net interest margins compress as Fed rate cuts squeeze deposit yield economics.

On the balance sheet, SoFi maintains a strong liquidity cushion with $7,352.2M in liquid cash and investments against $3,301.0M in total debt, yielding $+4,051M in net cash ($+3.00 per share). While the B2B Tech Platform segment faced client loss headwinds ($85M revenue in Q2 2026), the Lending and Financial Services divisions continue to replace warehouse credit lines with $45.5B+ in lower-cost member deposits, sustaining attractive asset-liability spreads.

However, forensic details require careful monitoring. SoFi retained $11.7B (79%) of its $14.8B Q2 originations directly on its balance sheet, leaving its credit exposure heavily weighted in personal loans ($10.7B). Management's reported Personal Loan NCO rate of 2.62% benefits from late-stage delinquent loan sales; excluding these offloadings, the all-in annualized NCO rate reaches 3.7%, emphasizing the need for disciplined underwriting as loan vintages mature.
