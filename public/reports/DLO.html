Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

DLocal Limited (“dLocal”) represents a specialized, high-velocity financial technology infrastructure platform engineered to solve the acute friction of cross-border and localized payment processing across 60+ complex emerging markets in Latin America, Africa, and Asia. In its Q2 2026 financial performance, dLocal demonstrated significant operational momentum, delivering a record Total Payment Volume (TPV) of $17.69 billion—a 92.1% year-over-year surge that marks seven consecutive quarters of 50%+ TPV growth. Top-line quarterly revenue expanded 55.8% year-over-year (69% on an FX-neutral basis) to reach $399.7 million, comfortably exceeding consensus estimates. This top-line expansion was powered by accelerating digital merchant adoption in key regions like Brazil and Argentina, alongside explosive volume scaling across high-growth verticals, including ride-hailing, e-commerce, digital assets, and streaming services. Net revenue retention (NRR) reached 153%, marking the fifth consecutive quarter above 140%, underscoring the expanding wallet share dLocal captures as global enterprise clients scale their emerging market footprints.

From an owner earnings cash generation perspective, dLocal generated a record $127.2 million in quarterly gross profit (+28.6% YoY) and $64.2 million in operating profit (+15.0% YoY). Operating leverage showed tangible sequential recovery, with the operating profit-to-gross profit ratio expanding by 600 basis points quarter-over-quarter to reach 50.5%. Adjusted Free Cash Flow rose 41.2% year-over-year to $68.55 million, representing a robust 125% FCF conversion rate relative to GAAP net income ($54.8 million, or $0.18 diluted EPS). While top-line growth outpaced gross profit growth—driven by a natural shift toward lower-margin local-to-local transaction volumes (61% of total TPV) and high-volume merchant tiering—management raised its full-year 2026 TPV growth guidance to 60%–70% YoY and gross profit growth guidance to 25%–30% YoY, while maintaining operating profit growth guidance at 27.5%–32.5%.

Capital allocation remains disciplined and shareholder-yield focused, guided by a debt-free operational balance sheet anchored by $794.9 million in total cash and cash equivalents. Under the $300 million share repurchase authorization initiated in March 2026, dLocal deployed $86.1 million during Q2 to buy back and permanently cancel 6.9 million Class A common shares, reducing total share count and directly enhancing per-share intrinsic value. Furthermore, the company bolstered its strategic liquidity by securing a new $150 million senior unsecured credit facility maturing in 2029 at Term SOFR + 2.0%. Institutional backing remains concentrated among long-term growth compounders: General Atlantic holds a 15.8% equity stake (46.66 million shares), while fundamental LatAm asset manager Atmos Capital Gestão de Recursos maintains a 3.83 million share position.

Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

dLocal operates a capital-light, enterprise-grade financial technology bridge that enables global enterprise merchants (such as Amazon, Uber, Spotify, Meta, and major AI/digital asset platforms) to accept local payment methods (pay-ins) and disburse funds (pay-outs) across emerging markets without establishing local corporate entities in every operating jurisdiction. dLocal monetizes this infrastructure by charging enterprise clients a percentage-based fee or fixed transaction fee on Total Payment Volume (TPV). The company’s unit economics are governed by the spread between its gross take-rate and the direct cost of payments (interchange fees, local scheme processing costs, acquirer markups, and cross-border FX conversion costs). In Q2 2026, dLocal's overall gross take-rate averaged ~2.26% ($399.7M revenue on $17.69B TPV), down from prior years due to three structural factors: tiered pricing volume discounts hit by scaling enterprise clients, an increasing mix of local-to-local transaction flows (which carry lower nominal take-rates but lower operational friction), and rapid penetration in lower-take-rate verticals like ride-hailing and remittances.

Despite take-rate compression, dLocal's economic flywheel creates structural switching costs and network effects that form a durable competitive moat. Global merchants integrate dLocal via a single API direct connection (“One dLocal”). Through this single technical integration, a global enterprise instantly gains access to over 900 local payment methods across 60+ countries, including instant payment rails (Pix in Brazil, UPI in India, OXXO in Mexico), local credit/debit card schemes, digital wallets, and local bank transfers. Re-architecting this infrastructure through disparate local acquiring relationships or competing payment service providers requires years of complex regulatory licensing, localized compliance setup, tax clearing mechanisms, and heavy engineering overhead. Consequently, once embedded, dLocal acts as an indispensable operational utility, evidenced by its extraordinary 153% Net Revenue Retention rate and 188% TPV retention rate.

An audit of dLocal's long-term competitive dynamics reveals powerful structural demand drivers balanced by measurable disruption threats:

  • Structural Demand Drivers (Long-Term Secular Tailwinds):
  • Enterprise Ecosystem Breadth & Product Expansion: Deepening penetration into high-growth verticals like generative AI services, digital asset exchanges, and merchant-of-record solutions (dMore) and Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) across 8 markets.

  • Competitive Disruption Threats & Headwinds:

  • Enterprise Take-Rate Compression: As mega-merchants (e.g., ride-hailing giants) achieve immense scale on dLocal's network, tier-based contractual fee schedules naturally depress gross take-rates and gross profit margins (gross margin fell from 39% to 32% YoY).

Section 3: Real Cash Flow, SBC Dilution & Owner Earnings Audit

A rigorous Graham & Buffett cash flow evaluation of dLocal Limited requires piercing through Wall Street’s preferred "Adjusted EBITDA" and "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" metrics to expose the underlying economic cash generation of the enterprise. In cross-border payment processing, reporting conventions frequently obscure true profitability by blending merchant settlement balances with corporate cash and by treating Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as an ignorable non-cash add-back. Under our doctrine, SBC is 100% a real cash expense—it either dilutes existing owners or requires direct cash outlays via share repurchases to neutralize dilution.

The Anti-Silicon Valley SBC Rule: Stock-based compensation is not manna from heaven; it is compensation paid in equity rather than cash. If a company issues $25M in stock to employees, existing shareholders suffer immediate economic dilution. In our Owner Earnings framework, 100% of SBC is deducted directly from Operating Cash Flow alongside Maintenance CapEx to determine true distributable cash flow.

Furthermore, dLocal's GAAP Operating Cash Flow exhibits extreme volatility due to working capital swings in merchant settlement accounts. In periods of rapid TPV acceleration (such as Q2 2026's 92% YoY TPV expansion to $17.7B), merchant receivables and payables fluctuate by tens of millions of dollars based on settlement calendar timing across 60+ emerging market jurisdictions. To establish normalized Buffett Owner Earnings, these temporary merchant float swings are fully stripped out.

Working Capital Float & Customer Prepayment Dynamics

DLocal operates a classic pay-in and pay-out cross-border architecture. In pay-in transactions, dLocal collects local fiat currency (e.g., Brazilian Real via Pix, Mexican Peso via OXXO, Nigerian Naira) from consumers on behalf of global enterprise merchants (such as Spotify, Amazon, Uber, and emerging AI platforms). In pay-out transactions, dLocal disburses local currency to local suppliers or ride-share drivers on behalf of global platforms.

Because local payment rails process funds faster than international treasury clearance cycles, dLocal temporarily holds substantial merchant funds on its balance sheet. As of Q2 2026, total cash and equivalents stood at $794.9M, of which $425.8M represented merchant settlement balances and $369.1M represented pure Corporate Cash.

Working Capital Float Advantage: Merchant settlement balances generate an interest-free funding float. While these funds cannot be deployed into long-term illiquid assets, dLocal sweeps settlement cash into ultra-short-term yield-bearing liquid accounts in stable jurisdictions, generating non-operating interest income ($8.7M in FY 2025) that offsets local foreign exchange hedging costs.

Share Buyback Cannibalization: Repurchase Acceleration vs. SBC Dilution

In March 2026, dLocal's Board of Directors authorized a landmark $300.0M Class A share repurchase program alongside its ongoing policy of distributing 30% of prior-year free cash flow as dividends ($57.0M paid in 2026). During Q2 2026, management executed aggressively, repurchasing approximately 6.9M Class A shares for $86.1M at an average price of ~$12.48 per share. Crucially, all 6.9M repurchased shares were permanently canceled.

Cannibal Economics in Action: In a single quarter (Q2 2026), management retired ~2.3% of the company's total outstanding share count (~294.9M basic shares). Annual SBC issuance dilutes shareholders by approximately 1.5%–2.0% per annum (~4.5M to 5.5M shares). By deploying $86.1M in Q2 alone, dLocal completely wiped out two full years of SBC dilution, transforming from an equity diluter into a disciplined share cannibal.

With ~$213.9M remaining under the buyback authorization as of mid-2026, dLocal possesses sufficient corporate liquidity to repurchase an additional ~15.3M shares at current trading levels ($13.98), which would reduce the diluted share count below 275M shares and compound Owner Earnings per share by an additional 5.5%.

1. Structural Vulnerabilities & Operational Pre-Mortem

What specific operational missteps, competitive shifts, or macro shocks would render this thesis fundamentally wrong?

  • Uncompensated Take-Rate Collapse: The core bear argument centers on take-rate degradation. In Q2 2026, gross profit over TPV compressed to 0.72% (down from 1.07% in Q2 2025) as local-to-local (L2L) volumes reached 61% of total payment volume. If pricing tier compression on enterprise mega-merchants (e.g., Uber, Spotify, Shein) is not offset by operating leverage and higher-margin software services (such as Merchant of Record or BNPL expansion), gross profit dollars will stall despite booming volume.

Sudden devaluations or locked merchant funds force local-to-foreign currency losses, eroding operating margins.
  • Key Customer Churn & Concentration Risk: DLocal's Top 10 merchants account for 63% of TPV. The loss of two major enterprise global accounts to internal build-outs or competitors (such as Adyen or Stripe) would severely impair top-line gross profit generation.
  • Government-Sponsored Instant Payment Disintermediation: Acceleration of state-backed real-time payment networks (e.g., Pix in Brazil, UPI in India) could compress processing fees toward zero on local payment flows, destroying cross-border FX spread opportunities.
