### 1. Executive Primary Source Audit & Operational Shift A rigorous audit of Vistry Group’s latest Q2 2026 trading update, executive commentary, and corporate filings reveals an aggressive operational reset under new CEO Adam Daniels, accompanied by significant near-term friction: * **Financial Performance & Margin Compression:** Total H1 completions fell 11% YoY to ~6,100 units. Statutory profit before tax swung to a loss of ~$39M USD (£30M) due to a ~$65M USD (£50M) adverse cash-generation hit. To maintain sales velocity (open market sales rate of 1.03), Vistry increased private sales discounting to 7.1% (up from 1.4% in H1 2025). Full-year FY26 adjusted PBT guidance was revised downward from ~$290M USD (£223M) to ~$260M USD (£200M). * **Balance Sheet Stress & Daily Debt Spike:** Average daily net debt in H1 expanded sharply to ~$1.04B USD (£799M), driven by accelerated land creditor payments (reduced by >$195M USD / £150M in H1), faster supplier payables, and delayed partner-funded completions. Period-end net debt stood at ~$611M USD (£470M). However, management reiterated its target to exit FY26 in a net cash position exceeding $130M USD (>£100M), expecting average daily net debt to drop below $845M USD (£650M) in H2. * **Capital-Light Execution & Inventory Disposal:** Vistry has halved its unsold private work-in-progress (WIP) stock from ~$780M USD (£600M) at the start of the year to under $390M USD (£300M), with ~$247M USD (£190M) of cash conversion scheduled for H2. In July, the group completed a transaction to substantially exit its Part Exchange portfolio, permanently freeing ~$65M USD (£50M) in tied-up capital. * **Leadership Transition & Restructuring:** CFO Tim Lawlor resigned to accept a role in a private firm (remaining through October to assist with the H1 results). Simultaneously, CEO Adam Daniels implemented a voluntary redundancy scheme, generating ~$32.5M USD (£25M) in annualized overhead savings while executing a broader organizational review.
