What the Market is Pricing In

Skepticism surrounding Copart ($33.26) stems from a visible operational disconnect between contracting core U.S. insurance volumes and top-line growth sustained largely by elevated vehicle selling prices. Mr. Market is concerned that consolidated U.S. insurance auction volumes dropped 4.2% YoY in recent quarterly filings as auto insurance premium inflation forced cash-strapped motorists to drop collision coverage or raise deductibles. Skeptics argue that temporary Average Selling Price (ASP) tailwinds are masking underlying unit processing decay, leaving Copart exposed to significant margin compression once used car prices normalize.

Furthermore, investors fear competitive market share erosion and long-term technological disruption. Following RB Global’s acquisition and integration of Insurance Auto Auctions (IAA), major insurance carriers are actively allocating volumes away from Copart to avoid vendor lock-in, resulting in a positive volume differential for RB Global. Concurrently, the long-term adoption of Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) raises terminal concerns regarding accident frequency reductions. Consequently, the market prices Copart at 22.0x P/OE, treating current owner cash flows as cyclically peak earnings subject to structural headwinds.

Business Cyclicality & Cycle Stance
Howard Marks & Warren Buffett mental models for cycle positioning and earnings durability.
1. Cyclicality Profile (The Nature of the Business)
Secular Compounder
Mission-critical software, tolls, or subscriptions (e.g. Microsoft, Visa, Apple). Revenue and cash flows compound steadily through economic downturns.
Moderate Cyclical
Consumer retail, merchant payments, or advertising (e.g. StoneCo, Amazon, Lululemon). Earnings fluctuate with interest rates and consumer spending, but long-term secular growth continues.
Deep Cyclical
Commodities, homebuilders, freight shipping, or manufacturing (e.g. NVR, Vale, GigaCloud). Earnings swing between extreme peaks and valleys. Never extrapolate peak earnings.
2. Cycle Stance (Where the Business Sits Today)
Downcycle Contraction / Trough
Operating under high interest rates, credit contractions, or inventory destocking. Starting earnings are depressed, offering asymmetric upside when conditions normalize.
Mid-Cycle Run-Rate
Operating near sustainable historical margins and normal unit economics. Reliable baseline for 3-year compounding calculations.
Peak / Over-Earning Risk
Generating temporary peak margins due to shortages or bubble pricing. Downside risk if margins mean-revert to historical levels.
