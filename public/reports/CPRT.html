Section 1: Company Overview & Audited Financial Baseline

Copart, Inc. (NASDAQ: CPRT) is the world's dominant online vehicle auction and remarketing platform, operating a highly defensible, asset-heavy digital marketplace for salvage, total-loss, and clean-title vehicles. Founded in 1982, Copart acts as the primary intermediary connecting institutional vehicle consignors—principally automotive insurance carriers (representing 75%–80% of total volume)—with a global buyer network spanning dismantlers, rebuilders, used car dealers, exporters, and individual buyers across 185+ countries. The company operates a physical network of over 280 storage and processing facilities encompassing more than 10,000 acres of land across North America, the United Kingdom, Germany, Brazil, Spain, Ireland, Finland, and the Middle East.

Primary Economic Moat Rating: [Wide Moat]

Copart's wide economic moat is underpinned by a classic two-sided network effect paired with localized scale barriers and irreplaceable real estate infrastructure. In the salvage vehicle market, insurance carriers default to the auction venue that maximizes vehicle cash realization. Copart's proprietary Virtual Bidding Third Generation (VB3) platform aggregates over 1 million registered buyers globally, driving intense bidding competition that delivers industry-leading sale returns to insurers. In turn, buyers flock to Copart due to its unmatched inventory selection of over 260,000 vehicles listed on any given day. Crucially, this digital network effect is anchored by physical moat barriers: securing municipal zoning, environmental permits, and contiguous land parcels (50–100+ acres per yard) near major metropolitan areas presents an almost insurmountable barrier to potential entrants. Copart owns approximately 85% to 90% of its physical yard footprint outright, sheltering the operating model from industrial real estate lease inflation and creating an enduring cost advantage over capital-constrained competitors.

Buffett & Munger Pricing Power & Inflation Resistance Audit

Pricing Power Classification: [Absolute Pricing Power]

  • The 'Prayer Session' Test: Copart exhibits complete pricing authority over its marketplace fee schedules. Management regularly adjusts both seller administrative fees and buyer tiered fee structures without triggering customer churn or volume leakage. When used vehicle values or collision repair costs inflate, average selling prices (ASPs) rise naturally, automatically scaling Copart's percentage-based fee realization without requiring incremental operational effort.
  • Customer Share of Wallet & Value Surplus: For insurance carriers, salvage auction fees represent a friction charge compared to the total claim payout. Copart's platform reduces claim cycle times, eliminates storage liabilities, and maximizes salvage recovery value. For buyers, Copart provides essential raw material inventory for secondary repair and parts harvesting that cannot be sourced elsewhere at scale.

Predictability Classification: [High Predictability]

  • 10-Year Visibility Test: Structural secular trends strongly favor Copart over a 10-year horizon. Vehicle complexity—driven by advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS), sensor arrays, and electric vehicle (EV) battery packs—has elevated average repair costs relative to vehicle pre-accident value. Consequently, total loss frequency (the percentage of insured accident claims deemed economically unrepairable) has risen from ~15% historically to over 20% today, expanding Copart's addressable pool of salvage vehicles regardless of broader economic cycles.
  • Obsolescence & Reinvestment Drag: Unlike tech companies subject to rapid platform obsolescence, Copart's digital infrastructure (VB3) compounds in value alongside its underlying land bank. Reinvestment in yard acquisitions expands capacity permanently.
  • Recurring vs. Discretionary Demand: Auction volume is non-discretionary, tied directly to auto collision frequency, weather events (hurricanes, severe hail), and miles driven.

Insurance carriers respond by declaring a higher percentage of damaged vehicles total losses rather than attempting expensive, liability-laden repairs. Copart leverages its physical footprint of over 10,000 acres, steadily acquiring 500 to 700 acres of additional industrial land annually near major metropolitan markets to prevent capacity bottlenecks.
