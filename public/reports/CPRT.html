Section 1: The Premise of the Company

Copart, Inc. (CPRT) operates as the premier global online vehicle auction marketplace, connecting seller supply—primarily major auto insurance carriers, fleet operators, financial institutions, and rental companies—with worldwide buyer demand across 170+ countries. The operational engine rests on two interdependent vectors: an asset-heavy physical land footprint consisting of over 200 fee-simple salvage processing yards spanning more than 11,000 acres, and a proprietary virtual bidding platform (VB3). Municipal zoning restrictions, NIMBYism ("not in my backyard"), and stringent environmental regulatory frameworks make securing multi-acre industrial land rights near metropolitan hubs nearly impossible today, establishing an effective physical tollbridge against potential market entrants.

Monetization operates through a high-margin, transaction-based fee model. Service Revenues (representing ~85.5% of total sales) encompass tiered buyer fees scaled to vehicle hammer prices, seller assignment fees, transportation, title handling, and storage fees. Vehicle Sales (representing ~14.5% of sales) involve direct principal purchases and wholesale resales of salvage inventory, primarily in international markets. The enterprise is propelled by a structural tailwind: expanding Total Loss Frequency (TLF). As vehicles incorporate advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), complex unibody structures, and lithium-ion battery packs, collision repair costs reach Actual Cash Value (ACV) earlier in the asset lifecycle. U.S. Total Loss Frequency expanded from 22.6% in FY2025 to a record 23.6% in calendar Q1 2026, widening Copart's processing volume addressable market.

In recent quarters, raw U.S. insurance unit volumes experienced temporary retrenchment (-2% to -4% YoY) due to consumer insurance adjustments (higher deductibles and dropped comprehensive coverages following premium spikes) and comparisons against prior-year storm events. However, Copart's pricing power and international bidding density (where overseas buyers represent >35%–40% of winning bids) drove U.S. insurance Average Selling Prices (ASPs) to seasonal highs (+4.1% YoY), protecting enterprise revenue and expanding gross margins. Non-insurance volume—including Dealer Services, Blue Car Commercial, and Purple Wave heavy equipment—now accounts for over one-third of U.S. unit volume and nearly half of auction proceeds (with Purple Wave gross transaction value expanding over 25%). Management maintains capital allocation discipline: reinvesting cash into fee-simple real estate globally ($500M+ annual CapEx) while executing share repurchases ($1.6 Billion year-to-date in FY2026) backed by $5.5 Billion in total liquid cash and short-term Treasuries with zero funded bank debt.

Pricing Power & Predictability Audits

Pricing Power Audit: Copart exhibits structural pricing power. On the buyer side, non-linear fee schedules scale with auction hammer prices. Buyers absorb fee increases because Copart's liquidity pool maximizes inventory conversion rates and resale margins for recyclers and rebuilders. On the seller side, auto insurance carriers remain price-insensitive to service fees because Copart's global buyer network generates a 10%–20% higher net salvage recovery value per vehicle than regional physical auctions, directly lowering net claim payouts.

Predictability Audit: Operating cash flow predictability remains robust. Total loss volumes are governed by vehicle miles driven, weather patterns, traffic density, and repair technology complexity. In macroeconomic downturns, consumers retain older vehicles longer and insurers declare total losses at lower severity thresholds due to high used-car repair costs relative to vehicle values, stabilizing Copart's processing volumes.

Section 2: The Probable Future Paths

Based on statutory SEC disclosures, operational volume metrics, and management capital deployment execution, the following operational paths cover the probabilistic distribution of enterprise performance over the next 3–5 years:

Path 1: Core Compounder — TLF Expansion & Non-Insurance Scaling [70% Probability]

Near-term insurance unit volume normalizes following the post-FY2026 consumer coverage retrenchment. Total Loss Frequency continues its secular climb from 23.6% toward 25.0% over 4 years, driven by ADAS adoption, EV repair complexity, and structural labor/parts cost inflation. Total processed unit volume grows at a 4.5% annual rate, expanding from ~4.0M units to ~4.7M units by Year 4. International bidding participation (>35%–40% of bids) and non-insurance volume expansion (Purple Wave GTV growth and Dealer Services) drive Service Revenue per unit from ~$960 to $1,040 USD. Fixed-cost leverage across fee-simple real estate expands GAAP operating margin from 36.5% to 38.0%.

Capital Allocation & Share Accretion: Management deploys retained earnings into fee-simple real estate ($640M annual CapEx) while utilizing surplus liquidity to execute disciplined share repurchases. Reflecting the $1.6 Billion share repurchase executed year-to-date in FY2026 and modest ongoing repurchases, diluted share count contracts from 977.1M to 925.0M shares by Year 4.

Section 3: Normalized Owner Earnings Multiple & Yield Inversion Valuation

To value Copart, Inc. (CPRT) without market price anchoring, we deploy a 5-Year Owner Earnings Compounding Framework rooted in Ben Graham's margin of safety and Warren Buffett's 1986 owner earnings definition. Starting from audited FY 2025 normalized baseline Owner Earnings of $1,619.3 Million USD ($1.66 USD per share across 977.1 Million diluted shares) and crediting $4,314.1 Million USD in net surplus cash (+$4.42 USD per share), each operational path models bottom-up unit growth, fee monetization, margin dynamics, and capital deployment discipline through Year 5.

Terminal capitalization multiples are assigned strictly using economic yield logic: Terminal Multiple = (1 - g / ROIC) / (r - g), assuming a standard equity hurdle rate of 9.5% (r) and Copart's exceptional returns on invested capital (>25%–30%). For a wide-moat compounder protected by physical land monopolies and network effects, moderate 8.5% OE growth commands a 20.0x terminal multiple (5.0% owner cash yield), while accelerated 14.0% compounding commands a 23.0x multiple (4.35% yield).
