Section 1: The Premise of the Company

Core Business Model & Unit Monetization

Copart, Inc. (CPRT) is the undisputed global leader in online vehicle auctions and remarketing services. Operating a highly consolidated duopoly alongside its primary competitor, RB Global (formerly IAA), Copart acts as a critical clearinghouse for salvage, damaged, and clean-title vehicles. The operational engine is powered by its proprietary Virtual Bidding Third Generation (VB3) auction platform, which connects vehicle consignors to over one million registered members across more than 185 countries.

Copart’s unit monetization model is beautifully structured as a two-sided marketplace. The company charges fees to both the consignor (the seller) and the buyer (the member):

  • Seller-Side Fees: Copart charges insurance companies, dealers, fleet operators, and charities a combination of listing fees, title processing fees, and towing/transportation fees.
  • Buyer-Side Fees: Buyers pay tiered transaction fees based on the final vehicle sales price, along with gate fees, internet bidding fees, and storage fees if the vehicle is not picked up promptly.

The business is segmented into two primary revenue streams: Service Revenues and Vehicle Sales. Service Revenues represent the high-margin core auction fee business where Copart acts strictly as an agent. Vehicle Sales occur when Copart acts as a principal, purchasing salvage vehicles outright and reselling them for its own account. This principal model is primarily deployed in international markets (such as the UK and Germany) to seed marketplace liquidity and demonstrate the value of salvage auctions to local insurance carriers who are accustomed to traditional, fragmented salvage processes.

Economic Moat Classification: Wide Moat

Copart possesses an exceptionally durable Wide Moat anchored by three structural barriers:

  1. Multi-Sided Network Effects: Copart’s global buyer pool is the largest in the world. This density of demand ensures that insurance companies achieve the highest possible salvage recovery values (Average Selling Prices, or ASPs). Higher ASPs incentivize insurers to declare more vehicles a "total loss" rather than repairing them, which directs more volume to Copart, further expanding the buyer base.
  2. Physical Land Density & Zoning Barriers: Salvage vehicles are physical assets that require storage. Copart owns and operates over 250 facilities globally, encompassing thousands of acres of land. Acquiring large parcels of land near major metropolitan areas and securing the necessary environmental permits and municipal zoning licenses for salvage yards is an insurmountable barrier for new entrants.
  3. Deep Workflow Integration: Copart is deeply embedded in the operational workflows of major insurance carriers. From direct API integrations for salvage estimation to managing title processing and vehicle pickup logistics, Copart acts as an outsourced administrative arm for insurers, creating exceptionally high switching costs.

Governance, Executive Control & C-Suite Audit

Copart features a highly stable, founder-influenced governance structure. Founder Willis J. Johnson and his son-in-law, Jayson "Jay" Adair, maintain significant voting control over the enterprise.

C-Suite Audit & Leadership Transition (2026): In late June 2026, Copart announced a surprise leadership transition. CEO Jeff Liaw, who had successfully led the company through a decade of senior leadership, stepped down effective July 31, 2026. Executive Chairman Jay Adair reassumed the Chief Executive Officer role. Concurrently, Jane Pocock, who previously scaled Copart’s UK and Ireland operations to outstanding growth, was promoted to President of Copart, effective August 1, 2026. This transition pairs Adair’s strategic M&A mandate with Pocock’s proven international operational expertise.

Activist & Regulatory Audit: There are no active activist campaigns (such as Elliott or Starboard) targeting the company. However, Copart is currently managing a legal and regulatory overhang. The company has disclosed an ongoing U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) investigation into potential violations of money laundering laws related to its auction platform. Specifically, the probe focuses on Copart's practices for preventing and detecting money-laundering activity by auction platform members. The company has stated that the duration, scope, and potential financial impact of this investigation are not yet estimable.

4-Quarter Statutory Realities & Financial Audit

An audit of Copart's trailing four quarters reveals a transition from a high-growth post-pandemic cycle to a period of volume normalization. In Q3 FY2026 (ended April 30, 2026), Copart reported revenue of $1.24 billion, up a modest 2.1% year-over-year. This deceleration was driven by a 4% decline in U.S. insurance unit volumes. Rising auto insurance premiums have led some consumers to reduce coverage or drive less, resulting in softer claims activity and lower total loss volumes for insurers.

Despite softer volumes, Copart's pricing power remains exceptionally resilient. Global Average Selling Prices (ASPs) rose 4.6% globally in Q3 FY2026, with U.S. insurance ASPs hitting a seasonally adjusted all-time record high. This pricing power, combined with a 5.6% decline in vehicle sales cost of goods, allowed Copart to expand its global gross margin by 71 basis points to 46.3%. Operating income grew 2.8% to $464.3 million, maintaining a stellar operating margin of 37.5%.

Capital Allocation Shift: Historically, Copart has preferred to hoard cash or reinvest in land. However, in FY2026, management initiated a massive, rare share repurchase program. In the nine months ended April 30, 2026, Copart deployed $1.632 billion to buy back shares, including a major post-Q2 block purchase of 24.26 million shares at $37.11 per share. This aggressive buyback program reduced the diluted share count by 3.6% year-over-year, providing meaningful EPS accretion.

Fundamental Compounding Velocity Audit

Copart's compounding engine is driven by its high-margin service fee structure and asset-light virtual bidding platform. In FY2025, the company achieved a non-cash Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) of 30.8% (calculated as NOPAT of $1.39 billion over Net Invested Capital of $4.50 billion). This high return profile is supported by the fact that once a salvage yard is purchased and zoned, it requires very little incremental capital to support higher transaction volumes, allowing operating leverage to flow directly to the bottom line. The recent pivot to aggressive share repurchases in FY2026 represents a highly rational allocation of surplus cash, as buying back shares below intrinsic value compounds the long-term wealth of remaining owners.

Section 2: The Probable Future Paths

Based on the company's audited statutory filings, segment dynamics, 4-quarter earnings call commentary, and institutional counter-theses, here are the distinct, realistic operational paths covering 90%–95% of the fundamental probability space over the next 3–5 years:

Path 1: Cyclical Normalization and Global Network Expansion (Central Baseline)

Under this central path, Copart's dominant duopoly model continues to demonstrate its structural resilience. Near-term cyclical headwinds in U.S. insurance volumes—driven by temporary fluctuations in vehicle miles traveled and used car price normalization—gradually stabilize. International markets, particularly the UK and Germany, continue their transition to high-margin agency auctions, expanding the company's global buyer network. Operating margins remain highly stable around 36.5%, and the company's exceptional capital efficiency (ROIC of ~31%) allows it to fund organic yard expansions entirely through cash flow while opportunistically retiring shares. This path represents a steady-state continuation of Copart's compounding engine.

Path 2: The Friction of Maturity: Competitive Encroachment and Cost Inflation (Downside Friction)

In this downside scenario, Copart faces a more challenging operating environment. A revitalized RB Global (following its integration of IAA) competes aggressively on pricing and service levels, leading to fee compression and some market share loss in key metropolitan regions. Concurrently, persistent inflation in towing, yard labor, and land acquisition costs puts downward pressure on operating margins, which contract to 34.0%. Softening insurance volumes and a slower-than-expected adoption of the salvage auction model in continental Europe result in flat owner earnings growth, testing the limits of Copart's pricing power.

Path 3: The Global Network Flywheel: International Insurance Adoption and Clean-Title Dealer Expansion (Upside Acceleration)

This upside scenario envisions a powerful acceleration of Copart's global flywheel. Leveraging its proprietary VB3 online auction platform, Copart rapidly expands its international footprint, unlocking massive TAM in developing markets where insurance salvage adoption is in its infancy. In the domestic market, Copart successfully penetrates the clean-title dealer and non-insurance vehicle segments, driving a high-margin volume surge. Operating margins expand by 300 bps to 39.5% due to substantial operating leverage on its fixed yard infrastructure, while ROIC climbs to 34.5%, cementing Copart's status as an elite global compounder.

Capital Allocation Recommendation: We recommend a HOLD at the current price of $34.16. While Copart is an elite, wide-moat compounder with exceptional ROIC and a pristine balance sheet, the current valuation offers an insufficient Margin of Safety under our strict risk-adjusted calibration.
