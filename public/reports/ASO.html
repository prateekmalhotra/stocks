01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market is currently pricing Academy Sports and Outdoors at an austere 8.3x P/OE and an 8.8x EV/OE multiple (off normalized Owner Earnings of $6.13 per share), reflecting entrenched institutional skepticism regarding the durability of its post-pandemic cash flow baseline. Sell-side bears—led by cautious notes from Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan—argue that Academy’s core customer base is experiencing severe economic fatigue. In particular, negative store traffic persists among sub-$50,000 household income cohorts, where transaction counts dropped 5.3% in the latest quarter despite higher promotional cadence. Skeptics argue that top-line stability has relied disproportionately on ticket inflation (+4.9% AUR) and that recent gross margin performance is distorted by non-operating windfalls, most notably an $83.7 million IEEPA tariff refund.

Furthermore, Wall Street is treating Academy's square-footage expansion pipeline (targeting 125 to 160 new boxes over five years) as an operational liability rather than an accretive compounding engine. Bears model this expansion into an uncertain macro environment as a primary driver of fixed-cost deleveraging and inventory bloat, particularly as national competitors like Dick's Sporting Goods (House of Sport) push down-market and mass giants like Walmart dominate essential consumables. The current $51.15 market price assumes Academy is trapped as a cyclical regional discounter destined to suffer terminal comp declines and multi-year gross margin compression toward pre-2020 levels (low-30% range).

How Things Are Going Now

A sequential forensic review of the last 3–4 quarters (Q3 FY2025 through Q2 FY2026) demonstrates operational stabilization, disciplined inventory control, and resilient cash generation despite choppy discretionary spending. Over the past four quarters, net sales progressed from $1.50 billion in Q3 FY2025 to an annualized run-rate of $6,589.2 million in Q2 FY2026 ($1,647.3 million quarterly, +3.0% YoY). Year-to-date net sales expanded 4.7% to $3.09 billion, while comparable sales held at +1.1% YTD (-0.4% in Q2, where a +4.9% ticket size partially offset a -5.3% transaction decline). Omnichannel penetration accelerated sequentially, with e-commerce surging +12.8% YoY to reach 12.1% of total merchandise sales, bolstered by expedited regional delivery partnerships and mobile app adoption.

Underlying cash profitability has expanded significantly. Operating cash flow grew 47.9% year-to-date to $349.0 million, supporting over $270 million in adjusted free cash flow. While reported Q2 gross margin expanded to 40.4% due to an $83.7 million IEEPA tariff refund, management reinvested $10.9 million into strategic value pricing, yielding a net tariff benefit of 440 bps and lifting full-year normalized gross margin guidance to 34.5%–35.0%. The active store network reached 327 stores across 21 states spanning 22.19 million gross square feet (opening 3 stores in Q2, with 11 on deck for Q3). Capital allocation remains focused on per-share compounding: diluted share count shrank 4.4% YoY to 63.50 million shares via $182.1 million in YTD repurchases, while the balance sheet was derisked by refinancing funded debt into $500 million of 5.875% senior secured notes due 2031, leaving the $1.0 billion ABL credit facility completely undrawn.

Howard Marks & Warren Buffett mental models for cycle positioning and earnings durability.
1. Cyclicality Profile (The Nature of the Business)
Secular Compounder
Mission-critical software, tolls, or subscriptions (e.g. Microsoft, Visa, Apple). Revenue and cash flows compound steadily through economic downturns.
Moderate Cyclical
Consumer retail, merchant payments, or advertising (e.g. StoneCo, Amazon, Lululemon). Earnings fluctuate with interest rates and consumer spending, but long-term secular growth continues.
Deep Cyclical
Commodities, homebuilders, freight shipping, or manufacturing (e.g. NVR, Vale, GigaCloud). Earnings swing between extreme peaks and valleys. Never extrapolate peak earnings.
2. Cycle Stance (Where the Business Sits Today)
Downcycle Contraction / Trough
Operating under high interest rates, credit contractions, or inventory destocking. Starting earnings are depressed, offering asymmetric upside when conditions normalize.
Mid-Cycle Run-Rate
Operating near sustainable historical margins and normal unit economics. Reliable baseline for 3-year compounding calculations.
Peak / Over-Earning Risk
Generating temporary peak margins due to shortages or bubble pricing. Downside risk if margins mean-revert to historical levels.
