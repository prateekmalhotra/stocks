Section 1: Company Overview & Audited Financial Baseline

1. The Core Business Machine, Moat & Unit Economics

Yelp Inc. (NYSE: YELP) operates as a primary digital marketplace and local commerce engine connecting consumers with local service providers across North America and select international markets. Founded in 2004, the platform monetizes high-intent consumer commerce through performance-based Cost-Per-Click (CPC) local advertising, search placement, multi-location enterprise partner solutions, local service lead generation (e.g., "Request-a-Quote"), subscription software, and data licensing agreements.

Over the past several fiscal years, Yelp’s core monetization engine has undergone a deliberate structural pivot toward high-value project verticals. High-margin Home & Local Services now represents the foundation of the business machine, generating $948.0M in FY 2025 (64.71% of total net revenue). This transition reflects a strategic reallocation away from low-margin, highly churn-prone independent restaurants and brick-and-mortar retail toward service categories (Auto, Home, Health, Professional Services) characterized by higher average job values ($500–$5,000+) and resilient merchant ad budgets.

Primary Economic Moat Rating: Narrow Moat. Yelp’s network advantage stems from a two-sided local ecosystem anchored by over 330 million cumulative crowdsourced reviews and 28 million monthly active app unique devices. This proprietary repository creates a localized information network where 83% of active users complete a purchase from a business discovered on Yelp, with 57% initiating contact within 24 hours.

However, Yelp’s moat remains structurally bounded and is rated Narrow rather than Wide due to escalating top-of-funnel friction from Google AI Overviews and zero-click search features. Google’s aggressive insertion of generative search summaries and direct map widgets at the top of Search Engine Results Pages (SERPs) increasingly intercepts organic consumer traffic before users can navigate to Yelp. This top-of-funnel erosion creates conversion drag, requiring Yelp to increase product development spend and acquire specialized workflow tools to preserve user engagement.

Buffett & Munger Pricing Power & Inflation Resistance Audit

Pricing Power Classification: Constrained Pricing Power. Yelp operates an automated, auction-driven CPC ad pricing framework. While the company retains pricing strength in high-demand home services categories, overall pricing power is constrained by advertiser Return-on-Ad-Spend (ROAS) sensitivity among small-and-medium businesses (SMBs).

The "Prayer Session" Test: Yelp cannot unilaterally raise cost-per-click rates without incurring customer volume friction. Attempts to pass higher CPC prices onto lower-margin merchant categories (such as casual dining and local retail) trigger immediate advertiser pause rates and budget contractions. This operational constraint was highlighted in Q2 2026, where a 10.0% year-over-year drop in Restaurants, Retail & Other (RR&O) advertising revenue (to $102.0M), combined with a 5% YoY decline in total ad clicks, limited overall ad revenue expansion.

Inflation Capital Intensity: Under Warren Buffett's 1981 inflationary evaluation framework, Yelp demonstrates favorable capital-light characteristics. Buffett & Munger Cash Flow Predictability & "Too Hard" Pile Audit

Predictability Classification: Moderate Predictability.

The 10-Year Visibility Test: Yelp falls within an institutional circle of competence as an established local search utility and service enablement platform. However, 10-year cash flow predictability is classified as Moderate due to search engine algorithmic changes, the rapid adoption of AI-driven zero-click search tools, and ongoing advertiser acquisition friction.

Obsolescence & Reinvestment Drag: Yelp avoids physical capital degradation, with traditional CapEx representing 2.5%–3.5% of revenue. However, it faces an ongoing software R&D reinvestment mandate—allocating over $300M annually to product development to enhance AI matching tools (e.g., Yelp Assistant) and acquire client management technology (e.g., the $270M acquisition of Hatch in early 2026) to defend its ecosystem value.
