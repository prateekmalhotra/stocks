Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Yelp Inc. (NYSE: YELP) operates a leading local directory and high-intent local services marketplace, currently led by Co-Founder and active Chief Executive Officer Jeremy Stoppelman alongside Chief Financial Officer David Schwarzbach and Chief Operating Officer Jed Nachman. In its second quarter 2026 financial report (ended June 30, 2026), Yelp reported GAAP net revenue of $375.5 million, up 1.4% year-over-year. Top-line growth was sustained by non-advertising "Other Revenue"—which nearly doubled (+98% YoY to $33.0 million) due to the acquisition of AI lead management platform Hatch, rapid adoption of the Yelp Host call-answering SaaS product, and multi-year data licensing partnerships with OpenAI and enterprise AI developers. However, core advertising revenue contracted 3% YoY to $342.5 million as persistent macroeconomic pressure squeezed small and medium-sized business (SMB) promotional budgets, particularly within Restaurants, Retail & Other (RR&O) categories (-10% YoY).

Operating profitability faced near-term margin compression during Q2 2026. GAAP Operating Income (EBIT) stood at $43.7 million, representing an 11.6% operating margin compared to 14.4% in the prior-year period (-280 bps YoY). This contraction stemmed from increased website infrastructure overhead required to power AI tools, higher capitalized software depreciation, and expanded sales headcount for Hatch. GAAP Net Income fell 28% YoY to $31.7 million ($0.57 diluted EPS), while Adjusted EBITDA came in at $91.4 million (24.3% margin). Operating cash flow remained resilient at $75.8 million, yielding $61.1 million in Free Cash Flow. Management narrowed full-year 2026 guidance, projecting net revenue between $1.460 billion and $1.470 billion and Adjusted EBITDA between $315 million and $325 million. Importantly, Yelp has temporarily paused its share repurchase program to prioritize paying down $100.0 million drawn on its revolving credit facility to fund the $271.2 million cash purchase of Hatch, planning to resume repurchases in 2027.

Section 2: Business Model Reality, Unit Economics & Competitive Moat

Yelp monetizes commercial intent at the point of local discovery. The business model has strategically shifted away from low-margin restaurant and retail ad placements toward high-value Home & Local Services (plumbing, HVAC, legal, roofing, auto care). Advertisers purchase cost-per-click (CPC) ads, sponsored search placements, and project lead generation products through "Request-a-Quote" workflows. Additionally, Yelp generates recurring SaaS subscription fees from business tools (Yelp Host AI phone reservation and call handling, Hatch AI lead conversion) and collects multi-year licensing fees by providing clean, human-generated review data to AI models like OpenAI's ChatGPT.

Structural Secular Tailwinds vs. Competitive Disruption Risks:

  • Secular Tailwinds: The rapid digitization of home services provides a structural expansion runway for Yelp’s highest-margin category (Services represent 64% of ad revenue). Furthermore, generative AI platforms require reliable, real-time local business metadata and authentic human opinions. Yelp’s multi-year licensing deals (e.g., OpenAI partnership) monetize its 330M+ review database as high-margin data licensing float. Proprietary AI tools like Yelp Assistant (+10% project submission lift) and Yelp Host (2.4M automated call run-rate) enhance merchant productivity and create sticky recurring SaaS revenue.
  • Competitive Disruption & Substitution Threats: Google Search remains Yelp's dominant top-of-funnel discovery bottleneck. The rollout of Google AI Overviews (SGE) risks cannibalizing organic referral traffic by answering user local queries directly on Google search results pages without directing users to external sites. Furthermore, SMB ad budgets are highly sensitive to macro slowdowns, as evidenced by the 10% YoY revenue decline in RR&O categories during Q2 2026.

Section 3: Forensic Cash Flow, SBC Dilution & Owner Earnings Audit

A rigorous audit of Yelp Inc. (YELP) requires looking past management's preferred Non-GAAP metric—Adjusted EBITDA ($369.2 million in FY 2025)—which routinely adds back real economic operating expenses, most notably Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) and property/software maintenance capital expenditures. Treating stock-based compensation as a non-cash item creates a massive distortion in reported profitability: while SBC does not require immediate cash disbursement, it represents direct economic equity dilution that transfers enterprise value from public shareholders to employees.

Forensic Cash Flow Insight: Management promoted an Adjusted EBITDA of $369.2M in FY 2025 and guided to $315M–$325M for FY 2026. However, after deducting 100% of SBC ($131.2M in FY 2025) and required Maintenance CapEx ($48.4M), true Buffett Owner Earnings were $192.4M in FY 2025 and $184.5M on a TTM run-rate basis. While Yelp has structurally reduced SBC as a percentage of revenue from ~12% in prior years down to 8%–9% by late 2025, SBC remains an unavoidable annual equity burden of $108M–$113M that must be deducted from operating cash flows.
