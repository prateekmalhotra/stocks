01

What the Market is Pricing In

At today's market price of $20.61, KeyCorp trades at 20.6x normalized Owner Earnings ($1.00 per share) and 29.3x Enterprise Value to Owner Earnings, reflecting an aggressive market expectation of an unencumbered balance sheet repricing cycle. Mr. Market is pricing in a frictionless exit toward a 3.00% to 3.05% Net Interest Margin (NIM) by late 2026, scaling to over 3.25% NIM and a 15%+ Return on Tangible Common Equity (ROTCE) by 2027. Under this optimistic narrative, more than $30 billion of deeply underwater, fixed-rate legacy swaps, residential mortgages, and low-yielding Treasury portfolios are assumed to roll off seamlessly into higher market yields, delivering substantial net interest income expansion without encountering deposit competition or credit friction.

However, this optimistic pricing creates a dangerous asymmetry. The market is capitalizing KeyCorp as if its cyclical earnings recovery is guaranteed, while entirely ignoring that its underlying Owner Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) sits at an audited 3.7%, its balance sheet carries $9,256 million in net debt ($-8.63 per share in liquid cash relative to borrowings), and its core deposit base ($147.6 billion) has remained essentially stagnant. Sell-side consensus overlooks that KeyCorp's historical NIM from 2010 to 2019 averaged just 3.18% even with non-interest-bearing deposits comprising 29%–37% of the base—a liability profile far superior to today's rate-sensitive funding mix. Far from pricing genuine structural growth or an unassailable economic moat, the market is pricing a high-multiple cyclical hope on a commoditized regional banking franchise facing significant funding timing mismatches and nascent asset-quality degradation.

How Things Are Going Now

Examined across the last four reporting periods (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), KeyCorp's operational reality reflects an institution recovering from its 2023–2024 balance-sheet repositioning, but encountering mounting operational friction. Latest quarter revenue of $1,964.0 million generates an annualized run-rate of $7,856.0 million (+6.7% YoY). Period-end portfolio loans stand at $110.3 billion ($110.1 billion average loans), while average deposits remain flat at $147.6 billion. The franchise achieved modest sequential net interest margin expansion to 2.89% (up from 2.75% in Q3 2025), driven by the scheduled roll-off of underwater fixed-rate swaps and securities into higher reinvestment yields.

However, sequential operational momentum exhibits clear bifurcation. Balance sheet expansion was driven exclusively by corporate C&I loans (+5.0% QoQ), while consumer portfolios contracted 1.9% QoQ. Because commercial loan demand coincided with a seasonal deposit trough, the bank was forced into high-cost wholesale funding rather than low-cost core deposits, muting NIM expansion to just +2 basis points QoQ. Meanwhile, fee momentum slowed as investment banking fees dropped from $243 million in Q4 2025 to $169 million in Q2 2026, prompting management to acquire UK-based Clearwater Corporate Finance LLP to supplement domestic advisory volume.

Capital management remains aggressive despite mixed credit signals. Net charge-offs rose to 42 bps ($115 million), and nonperforming assets climbed to 74 bps, yet the bank lowered its quarterly provision to $92 million and enacted a $23 million reserve release, lowering allowance coverage to 1.56% of loans. KeyCorp repurchased $341 million in common stock at an average price of $21.95 in Q2 2026, drawing stated CET1 down to 11.2% and leaving Marked CET1 (inclusive of AOCI) at 9.8%. The franchise generated GAAP net income to common of $472 million in Q2 2026 (a 12.9% ROTCE), but true distributable cash flow is constrained by regulatory capital requirements and sticky operating expenses ($1,217 million).
