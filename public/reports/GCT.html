Section 1: Business Model & Moat Reality

GigaCloud Technology Inc. (NASDAQ: GCT) operates a specialized B2B technology platform designed to resolve the fundamental structural inefficiencies of cross-border trade in large-parcel merchandise. While traditional consumer e-commerce platforms (e.g., Amazon, Shopify, Temu) are optimized for small, lightweight, high-density items that fit seamlessly into standard parcel logistics, large-parcel merchandise—such as residential furniture, major home appliances, outdoor equipment, and fitness machinery—faces prohibitive shipping costs, high damage rates, and complex inventory drag. GigaCloud addresses these friction points through a proprietary end-to-end framework that unifies product discovery, cross-border payments, ocean transport, warehousing, and last-mile delivery into a single fixed-price transactional execution engine.

1. Core Economic Engine: Supplier Fulfilled Retailing (SFR) Architecture

At the core of GigaCloud's competitive differentiation is its proprietary Supplier Fulfilled Retailing (SFR) model. Traditional wholesale supply chains require multiple intermediaries: Asian manufacturers sell to importers/wholesalers, who sell to regional distributors, who in turn sell to brick-and-mortar or online retailers, with each layer stacking gross margin markups and accumulating inventory risks. Under the SFR model, GigaCloud bridges primary manufacturers (primarily based in Asia) directly with downstream B2B buyers and resellers across North America and Europe.

The SFR paradigm alters the capital intensity and inventory risk profile of large-parcel commerce through two distinct, complementary channels:

  • 3P (Third-Party) Marketplace Platform: High-margin, asset-light transactions where independent suppliers list inventory directly on the GigaCloud Marketplace. Sellers retain title to the inventory while leveraging GigaCloud's global warehouse network and logistics contracts. GigaCloud earns transactional commission fees, warehouse storage charges, last-mile fulfillment fees, and ocean freight shipping fees. 3P seller GMV grew 27.0% YoY to $962.3M for the 12 months ended June 30, 2026, representing over 55% of total marketplace GMV.
  • 1P (First-Party) Inventory Market Making: GigaCloud directly purchases inventory from manufacturers to sell under its own product programs across major third-party off-platform channels (such as Wayfair, Amazon, Walmart, and Target) and directly within its B2B marketplace. Crucially, 1P is not designed as a standalone retail strategy, but as an operational catalyst: it provides baseline volume density for GigaCloud’s logistics network, validates new SKU demand, seeds new geographic markets (e.g., Europe), and establishes reliable pricing benchmarks that attract third-party sellers to the ecosystem.

2. Network Effects, SKU Density & Logistics Infrastructure

GigaCloud's moat is reinforced by localized logistics scale and supply-side network effects. As of Q2 2026, the company operates 38 leased fulfillment centers encompassing approximately 12.4 million square feet across North America, Europe, and Asia. By aggregating ocean shipping volume across thousands of small-to-medium merchants, GigaCloud secures volume-discounted long-term freight rates with ocean carriers and optimizes warehouse rack utilization for bulky items.
