01

What the Market is Pricing In

At today's current market price of $264.36, Mr. Market is pricing Texas Instruments at an aggressive 40.9x Normalized Owner Earnings (P/OE) and 42.1x EV/OE. This premium valuation reflects an expectation that the semiconductor industry's broader cyclical downturn has fully bottomed, and that Texas Instruments is embarking on an unprecedented multi-year cash flow expansion as its massive 300mm wafer fab construction phase transitions into commercial production.

However, sell-side bears—highlighted by Goldman Sachs double-downgrading TXN to Sell with a $156 price target and Morgan Stanley maintaining an Underweight stance—emphasize a profound structural disconnect. Investors paying nearly 41x owner cash yield (a thin 2.4% yield) are effectively treating peak structural CapEx cycle recovery as guaranteed smooth compounding, while completely ignoring the permanent non-cash depreciation overhang ($2.3B–$2.7B annually) from new fabs, domestic mature-node price erosion in mainland China from domestic suppliers like SG Micro, and financial integration risk from the $7.5B all-cash Silicon Labs acquisition.

How Things Are Going Now

Texas Instruments is demonstrating a robust operational recovery in its real-time statutory metrics. Latest quarter (Q2) revenue reached $5,463.0M (+23% YoY and +13% sequentially), establishing an annualized revenue run-rate of $21,852.0M. Gross margins rebounded to 61.4%, supported by expanding factory utilization across its internal 300mm manufacturing footprint. Operating cash flow over the trailing twelve months reached $8.7B, supporting $3.3B in CapEx and delivering $6.5B in free cash flow. Management guided Q3 revenue upward to $5.65B–$6.15B.

At the operational product level, demand is driven by over 80,000 catalog analog and embedded processing SKUs supplied to approximately 100,000 global customers. In the latest quarter, performance was anchored by the core Analog segment at $4,365M in revenue (+26% YoY), alongside Embedded Processing at $788M (+16% YoY). Broad end-market momentum was led by industrial (up ~30% YoY), data center (doubling YoY), and automotive (up mid-teens YoY).

Strategic execution centers on expanding internal 300mm wafer fab production (Sherman, TX; Lehi, UT; Richardson, TX) to capture a ~40% cost reduction per unpackaged die versus 200mm wafers. Concurrently, TI announced the $7.5B all-cash acquisition of Silicon Labs ($231/sh) to cement its leadership in embedded wireless connectivity and edge AI, while maintaining disciplined capital allocation under incoming CFO Julie Knecht following Rafael Lizardi's retirement.

Secular Compounder
Mission-critical software, tolls, or subscriptions (e.g. Microsoft, Visa, Apple). Revenue and cash flows compound steadily through economic downturns.
Moderate Cyclical
Consumer retail, merchant payments, or advertising (e.g. StoneCo, Amazon, Lululemon). Earnings fluctuate with interest rates and consumer spending, but long-term secular growth continues.
Deep Cyclical
Commodities, homebuilders, freight shipping, or manufacturing (e.g. NVR, Vale, GigaCloud). Earnings swing between extreme peaks and valleys. Never extrapolate peak earnings.
