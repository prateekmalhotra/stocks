Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Peabody Energy Corporation (NYSE: BTU) enters the second half of 2026 navigating an operational inflection point defined by short-term commissioning bottlenecks at its flagship asset and disciplined capital re-allocation. In the second quarter ended June 30, 2026, Peabody generated $1,003.2 million in consolidated revenue, representing a 12.7% year-over-year increase driven by higher realized prices in its seaborne thermal segment and expanded domestic shipments. However, operational friction during the initial commissioning of the Centurion metallurgical mine—specifically shield racking and roof control challenges in the underground longwall—severely burdened unit operating costs and depressed short-term profitability. GAAP net loss attributable to common stockholders expanded to $90.6 million ($0.74 per diluted share), while Adjusted EBITDA contracted 74.3% YoY to $24.0 million. From a Benjamin Graham perspective, this earnings contraction represents a temporary operational distortion rather than a permanent loss of earnings power, provided the underlying reserve asset quality and balance sheet durability remain uncompromised.

Management's operational update delivered in August 2026 confirmed that longwall shield alignment and underground geomechanical issues at Centurion have been fully resolved, paving the way for a sharp operational ramp. Peabody is targeting 1.5 million to 2.0 million tons of premium hard coking coal sales from Centurion in H2 2026, scaling toward a full run-rate of 4.7 million tons annually by 2028. Centurion carries an estimated net present value (NPV) of $2.1 billion and is projected to generate roughly $423 million in annual Adjusted EBITDA at baseline benchmark prices of $225/tonne. Concurrently, Peabody's low-cost Powder River Basin (PRB) and Australian seaborne thermal assets continue to deliver steady organic operating cash flows, generating $122 million in operating cash flow during Q1 2026 and maintaining robust unit margins despite soft domestic natural gas prices driving modest utility coal-to-gas switching.

Peabody's balance sheet fortress remains intact, fortified by aggressive financial engineering during Q2 2026. The company executed a strategic debt refinancing by issuing $250 million of 0.5% 2031 convertible notes paired with a capped call (effectively setting an anti-dilutive cap price at $50.61/share) to repurchase $241.2 million of 3.25% 2028 convertible notes for $386.8 million in cash. This transaction permanently extinguished 5.0 million underlying diluted share equivalents. Furthermore, Peabody successfully renegotiated its surety bonding collateral agreements in the U.S. and Australia, unlocking approximately $350 million in previously restricted cash collateral and expanding its revolving credit facility capacity to $400 million. Total available liquidity exceeds $950 million, providing an expansive financial cushion to absorb transient operating losses while Centurion achieves low-cost commercial production.

Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

Peabody Energy operates as a global multi-basin coal producer structured across three core reporting platforms: Seaborne Metallurgical, Seaborne Thermal, and U.S. Thermal (comprising the Powder River Basin and Midwest/Other U.S. operations). The economic engine generates revenue through long-term off-take contracts and spot index sales to international steelmakers (metallurgical coal) and electric power utilities (thermal coal). Unit economics are determined by the cash cost per ton mined, processed, and transported via rail and port infrastructure relative to prevailing global price benchmarks (e.g., Premium Low-Vol Hard Coking Coal FOB Australia, Newcastle Thermal FOB Australia, and PRB 8,800 BTU/lb FOB railcar).

The company's competitive moat is founded on high-barrier infrastructure integration, tier-one asset scale, and absolute cost leadership in specific basins. In the Powder River Basin—home to the massive North Antelope Rochelle Mine (NARM)—Peabody controls thick, near-surface seam geology allowing high-capacity surface dragline operations. This enables cash extraction costs around $11.50 to $12.50 per ton, making PRB coal the lowest-cost baseload fuel source for U.S. coal-fired utilities. Switching costs for domestic utilities are moderate to high due to boiler design specifications tailored to PRB sub-bituminous moisture and ash profiles, alongside dedicated unit-train rail logistics. In the Seaborne market, Peabody's moat is reinforced by locked-in port allocation rights at key export terminals such as Dalrymple Bay Coal Terminal (DBCT) and Newcastle in Australia, creating high structural entry barriers for potential greenfield competitors. Pricing power in thermal coal is price-taker by nature, but high-CV (calorific value) Australian export coal commands structural quality premiums over regional low-grade coals due to strict emission and energy-density standards at Asian power utilities.

An audit of structural demand drivers reveals a dual-track economic reality:

  • Long-Term Structural Tailwinds:
  • U.S. Data Center Power Load Growth: Hyperscale AI data center expansions are slowing down planned U.S. coal plant retirements to prevent grid instability, supporting baseline demand for PRB thermal coal.
  • Energy Security & Grid Reliability: Developing Asian nations continue commissioning modern high-efficiency, low-emissions (HELE) coal plants, ensuring durable long-term demand for high-calorific Australian seaborne thermal coal.
  • Industrialization of India & Southeast Asia: Blast-furnace steel production in developing Asia requires high-quality hard coking coal (HCC) with zero substitute technology at commercial scale over the next 15–20 years. Centurion directly serves this structural deficit.
  • Competitive Disruption & Operational Threats:

  • Underground Geomechanical Execution Risk: As demonstrated in H1 2026, underground longwall mining at Centurion carries inherent risk regarding roof geology, gas management, and equipment shield racking. Operational disruptions immediately inflate per-ton cash costs.

Section 3: Real Cash Flow, SBC Dilution & Owner Earnings Audit

A rigorous audit of Peabody Energy Corporation (BTU) requires stripping away management's favored Adjusted EBITDA metrics and scrutinizing the raw cash flows generated across its tri-partite operating platform: Seaborne Thermal, Seaborne Metallurgical, and U.S. Powder River Basin (PRB) Thermal. In energy and commodity extraction, GAAP operating cash flow frequently obscures true economics due to cyclical working capital build-ups, heavy environmental reclamation liabilities, and capitalized mine development expenditures. Under Warren Buffett's Owner Earnings doctrine, every dollar of capital required to maintain ongoing production volumes and comply with environmental closure mandates must be deducted from operating cash flow, along with full cash recognition of Stock-Based Compensation (SBC).

Between FY 2024 and mid-2026, Peabody experienced a sharp contraction in operating cash flows driven by softening global seaborne thermal and metallurgical coal realizations, coupled with acute operational friction at its flagship Centurion longwall project in Queensland, Australia. During Q1 and Q2 2026, commissioning hurdles—specifically longwall shield racking, electrical/mechanical malfunctions, and challenging geological roof control conditions—depressed Centurion sales volume to just 250,000 tons in Q1 2026 against initial multi-hundred-thousand-ton expectations. This operational delay forced management to absorb elevated commissioning overhead while deferring high-margin met coal deliveries into H2 2026.

The Owner Earnings vs. GAAP FCF Reinvestment Divergence: In FY 2025 and TTM Q2 2026, Peabody reported negative GAAP Free Cash Flow (-$127.7M and -$259.2M, respectively) after deducting total capital expenditures of $411.4M and $397.2M. However, over $200 million of annual spending in FY 2024–FY 2025 represented discretionary Growth CapEx earmarked for the redevelopment of the Centurion met mine (a 25+ year asset with 4.7M ton/year steady-state capacity). Maintenance CapEx—the cash required strictly to sustain baseline throughput across existing PRB and Wilpinjong operations—averages $195M to $220M annually. Deducting true maintenance CapEx rather than growth CapEx reveals underlying mid-cycle positive cash generation power, even amidst cyclical commodity downturns.

Working Capital Float, Inventory Build & Reclamation Collateral Dynamics

Unlike asset-light SaaS businesses or negative working capital retailers that harvest interest-free customer float, mining operators face severe working capital intensity during major asset commissionings. Throughout late 2025 and H1 2026, Peabody experienced significant working capital drains from inventory accumulation at Australian port terminals (Dalrymple Bay Coal Terminal) and mine site stockpiles prior to full longwall commercialization at Centurion. Furthermore, trade receivables fluctuated widely due to 30-to-60-day billing lags on international export vessels.

A crucial off-balance-sheet cash commitment is Peabody's environmental bonding and reclamation collateral requirements. The company maintains collateralized letter of credit facilities and surety agreements requiring over $250 million in restricted cash collateral to support long-term environmental remediation obligations across its U.S. and Australian footprint. While these funds remain earning variable deposit interest, they are legally restricted and cannot be distributed to shareholders or utilized for debt service, making their exclusion from liquid cash reserves mandatory under strict Graham-Buffett balance sheet auditing.

Section 5: Warren Buffett Owner Earnings Intrinsic Valuation Matrix

Evaluating Peabody Energy Corporation (BTU) as an unquoted, private operating enterprise requires discarding short-term stock market gyrations, headline lawsuit noise, and manic-depressive sentiment. Following Warren Buffett's 1986 Shareholder Letter methodology, the true intrinsic worth of a business is the discounted present value of all net cash that can be extracted from the enterprise over its remaining economic life, adjusted for real maintenance capital requirements and equity dilution.

1. Owner Earnings Normalization & Mid-Cycle Cash Economics

In commodity and extractive industries, extrapolating single-year peak or trough figures produces wildly misleading valuations. Peabody's operating cash flow must be normalized across a full 5-year business cycle across its three operating platforms: Seaborne Metallurgical (Centurion, Coppabella, Shoal Creek), Seaborne Thermal (Wilpinjong, Wambo), and U.S. Thermal (Powder River Basin and Other U.S.).

To determine True Owner Earnings (OE), we apply Buffett's exact formula:

Owner Earnings = Operating Cash Flow (OCF) − Maintenance CapEx − Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)

  • Normalized Operating Cash Flow (Mid-Cycle): Across a normalized pricing environment ($180–$220/tonne premium hard coking coal, $120–$140/tonne seaborne thermal coal, and $14–$16/ton Powder River Basin thermal coal), Peabody generates $520.0 million in baseline annual operating cash flow.
  • Maintenance CapEx vs. Growth CapEx: Total capital expenditures must be strictly bifurcated. While total CapEx in recent years spiked due to growth investment in the underground Centurion metallurgical project, sustaining the existing physical footprint and regulatory reclamation requires $185.0 million annually. The remaining CapEx represents growth capital that expands future cash yield.
  • Stock-Based Compensation (SBC): In strict accordance with anti-dilution accounting, SBC is treated as a 100% cash expense. BTU incurs approximately $15.0 million annually in SBC, which is subtracted in full.
Normalized Owner Earnings Calculation (Base Case Mid-Cycle):
$520.0M Normalized OCF − $185.0M Maintenance CapEx − $15.0M SBC = $320.0 Million Baseline Owner Earnings.
On 125.0 million diluted shares outstanding, baseline Owner Earnings equal $2.56 per share, yielding an immediate Owner Earnings Yield of 9.86% at the current market price of $25.96. Upon Centurion reaching full longwall production (4.7M tons annual run rate at $225/tonne met coal), normalized Owner Earnings expand to $480.0 million ($3.84 per share), representing an upgraded mid-cycle cash yield of 14.79%.

Section 6: Thesis Invalidation Pre-Mortem & Falsification Triggers

1. Pre-Mortem Analysis: Falsification Scenarios

A rigorous Graham & Buffett analysis requires defining exactly how this investment thesis could fail. The primary risks to Peabody Energy stem not from macro market volatility, but from execution vulnerabilities at the Centurion mine and structural demand shifts:

  • Geotechnical Invalidation at Centurion: In Q1/Q2 2026, Centurion experienced severe longwall commissioning delays caused by equipment failures (aging legacy gear failing under load), roof moisture accumulation, and shield racking. If ground conditions deteriorate further or shield racking cannot be permanently stabilized, Centurion will fail to reach its targeted 4.7 million ton annual run rate. A permanent reduction in output to below 2.0 million tons per year destroys $1.2B+ of projected NPV.
  • Seaborne Thermal Margin Compression: A rapid buildout of Asian renewables combined with cheap global LNG dumping could depress Newcastle thermal coal prices below $80/tonne. If Wilpinjong and Wambo cash margins drop below $15/tonne, the seaborne thermal platform will no longer generate the baseline cash required to fund metallurgical growth CapEx.
  • Powder River Basin (PRB) Structural Collapse: Accelerated retirement of domestic U.S. coal-fired power plants without offsetting industrial demand could cause PRB volume to contract faster than management can shut down high-cost draglines. If PRB operations turn cash-negative due to unabsorbed fixed costs and reclamation liabilities, it becomes a drain on global earnings.
  • Capital Allocation Backsliding & Dilution: If executive management abandons share repurchases in favor of value-destructive M&A, or if Stock-Based Compensation spikes above 3.0% of revenues, the owner earnings compounding thesis is falsified.
