01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market is currently caught in an acute perceptual tension, pricing Alibaba at $120.42 per share—which commands an elevated headline multiple of 59.3x normalized Owner Earnings ($2.03/sh) and 53.2x EV/OE. The market is attempting to price Alibaba not on its current depressed cash earnings, but as a dominant hyperscale artificial intelligence monopoly that will successfully transform massive upfront capital expenditures into high-margin software revenues. Optimists are betting that current cash flow contraction is merely a transitory trough caused by an aggressive three-year, RMB 380B+ ($56.4B) AI infrastructure deployment that will eventually yield compounding returns.

However, this optimistic pricing structure ignores the severe operational deleverage occurring within Alibaba's core cash-generating engine. In domestic e-commerce, Taobao and Tmall are experiencing relentless market share erosion against PDD and ByteDance/Douyin, forcing Alibaba into margin-diluting merchant subsidies and contra-revenue concessions. With consolidated free cash flow flipping deeply negative (-$6.58B in the June 2026 quarter) and share repurchases abruptly curtailed ($162M spent) while raising $10.2B in a discounted share placement that diluted existing holders by 3.6% to fund data center buildouts, the market is dangerously mispricing an asset with a 3.6% ROIC as if it possessed an unassailable wide moat, leaving no margin of safety against competitive commoditization.

How Things Are Going Now

Operationally, Alibaba presents two sharply diverging trajectories across its business units in the latest consecutive quarterly releases (Q2–Q4 FY2026 through Q1 FY2027 / June 2026 quarter). On one hand, AI Cloud and Compute Services are demonstrating robust top-line momentum. External Cloud revenue growth accelerated to +45% YoY to RMB 48.44B ($7.14B) in the June 2026 quarter, driven by 12 consecutive quarters of triple-digit growth in AI-related products, which now operate at an annualized run-rate exceeding RMB 49.5B ($7.3B). Cloud EBITA margins expanded to 12.0%, supported by active enterprise compute instances and proprietary T-Head processor deployments.

On the other hand, the domestic commerce engine is bearing severe financial strain to defend transaction density across 1.0B active consumers and ~10M active merchants. Core Customer Management Revenue contracted 7% YoY (or +1% on a like-for-like basis) as Alibaba subsidized merchants and funded new business development programs to combat Douyin and PDD. Quick Commerce (Taobao Instant Commerce & Freshippo) surged +45% YoY to RMB 53.30B, but promotional campaigns and logistics subsidies continue to dilute consolidated profitability, holding gross margins at 34.0%.

Capital allocation has shifted decisively toward capital expenditures and liquidity preservation. Driven by server procurement and hyperscale data center construction, quarterly CapEx surged 75% YoY to RMB 67.68B ($9.98B), driving quarterly consolidated Free Cash Flow into a negative RMB 44.67B (-$6.58B) outflow. Management effectively paused open-market share repurchases ($162M spent in Q1 FY27 vs. $4.1B quarterly historically) and issued an HK$80B ($10.2B) ordinary share placement at HK$112.70 per share (~3.6% dilution) to fund its 3-year RMB 380B AI buildout program.

Business Cyclicality & Cycle Stance
Howard Marks & Warren Buffett mental models for cycle positioning and earnings durability.
1. Cyclicality Profile (The Nature of the Business)
Secular Compounder
Mission-critical software, tolls, or subscriptions (e.g. Microsoft, Visa, Apple). Revenue and cash flows compound steadily through economic downturns.
Moderate Cyclical
Consumer retail, merchant payments, or advertising (e.g. StoneCo, Amazon, Lululemon). Earnings fluctuate with interest rates and consumer spending, but long-term secular growth continues.
Deep Cyclical
Commodities, homebuilders, freight shipping, or manufacturing (e.g. NVR, Vale, GigaCloud). Earnings swing between extreme peaks and valleys. Never extrapolate peak earnings.
2. Cycle Stance (Where the Business Sits Today)
Downcycle Contraction / Trough
Operating under high interest rates, credit contractions, or inventory destocking. Starting earnings are depressed, offering asymmetric upside when conditions normalize.
Mid-Cycle Run-Rate
Operating near sustainable historical margins and normal unit economics. Reliable baseline for 3-year compounding calculations.
Peak / Over-Earning Risk
Generating temporary peak margins due to shortages or bubble pricing. Downside risk if margins mean-revert to historical levels.
