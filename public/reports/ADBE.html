01

What the Market is Pricing In

At today's market price of $248.83 per share, Adobe Inc. (ADBE) is trading at a historic valuation multiple compression of just 11.8x normalized Owner Earnings and 11.7x Enterprise Value to Owner Earnings. Mr. Market is pricing in an aggressive structural disruption narrative, capitalizing Adobe as if its $8,575.0M in starting normalized Owner Earnings ($21.17 per share) represents a peak cyclical level facing permanent decline. The consensus skeptic case—amplified by Morgan Stanley's recent downgrade to Underweight with a $240 price target—contends that Adobe is entangled in three concurrent, high-risk strategic transitions.

First, Wall Street bears argue that standalone generative AI models (OpenAI Sora, Midjourney, FLUX) and flexible creative platforms (Canva, Figma) are disintermediating Photoshop and Illustrator, lowering the skill threshold for design and risking creative seat contraction during enterprise renewal cycles. Second, the market is rattled by governance uncertainty following C-suite turnover, with long-time CEO Shantanu Narayen transitioning to Executive Chair while Anil Chakravarthy steps in as CEO, coming shortly after CFO Dan Durn's exit. Third, analysts worry that Adobe's aggressive pivot to a freemium route-to-market—despite attracting 90M+ Monthly Active Users (MAUs)—creates heavy near-term friction in converting free users into high-margin paid subscriptions while cloud compute expenses for Firefly GenAI inference erode unit margins.

This pricing disconnect creates a profound value asymmetry. The market is completely discounting Adobe's audited 85.8% Owner ROIC, its wide competitive moat anchored by proprietary file standards (.PSD, .AI, .PDF) and enterprise copyright indemnification, its pristine $+640M net cash balance sheet ($+1.58/sh), and its current top-line trajectory expanding at +13.0% YoY. The equity is being priced as a distressed legacy utility rather than an elite, cash-generative compounding franchise.

Business Cyclicality & Cycle Stance
Howard Marks & Warren Buffett mental models for cycle positioning and earnings durability.
1. Cyclicality Profile (The Nature of the Business)
Secular Compounder
Mission-critical software, tolls, or subscriptions (e.g. Microsoft, Visa, Apple). Revenue and cash flows compound steadily through economic downturns.
Moderate Cyclical
Consumer retail, merchant payments, or advertising (e.g. StoneCo, Amazon, Lululemon). Earnings fluctuate with interest rates and consumer spending, but long-term secular growth continues.
Deep Cyclical
Commodities, homebuilders, freight shipping, or manufacturing (e.g. NVR, Vale, GigaCloud). Earnings swing between extreme peaks and valleys. Never extrapolate peak earnings.
2. Cycle Stance (Where the Business Sits Today)
Downcycle Contraction / Trough
Operating under high interest rates, credit contractions, or inventory destocking. Starting earnings are depressed, offering asymmetric upside when conditions normalize.
Mid-Cycle Run-Rate
Operating near sustainable historical margins and normal unit economics. Reliable baseline for 3-year compounding calculations.
Peak / Over-Earning Risk
Generating temporary peak margins due to shortages or bubble pricing. Downside risk if margins mean-revert to historical levels.
