What the Market is Pricing In

At today's market price of $275.30, trading at 13.4x normalized Owner Earnings ($20.53/sh) and offering a 7.5% Owner Cash Yield, Wall Street is pricing in severe panic over a structural terminal transition. Sell-side bear notes (Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, Bernstein) argue that Adobe's legacy seat-based software monopoly is being disintermediated by AI-native tools (Midjourney, Flux, Sora), low-friction creative platforms (Canva at $4B ARR, Figma at $1.2B ARR), and open-source models. Skeptics maintain that automated generative workflows naturally destroy junior creative seats within enterprise design departments, causing a structural seat contraction that usage credit top-ups cannot offset.

Furthermore, the market views recent headline stability with deep suspicion, pointing to organic ARR deceleration to ~8.3% YoY (excluding the $480M Semrush contribution) as evidence of terminal decay. Bears emphasize rising AI compute inference costs dragging COGS, executive instability with CFO Dan Durn's sudden departure and CEO Shantanu Narayen's ongoing transition to Board Chair, and the deliberate deferral of planned Creative Cloud price hikes as definitive proof that Adobe has surrendered its pricing power.

High enterprise switching costs—anchored by standard file formats (.PSD, .AI, .PDF), enterprise legal indemnity on Firefly, and deep Adobe Experience Platform (AEP) workflows—protect Adobe's Wide Moat franchise.
