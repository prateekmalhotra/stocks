What the Market is Pricing In

At the current trading price of $185.28, representing a valuation of 23.2x P/OE and 21.9x EV/OE on normalized Owner Earnings of $8.00 per share, Mr. Market is pricing in sustained compounder multiples despite a pronounced organic growth deceleration. The market narrative assumes that Accenture's massive enterprise footprint and expanding $2.4B+ quarterly AI pipeline will easily shield its traditional consulting business from labor efficiency deflation, enabling steady mid-single-digit top-line expansion.

However, Wall Street sell-side research points to a growing structural disconnect. Morgan Stanley downgraded ACN to Equalweight with a reduced $177 price target, citing proprietary CIO survey data showing IT services budget growth slowing to just ~2% as enterprise AI spending crowds out traditional discretionary consulting budgets rather than expanding overall tech spending. Investors paying 23.2x P/OE are assuming that aggressive M&A roll-ups can permanently mask organic volume stagnation without degrading return on invested capital.

How Things Are Going Now

Accenture's recent operating performance demonstrates a clear divergence between stable managed services and decelerating short-cycle consulting. In Q3 FY26, GAAP revenue reached $18.72B (up 3% YoY in local currency), slightly missing consensus expectations ($18.78B) and prompting management to trim full-year local currency revenue guidance to 3%–4%. While managed services remain resilient, strategy and consulting deals face extended approval cycles and discretionary spending cuts across North America and Europe.

New bookings in Q3 FY26 totaled $19.32B (down 3% YoY in local currency), bringing book-to-bill ratios down toward 1.03x. Although GenAI bookings have expanded rapidly past $2.4B quarterly, institutional data indicates these deals are largely cannibalizing legacy systems integration budgets rather than providing additive high-margin growth. Meanwhile, rate-card realization pressure keeps gross margins constrained near the 0.0% run-rate baseline.

On the balance sheet and capital allocation front, Accenture returned $2.2B to shareholders in Q3 FY26 ($1.2B repurchases, $1.0B dividends) and continues targeted M&A (e.g., Dragos, runZero, NetRise in OT security). However, organic revenue growth remains constrained near 0.0% to 3.0% YoY, reinforcing the reality that capital deployment is increasingly required just to defend enterprise market share.
