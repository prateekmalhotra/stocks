Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Amaroq Minerals Ltd. (LSE/NASDAQ Iceland: AMRQ, OTCQX: AMRQF) has completed its operational inflection, transitioning from a cash-consuming exploration developer into a cash-generating, high-grade gold producer in Southern Greenland. According to the company's Q2 2026 financial and operational results (published August 13, 2026), Amaroq delivered revenue of C$37.3 million for the quarter and C$56.2 million for the first half of 2026, marking a 1,553% YoY surge compared to C$3.4 million in H1 2025. Net income for H1 2026 reached C$13.6 million (with Q2 generating C$11.1 million in net profit), representing a complete turnaround from the net loss of C$10.0 million posted in H1 2025. This earnings inflection was anchored by the ramp-up of the 100%-owned Nalunaq Gold Mine, which produced 8,985 ounces of gold in H1 2026 (5,363 ounces in Q2 alone), exceeding the midpoint of management's H1 guidance range of 7,000 to 10,000 ounces.

The operational highlight of the period was the commissioning of the Phase 2 flotation recovery circuit in June 2026. Historically operating on a gravity-only processing setup that recovered 50%–70% of contained gold, the addition of the flotation circuit drove metallurgical recovery rates to approximately 90% in July 2026, aligning with management's steady-state target of 90%–95%. Operational productivity was further enhanced by processed head grades averaging 19.8 g/t Au in H1 2026 and 22.2 g/t Au in Q2 2026—substantially outperforming initial guidance of 14–15 g/t Au. Concurrently, gold sales for H1 2026 reached 8,610 ounces at an average realized price of US$4,696 per ounce (Q2 sales of 5,640 ounces at US$4,678 per ounce). Consequently, management reiterated full-year 2026 production guidance of 25,000 to 35,000 ounces of gold.

From a balance sheet and capital allocation standpoint, Amaroq ended Q2 2026 with C$28.5 million in cash and cash equivalents and total assets of C$426.8 million. Total available group liquidity expanded to approximately US$47 million following the execution of an expanded US$70 million revolving credit facility (doubled from US$35 million) at reduced interest margins. In tandem with core gold production, Amaroq continues to advance its multi-commodity strategy across Greenland, funding exploration in strategic critical minerals via its 51%-owned Gardaq A/S Joint Venture (partnered with GCAM and Moore Capital) and scaling its logistics arm, Suliaq A/S (90% owned, with private UK family office JLE Group acquiring 10%). On July 31, 2026, Amaroq successfully completed its listing migration to the Main Market of the London Stock Exchange, positioning the business for broader institutional capital access and index inclusion.

Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

Amaroq operates a dual-engine mining business model in Greenland, combining cash-flow generation from high-grade precious metal extraction with high-optionality exploration across strategic critical minerals. The core financial driver is the 100%-owned Nalunaq Gold Mine in South Greenland. Amaroq generates revenue primarily through the processing of underground ore into gold doré bars, which are transported and sold to international precious metals refiners and off-takers (such as Auramet) at prevailing global spot prices (XAU/USD). A portion of production is accredited as Single Mine Origin (SMO) gold, commanding modest realized premiums from European jewelry and retail buyers.

The unit economics of Nalunaq are governed by four fundamental variables: mined ore tonnage, processed head grade (g/t Au), metallurgical recovery efficiency (%), and operational cost absorption. Because gold producers are absolute price-takers with zero pricing power or customer switching costs, competitive advantage rests entirely on asset quality and cost curve positioning. Nalunaq’s structural economic moat stems from its extraordinary head grade, averaging 19.8 to 22.2 g/t Au during H1 2026—roughly 12 to 15 times the global average underground gold mine grade (~1.5 g/t Au). This ultra-high grade means Amaroq extracts significantly more dollar value per tonne of rock moved, insulating unit operating margins against general mining cost inflation. While initial H1 2026 All-In Sustaining Costs (AISC) were elevated near ~$4,000/oz due to flotation circuit construction, winter rotation expenses, and owner-operator transition costs, management targets Q4 2026 AISC of US$1,250–$1,450/oz as processing rates scale toward the nameplate plant capacity of 260–300 tonnes per day.

Beyond Nalunaq, Amaroq possesses a regional infrastructure and licensing moat. The company holds a dominant ~7,800 km² mineral license footprint across the Nanortalik Gold Belt, Sava Copper Belt, and Gardar Province. To solve the logistical entry barrier of operating in Greenland, Amaroq established Suliaq A/S (90% owned), a dedicated logistics joint venture that controls specialized icebreakers, tugs, barges, heli-portable drill rigs, and heavy mobile equipment. This proprietary supply chain creates a formidable moat against potential regional entrants who lack specialized Arctic infrastructure.

Structural Demand Drivers & Long-Term Tailwinds

  • Secular Central Bank Accumulation & Gold Real Realignment: Global sovereign reserve diversification continues to drive structural demand for physical gold, maintaining high benchmark floor prices and maximizing cash margins for ultra-high-grade underground mines like Nalunaq.
  • Western Geopolitical Mandate for Non-Chinese Strategic Minerals: Amaroq's Gardaq JV (51% Amaroq / 49% GCAM) controls major prospective land packages in the Gardar Province for Rare Earth Elements (Ilua Pegmatite) and IOCG (Minturn), alongside past-producing Black Angel (Zinc, Lead, Silver, Germanium, Gallium). As the EU and US enforce Critical Raw Materials acts, Amaroq is positioned for non-dilutive strategic funding, grant capital, and customer off-take prepayments.
  • First-Mover Operational & Infrastructure Monopoly: Operating through Suliaq A/S, Amaroq controls key maritime and air support assets required to run industrial operations in Southern Greenland, creating an entrenched infrastructure competitive moat.

Competitive Disruption & Operational Execution Threats

  • Arctic Weather Seasonality & Logistical Vulnerability: Extreme winter ice conditions, fjord freezing, and severe Atlantic weather can interrupt supply shipments, air rotations, and sea transport, creating quarterly operational slippages (such as the 4-week drilling delay experienced at Minturn).
  • Single-Asset Cash Flow Concentration: Near-term revenues and operating cash flows depend 100% on the single underground mining face at Nalunaq. Any localized underground ground control issues, grade dilution, or mill equipment downtime would immediately impair quarterly free cash flow.
  • Execution Risk on AISC Compression: While H1 2026 produced net income, full-year AISC remain high (~$2,400/oz average) during initial plant ramp-up. Failure to scale throughput to nameplate 300 t/d capacity or manage diesel and labor costs would prevent AISC from compressing to the target US$1,250–$1,450/oz level.

Section 6: Thesis Invalidation Pre-Mortem & Falsification Triggers

1. Pre-Mortem Analysis: What Could Destroy This Thesis

To audit this investment objectively, we construct a pre-mortem imagining that in August 2029, Amaroq has destroyed shareholder capital and trades at penny-stock distress. The failure modes stem from three key vulnerabilities:

  • Metallurgical & Geostatistical Failure at Nalunaq: High-grade narrow-vein gold deposits are notoriously prone to extreme grade variability (nugget effect) and severe ore dilution. If stoping controls fail and processed head grades collapse below 12 g/t Au, the processing plant throughput (capped at 300 t/d) cannot generate sufficient gold ounces to cover heavy fixed Arctic infrastructure costs. Furthermore, if the flotation circuit suffers persistent chemical fouling or winter freezing, lowering recoveries back to 60-70%, cash flow will evaporate.
  • Capital Allocation Drain & Suliaq/Gardaq Over-Expansion: Rather than returning cash generated at Nalunaq to shareholders or paying down debt, management over-allocates capital into early-stage, capital-intensive exploration projects (e.g., Maarmorilik zinc/lead PEA or Suliaq logistics expansion). Unbridled exploration CapEx coupled with high debt servicing costs ($70M facility) could force highly dilutive equity raises.
  • Greenland Arctic Supply Chain Breakdown: Weather-induced delays, sea-ice blockages preventing vessel access to Nanortalik fjords, or delays in operating the newly acquired icebreaker vessel could choke supply lines, delay essential spare parts, and stall mine operations for months at a time.
