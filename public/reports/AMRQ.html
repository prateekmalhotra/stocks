01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market is currently pricing Amaroq Ltd. at a steep valuation of 373.5x P/OE and 385.2x EV/OE, reflecting deep skepticism regarding the company's ability to transition from a high-cost developer to a highly profitable, steady-state producer in the harsh Arctic environment of Greenland. The market is heavily anchoring to the historical and H1 2026 All-In Sustaining Costs (AISC) of ~$4,000 per ounce, fearing that the structural complexities of operating in South Greenland will permanently impair margins. Furthermore, the back-end-weighted nature of the FY2026 production guidance—requiring a massive 2x to 3x acceleration in H2 to meet the 25,000 to 35,000-ounce target—is viewed by skeptics as an accident waiting to happen.

Additionally, the market is pricing in severe dilution fatigue and capital overreach. With a massive multi-project pipeline spanning Nalunaq, Nanoq, and Black Angel, and a rapidly depleting cash balance that has forced the company to draw down $57 million of its $70 million revolving credit facility, investors fear that another massive equity raise is imminent. This overhang, combined with fragmented liquidity across multiple listings and selling pressure from Icelandic mutual funds, has kept the stock depressed at $1.42, pricing the business as a speculative junior miner rather than an emerging high-grade cash compounder.

03

How Things Are Going Now

In stark contrast to the bear narrative, Amaroq's operational reality reveals a highly successful transition into a cash-generating producer. In the latest quarter, the company generated $27.2M in revenue, representing a current annualized run-rate of $108.9M, with an exceptional gross margin of 67.0%. The company sold 5,640 ounces of gold (producing 5,363 ounces) at the Nalunaq Mine, capturing an extraordinary average realized gold price of US$4,678 per ounce sold. This high-grade profile has already begun to validate the economic potential of the Nanortalik Gold Belt.

Operationally, the critical catalyst was the successful commissioning of the Phase 2 flotation circuit in June 2026, which immediately boosted gold recovery rates from ~60% to over 90% in July. This technological milestone dramatically de-risks the H2 2026 production ramp-up. Furthermore, the company generated positive operating cash flow of $20.4M in H1 2026, demonstrating that the business is rapidly inflecting toward self-sustainability.

On the capital allocation front, management is actively mitigating dilution risks. The strategic minerals portfolio has been insulated via the Gardaq JV, securing non-dilutive funding from ACAM LP, while the services subsidiary Suliaq ApS is seeking third-party equity. With peak CapEx now in the past and the transition to an owner-operator model underway, Amaroq is poised to rapidly compress its AISC toward the targeted US$1,250–$1,450/oz range as throughput reaches nameplate capacity.

3. How Things Are Going Now

Amaroq is undergoing a major operational inflection across four consecutive quarters as it converts Nalunaq from a trial development project into commercial production. In Q3 2025, the company produced 3,536 ounces of gold during initial plant ramp-up. For full-year FY2025, production totaled 6.35 koz (5.31 koz sold), generating revenue of C$27.0 million and gross profit of C$11.1 million under Phase 1 gravity-only processing. Operational performance accelerated significantly in Q2 2026, with Nalunaq producing 5,363 ounces of gold and selling 5,640 ounces of gold at an average realized price of US$4,678 per ounce. Quarterly revenue reached C$37.3 million (~$26.9 million USD), establishing a real-time annualized revenue run-rate of $107.7 million USD. Processed head grades reached 22.2 g/t Au in Q2 (H1 2026 average: 19.8 g/t Au), significantly beating mine plan guidance of 14–15 g/t Au. H1 2026 revenue rose to C$56.2 million with a net profit of C$13.6 million and operating cash flow of C$20.4 million, while latest gross margins reached 67.3%.

The completion and commissioning of the Phase 2 flotation circuit in late June 2026 lifted gold recovery rates from ~63% in H1 to ~90% by July, setting up a heavily back-end weighted H2 2026 toward full-year guidance of 25,000–35,000 ounces. Capital allocation remains focused on internal reinvestment; the company ended Q2 2026 with $28.5 million in cash and completed its corporate listing transition to the London Stock Exchange Main Market on July 31, 2026, enhancing institutional access.

Secular Compounder

Moderate Cyclical
Consumer retail, merchant payments, or advertising (e.g. StoneCo, Amazon, Lululemon). Earnings fluctuate with interest rates and consumer spending, but long-term secular growth continues.
Deep Cyclical
Commodities, homebuilders, freight shipping, or manufacturing (e.g. NVR, Vale, GigaCloud). Earnings swing between extreme peaks and valleys. Never extrapolate peak earnings.
2. Cycle Stance (Where the Business Sits Today)
Downcycle Contraction / Trough
Operating under high interest rates, credit contractions, or inventory destocking. Starting earnings are depressed, offering asymmetric upside when conditions normalize.
Mid-Cycle Run-Rate
Operating near sustainable historical margins and normal unit economics. Reliable baseline for 3-year compounding calculations.
Peak / Over-Earning Risk
Generating temporary peak margins due to shortages or bubble pricing. Downside risk if margins mean-revert to historical levels.
