01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market is currently pricing in a severe structural reset in Novo Nordisk's long-term profitability. At the current price of $45.45, the stock trades at a modest 17.1x P/OE (~11.8x forward GAAP earnings), representing a ~60–70% drawdown from its 2024 peak. This valuation reflects deep sell-side and institutional skepticism regarding the sustainability of historical gross margins (which contracted from 83.5% in 2025 to 80.6% in Q1 2026) and pricing power. The market is heavily discounting ongoing gross-to-net (GTN) price compression in the United States, driven by Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM) rebate demands, direct-to-consumer cash-pay tiers ($149–$349/month), and impending Medicare price negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act.

Furthermore, sentiment was severely battered by clinical readouts—most notably the February 2026 REDEFINE 4 trial, where CagriSema (23.0% weight loss) failed to demonstrate non-inferiority against Eli Lilly’s Tirzepatide (25.5% weight loss). Sell-side desks at Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan have capitalized on this setback to argue that Novo Nordisk has permanently surrendered its high-margin market leadership to Lilly’s dual- and triple-agonist portfolio.

This creates a stark valuation dislocation. While the market is pricing current earnings as peak and unsustainable, it is ignoring the massive volume expansion potential of the newly approved oral Wegovy formulation and the structural unit-cost advantages of the Catalent fill-finish integration. By treating a temporary operational re-basing and capacity build-out phase as terminal decay, the market has compressed Novo's multiple to a level that provides an exceptional margin of safety for long-term compounder investors.

How Things Are Going Now

In contrast to the market's pessimistic narrative, Novo Nordisk's operational reality across the latest four consecutive quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) demonstrates resilient commercial execution. The company is currently generating a real-time annualized revenue run-rate of $48,360.0M, supported by a baseline adjusted gross margin of 80.6%. While U.S. net realized prices for injectable semaglutide experienced low-double-digit reductions, overall revenue rebounded in Q2 2026 (+7% adjusted sales growth), proving that self-pay and commercial volume growth are successfully offsetting net price compression.

The key operational breakthrough has been the commercial launch of oral Wegovy (semaglutide 25 mg tablets) in January 2026. By June 2026, oral Wegovy surpassed 3 million filled prescriptions in the U.S. (averaging one prescription filled every five seconds), with over 80% of prescriptions coming from GLP-1 naive patients. This confirms that the oral format is unlocking a massive untapped patient demographic rather than cannibalizing existing injectable Wegovy and Ozempic script volume. On the clinical front, while CagriSema missed non-inferiority in obesity against Tirzepatide in REDEFINE 4, it demonstrated superior HbA1c reduction (1.91%) and 14.2% weight loss in type 2 diabetes in the REIMAGINE 2 trial, keeping regulatory submission on track.

Capital allocation remains disciplined and heavily directed toward physical infrastructure. Novo is deploying DKK 56B to DKK 60B in annual CapEx to fully integrate the three acquired Catalent fill-finish sites, permanently resolving aseptic manufacturing bottlenecks. Despite this massive capital build-out phase, cash generation remains robust, allowing management to comfortably service net debt while maintaining dividend distributions.
