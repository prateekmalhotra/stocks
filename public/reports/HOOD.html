Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Robinhood Markets, Inc. (NASDAQ: HOOD) reported record financial performance for the second quarter of 2026, delivering $1.31 billion in total net revenues, representing a 32.3% year-over-year expansion. Performance was driven by high active retail engagement across options ($342 million, +29% YoY), equities ($129 million, +95% YoY), and a surge in event/prediction contracts ($156 million, up over 10x YoY), which comfortably offset a 38% pull-back in cryptocurrency transaction revenues ($100 million). Diluted GAAP earnings per share reached $0.62 (+48% YoY), though this figure includes a $0.14 per share ($129 million) non-operating accounting gain from the deconsolidation of Robinhood Ventures Fund I (RVI). Excluding this one-off credit, adjusted EPS stood at ~$0.48. Operating leverage expanded, driving GAAP operating margin to 43.9% and non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA to $741 million (56.7% Adjusted EBITDA margin).

The operational engine continues to scale customer assets and deposit capture. Net deposits hit a single-quarter record of $21.7 billion (a 28% annualized growth rate on platform assets), bringing Total Platform Assets to $369 billion (+32% YoY). Robinhood added nearly 1.0 million net funded accounts sequentially to reach 28.4 million, while Gold subscribers expanded 39% YoY to 4.84 million. The company now maintains 13 separate product lines operating at an annualized recurring revenue (ARR) run rate above $100 million—including the newly integrated Robinhood Legend desktop platform, Robinhood Credit Card, and event contracts—demonstrating structural revenue diversification away from pure crypto trading volatility.

From a capital allocation and balance sheet standpoint, Robinhood altered its capital structure during Q2 2026 by issuing $2.2 billion in zero-coupon convertible senior notes to bolster corporate liquidity for operational trading scale. Operating cash flow generation remains high, with LTM Free Cash Flow hovering near $2.10 billion. Management revised its full-year 2026 expense outlook downward, lowering combined Adjusted Operating Expenses and Stock-Based Compensation guidance to $2.675B–$2.775B (from $2.700B–$2.825B), demonstrating operating expense discipline despite ongoing investments in Canada, Singapore, and decentralized trading infrastructure (Robinhood Chain).

Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

Robinhood operates a multi-stream digital financial services platform monetizing retail trading activity, cash balances, and subscription services across three core revenue engines:

  • Transaction-Based Revenue ($776M in Q2 2026, 59% of Total): Earned primarily via Payment for Order Flow (PFOF) and execution rebates. Wholesalers and market makers pay Robinhood for routing retail market orders in options ($342M) and equities ($129M). Crypto transactions ($100M) generate direct spread markups. Event contracts ($156M) generate per-contract clearing and exchange fees through Robinhood's proprietary market venues (including Rothera and partner exchanges).
  • Net Interest Revenue ($389M in Q2 2026, 30% of Total): Generated from interest earned on customer uninvested cash sweep balances, securities lending, and margin loans extended to active accounts. Robinhood captures a yield spread between federal funds benchmark rates and interest paid out to non-Gold retail accounts.
  • Other & Subscription Revenue ($143M in Q2 2026, 11% of Total): Driven by Robinhood Gold subscriptions ($5/month or $50/year offering 4.0%–5.0% APY cash sweep rates, 3% IRA retirement matching, and level-2 market data), interchange fees on the Robinhood Gold Credit Card, and custodial service fees.

Unit Economics & Customer Switching Costs: Robinhood’s historical moat stemmed from low customer acquisition costs (CAC) driven by mobile-native UI/UX, social referral loops, and commission-free trading. However, standard commission-free trading is now ubiquitous across legacy brokerages. Robinhood’s modern switching cost moat relies on multi-product account integration and financial lock-in incentives. The 3% IRA deposit match requires customers to maintain retirement funds on the platform for five years to avoid match clawbacks, anchoring long-term customer assets under custody (AUC). Furthermore, users who combine a Gold subscription, cash sweep account, Gold Credit Card (3% cash back), and active trading software (Robinhood Legend) face behavioral and economic friction when attempting to migrate assets to alternative platforms.

Section 3: Real Cash Flow, SBC Dilution & Owner Earnings Audit

A rigorous audit of Robinhood Markets, Inc. (HOOD) requires penetrating the veil of brokerage working capital distortions and Silicon Valley non-GAAP add-backs. In retail brokerage accounting, GAAP Operating Cash Flow is inherently volatile due to multi-billion-dollar swings in customer cash, receivables from users (margin lending), payables to users, securities borrowed/loaned, and segregated regulatory funds. For example, in FY 2024, GAAP Operating Cash Flow plunged to negative $157 million despite $1.41 billion in net income, driven entirely by a massive $4.59 billion outflow into user margin receivables. Conversely, H1 2026 GAAP Operating Cash Flow surged to $2.76 billion as customer deposits and clearing balances expanded.

To establish Warren Buffett's true Owner Earnings (Operating Cash Flow minus Maintenance CapEx minus 100% of Stock-Based Compensation), we strip away non-economic working capital noise and treat Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as an indelible, full cash expense. SBC is not a non-cash benign adjustment; it represents real value transferred from existing equity holders to employees, diluting ownership unless offset by open-market repurchases funded by real operational profits.

Buffett Cash Flow Principle: Stock-Based Compensation is an operational payroll cost paid in equity rather than cash. Excluding SBC from earnings is an accounting fiction that inflates economic reality. In our audit, 100% of SBC is subtracted alongside physical maintenance CapEx and capitalized software development costs to derive true owner cash flow.

A fundamental strength of Robinhood's evolving financial engine is its capital-light, interest-free working capital float model:

  • Internalized High-Yield Cash Sweep Float: In February 2026, Robinhood modified its brokerage cash program, transferring over $6.0 billion of Cash Sweep balances into internal free credit balances. This provides low-cost funding directly to finance high-margin user margin loans (which grew over 121% YoY to $18.4+ billion), capturing an attractive interest rate spread without relying on external bank debt.
  • Robinhood Gold Subscription Float: With Gold subscribers reaching a record 4.8 million (+39% YoY) paying $5/month upfront, Robinhood generates over $288 million in annual recurring, highly predictable subscription cash flow with virtually zero incremental CapEx required.
  • Upfront Prediction & Derivatives Collateral: Rapid growth in event contracts ($156 million revenue in Q2 2026, up 10x YoY) and options volume (324 million contracts traded in July 2026) forces retail users to lock up cash collateral upfront, generating positive operational float that boosts liquidity before trade settlement.

For a hybrid digital brokerage and fintech infrastructure platform like Robinhood, calculating true Owner Earnings requires strict accounting adjustments to strip away stock-based compensation (SBC) dilution and cyclical cash float anomalies.

The Warren Buffett Owner Earnings Equation (HOOD 2026 Normalization):
Normalized Owner Earnings = GAAP Operating Cash Flow - Maintenance CapEx - 100% Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) +/- Mid-Cycle Cash Float Adjustments

Event contracts alone accounted for $156M in Q2 2026 transaction revenues.
  • Excessive SBC Dilution & Capital Allocation Failure: If management fails to sterilize employee stock-based compensation (currently ~$380M LTM) through aggressive execution of its $1.5B share buyback authorization, per-share Owner Earnings growth will be severely diluted.
