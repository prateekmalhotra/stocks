Section 1: Business Model, Scale Moat & GenAI Transition

Charter Communications, Inc. (NASDAQ: CHTR) operates as the second-largest cable operator in the United States, providing broadband connectivity, video entertainment, mobile, and commercial voice services to over 30.4 million connectivity customers under the Spectrum brand. The core economic engine relies on a high-fixed-cost, hybrid fiber-coaxial (HFC) and fiber network passing approximately 58 million homes and businesses. This capital-dense infrastructure creates a local natural monopoly or duopoly in most operating footprints, generating high gross margins once network deployment costs are sunk.

However, Charter's core broadband moat is facing its most rigorous structural challenge in two decades. Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) from cellular carriers (T-Mobile and Verizon) continues to capture cost-conscious low-end subscribers, while aggressive fiber overbuilding by incumbent local exchange carriers (ILECs) erodes Charter's pricing power in suburban and urban markets. In response, management is executing a dual-track strategic defense: (1) expanding mobile bundling via Spectrum Mobile (utilizing an MVNO agreement with Verizon complemented by internal Wi-Fi offload), and (2) pursuing market consolidation through the transformative $34.5 billion transaction to acquire Cox Communications.

Strategic Realignment & AI Integration: Charter is deploying generative AI models across customer service and network Operations Support Systems (OSS) to automate field dispatching, diagnose noise on coax lines before customer reports, and reduce live call center volumes. With 100% US-based customer service reps, automated self-service and AI routing are projected to drive $300M+ in operational cost savings by 2027. Furthermore, Charter's high-capacity transport infrastructure positions it to supply backhaul and edge compute capacity to regional AI data center hubs.

Section 2: Quarterly Operational Breakdown & Segment Performance

Total revenue declined 1.7% year-over-year to $13.53 billion, while Adjusted EBITDA dropped 4.3% to $5.45 billion. The compression was primarily driven by residential video subscriber churn (-21,000 in Q2; -107,000 YoY) and a decline of 172,000 residential Internet customers, accelerating from the 120,000 lost in Q1 2026. Section 3: Real Cash Flow & Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Audit

Evaluating Charter through Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett’s First Principles requires stripping away accounting adjustments to measure genuine Owner Earnings. During H1 2026, Charter generated $8.23 billion in operating cash flow, balanced against $5.73 billion in capital expenditures, yielding $2.34 billion in reported free cash flow. Capital expenditures remain elevated due to the execution of Charter's network evolution project (DOCSIS 4.0 upgrades delivering multi-gigabit symmetrical speeds) and subsidized rural line extensions (BEAD / RDOF programs).

Crucially, of the ~$11.0 billion annual CapEx budget, roughly $4.5 billion represents discretionary growth CapEx (subsidized rural buildouts and DOCSIS 4.0 infrastructure upgrade). True maintenance CapEx—the capital required to keep existing footprint plants operational and preserve volume—is estimated at ~$6.5 billion annually. Stock-based compensation (SBC) is exceptionally low for a company of Charter's scale (~$300 million annually), reflecting management's alignment with cash return objectives over dilutive equity grants.

Section 4: Capital Allocation & Balance Sheet Discipline

Charter's balance sheet is heavily leveraged, carrying $93.85 billion in total principal debt as of June 30, 2026, corresponding to a Net Debt to Last-Twelve-Months Adjusted EBITDA ratio of 4.18x. Management operates within a target leverage corridor of 4.0x to 4.5x, historically directing almost all discretionary free cash flow toward aggressive open-market share buybacks. In H1 2026, the company repurchased 8.3 million Class A shares for $1.80 billion while also buying back $1.2 billion in senior notes for $1.0 billion in cash, capturing a $239 million net gain on debt extinguishment.

The pending acquisition of Cox Communications alters near-term capital priorities. The August 12, 2026 8-K filing detailed the pricing of $4.75 billion in senior secured notes across tranches maturing between 2032 and 2056 with interest rates between 6.050% and 7.850%. These proceeds will fund the $4.2 billion cash consideration for Cox. Combined with Cox's assumed net debt of ~$12.4 billion, pro forma total debt will surpass $108 billion.
