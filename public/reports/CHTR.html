Section 1: Executive Summary & Operating Reality

Charter Communications, Inc. (CHTR) presents a classic Benjamin Graham special-situation setup: an essential infrastructure utility trading at historically compressed equity valuations while undergoing an operational shift. At a current stock price of $151.69, Mr. Market has heavily discounted Charter due to persistent core broadband subscriber erosion (-172,000 net internet customers in Q2 2026, ending at 29.4 million). This headwind stems from aggressive Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) competition at the low-end of the market and expanding suburban fiber overbuilds. However, looking past near-term top-line headwinds, Charter's Q2 2026 financial release demonstrated durable underlying cash generation. Consolidated revenue reached $13.53 billion (down 1.7% YoY), while Adjusted EBITDA came in at $5.45 billion (reflecting a robust 40.3% EBITDA margin). Operational efficiency and debt extinguishment gains drove a GAAP earnings per share beat of $10.66, exceeding Wall Street consensus by $0.25.

The core engine offsetting traditional wireline broadband softness is Charter's rapid scaling of Spectrum Mobile. The company added 406,000 mobile lines in Q2 2026 (+1.7 million over the last twelve months), expanding its total mobile base to 12.54 million lines (+16% YoY). This mobile convergence strategy—underpinned by an aggressive MVNO agreement with Verizon and proprietary Wi-Fi offload infrastructure—serves as both a high-margin growth vehicle and a defensive churn-reduction moat for broadband relationships. From a balance sheet and cash flow perspective, Charter generated $3.9 billion in operating cash flow and $969 million in Free Cash Flow (FCF) for the quarter. The company is currently executing the peak capital expenditure phase of its high-split DOCSIS 4.0 network upgrades and rural BEAD line extensions (standalone 2026 CapEx projected at ~$11.4 billion). Management has clearly outlined a post-2026 CapEx inflection point, where annual capital outlays will drop by over $3.0 billion to a sustainable run-rate below $8.0 billion, unlocking an immense wave of owner-earning cash flow.

Crucially, Charter is on the cusp of transformative corporate catalysts. The imminent closing of the Cox Communications acquisition in mid-to-late August 2026—combined with the contemporaneous closing of the Liberty Broadband combination—will create an unprecedented connectivity footprint serving over 37 million customer relationships with $67 billion in combined revenues. Management projects at least $800 million to $1.0 billion in annualized run-rate cost synergies. To manage its $93.8 billion debt principal profile (4.18x net leverage), Charter executed proactive debt exchange offers in August 2026 ($2.0 billion cap on new 2038 senior secured notes) and repurchased $1.2 billion of existing notes at a discount, capturing $239 million in net debt extinguishment gains. Complementing corporate debt repurchases, insider Form 4 filings reveal key director cluster purchases totaling $3.76 million across the spring and summer of 2026—notably Director Mauricio Ramos acquiring 9,929 shares at $140.93 per share—signaling strong internal alignment at current depressed valuations.

Section 2: Business Model Reality & Competitive Moat

Charter Communications operates as a last-mile telecommunications and data transmission utility. The primary cash flow driver is residential and commercial wireline broadband service, supplemented by mobile voice/data lines, video distribution, and enterprise connectivity. The underlying physics of Charter’s Hybrid Fiber-Coaxial (HFC) network creates compelling unit economics: once coaxial and fiber drops are passed along public rights-of-way, the incremental cash cost of routing additional terabytes of data through an existing neighborhood node approaches zero. Charter monetizes this infrastructure by pricing monthly broadband access across various speed tiers (e.g., 300 Mbps, 500 Mbps, 1 Gbps+), driving high incremental gross margins above 85% on standalone internet connections.

To defend against customer churn, Charter utilizes a high-friction customer bundling ecosystem centered on seamless fixed-mobile convergence. Under its Spectrum Mobile banner, Charter offers unlimited mobile lines at disruptive price points (often $29.99/line or guaranteed $1,000 annual family savings when switching from legacy wireless carriers). Charter achieves superior unit cost economics on mobile by routing over 85% of mobile data traffic over its own wireline network via indoor and outdoor Wi-Fi access points and CBRS spectrum offload, paying wholesale MVNO rates to Verizon only for remaining cellular roaming. Multi-product customers who bundle Spectrum Broadband with Spectrum Mobile exhibit 20% to 30% lower annualized churn than single-play broadband users, building switching costs and embedding customer lock-in.

However, Charter’s pricing power in unbundled, low-tier broadband has faced cyclical pressure due to the rapid rise of Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) from T-Mobile and Verizon. FWA offers excess 5G capacity to price-sensitive households at $40–$50 per month, capturing gross additions at the bottom end of the market. Furthermore, incumbent telcos (e.g., AT&T, Frontier) continue to overbuild fiber-to-the-home (FTTH) across Charter's legacy footprint. While FWA suffers from absolute capacity limits per cell site and FTTH requires prohibitive CapEx ($1,200–$1,800 per home passed), Charter's DOCSIS 4.0 high-split upgrade costs only ~$100–$150 per home passed to deliver symmetrical gigabit speeds. This structural capital efficiency advantage forms the bedrock of Charter's long-term economic moat.

Structural Demand Drivers vs. Competitive Disruption Threats

  • Structural Demand Drivers (Long-Term Tailwinds):
  • Cox Acquisition Synergies & Scale: The combined Charter-Cox enterprise will command over 37 million customer relationships. Operational integration will yield $800 million to $1.0 billion in cost synergies via combined programming procurement, customer service automation, and corporate overhead elimination.
  • Spectrum Mobile Expansion & CBRS Offload: With 12.54 million mobile lines and growing at +1.7M lines annually, mobile is evolving into a major standalone EBITDA contributor. As Charter deploys CBRS spectrum and expands its 50+ million Wi-Fi hotspot network, offload rates will rise, driving MVNO margins higher.
  • Post-2026 Capital Expenditure Cliff: As Charter completes its DOCSIS 4.0 rollout and subsidized rural expansion (BEAD program) in 2026–2027, annual CapEx will decline from $11.4 billion to under $8.0 billion. This ~$3.4 billion annual cash drain reduction flows directly into Owner Earnings and free cash flow.
  • Exponential Data Demand CAGR: Average household broadband consumption is expanding at 15–20% annually (currently >700 GB/month), driven by 4K/8K video streaming, cloud gaming, remote work, and home AI agent usage. This growing payload demand naturally pushes users off congested FWA connections onto Charter's unconstrained wireline pipe.
  • Competitive Disruption Threats (Key Risks):
  • Leverage & Refinancing Cost in Higher-for-Longer Rate Environment: Charter holds $93.85 billion in total principal debt. Although debt maturities are laddered and weighted toward fixed rates, refinancing legacy debt at current yields increases annual interest expenses, requiring strict cash allocation toward net debt reduction (targeting 3.5x net leverage).
  • Incumbent Fiber Overbuilds: Overlapping fiber footprint from competitors (AT&T, Frontier, regional builders) currently sits at ~45–50% of Charter’s network. When fiber enters a market, Charter typically loses 5–10 percentage points of broadband market share over a 3-year period before stabilizing.
  • Low-End FWA Price Dumping: Wireless carriers (T-Mobile/Verizon) continue to use excess mid-band 5G network capacity to offer low-cost home internet ($40–$50/mo). While FWA speed and latency are inferior, it caps Charter's short-term pricing power on entry-level broadband tiers.

Section 3: Real Cash Flow, SBC Dilution & Owner Earnings Audit

A cold, unvarnished audit of Charter Communications (NASDAQ: CHTR) requires cutting through Wall Street's favorite non-GAAP proxy, "Adjusted EBITDA" ($5.45B in Q2 2026, down 4.3% YoY), and examining the real cash entering and leaving the enterprise. Cable systems are capital-intensive utility-like networks undergoing an aggressive physical upgrade cycle. To determine true intrinsic value under Warren Buffett's 1986 Shareholder Letter doctrine, we must separate maintenance capital expenditures—the capital required to defend the existing 29.39 million internet subscriber moat against fixed wireless access (FWA) and fiber overbuilders—from discretionary growth CapEx (rural RDOF/BEAD line extensions and High-Split DOCSIS 4.0 upgrades).

Furthermore, Silicon Valley accounting practice routinely treats Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) as a non-cash add-back to inflate operating cash flows. In our Graham-Buffett framework, SBC represents 100% real economic dilution and cash cost to shareholders. Every share issued to executives is a share that must be repurchased on the open market to preserve per-share intrinsic value.

Buffett Owner Earnings Philosophy applied to CHTR: "If board members think stock options aren't compensation, what are they? If stock-based compensation isn't an expense, what is it? And if non-recurring capital expenses required to keep your plant operating aren't expenses, what are they?" CHTR's current standalone CapEx of $11.4B in FY 2026 represents a cyclical peak. Post-2026, as network evolution completes, CapEx will roll down below $8.0B, unmasking massive hidden Owner Earnings.

Working Capital Float & Customer Prepayment Dynamics

Charter operates with a structural working capital advantage rooted in its recurring subscription business model. Customers prepay monthly for broadband, video, and mobile connectivity services before Charter settles its operational expenses and programmer fees. This negative working capital float provides an interest-free source of operating capital.

However, two distinct cash flow dynamics require close monitoring:

  • Equipment Installment Plan (EIP) Financing: As Spectrum Mobile scales rapidly (+406,000 mobile lines in Q2 2026 to reach 12.54 million lines, up 1.7M LTM), Charter finances device purchases for customers. Receipts from mobile customers flow through operating cash flow, while borrowings under the off-balance sheet/SPV EIP facility ($1.596B carrying value) flow through financing activities, creating a minor timing mismatch that slightly elevates operating cash flow during hyper-growth mobile phases.
  • CapEx Accrual Lag: In Q2 2026, GAAP Free Cash Flow came in at $969 million (down $77M YoY) despite operating cash flow rising to $3.9 billion (vs. $3.6 billion in Q2 2025). The discrepancy was driven by an unfavorable $280M change in accrued expenses related to capital expenditures, emphasizing why multi-quarter cash flow tracking is vital rather than single-quarter extrapolation.

Share Buyback Cannibalization Analysis

Charter is one of the most aggressive "share cannibal" plays in modern corporate history. Since 2016, Charter has repurchased approximately 188.0 million Class A shares and common units for $80.6 billion, reducing the total diluted share count by over 55%.

In the first half of 2026, Charter bought back 8.28 million shares for $1.801 billion (including 4.0 million shares for $838 million in Q2 2026 at an average price near $209.50). However, management announced a temporary buyback pause in Q3 2026. This operational pause is a prudent capital allocation choice to preserve cash for the pending $34.5B Cox Communications transaction ($4.2B upfront cash consideration required) and accelerate net leverage down to the target 3.5x band. Once the Cox deal closes and post-2026 CapEx drops below $8.0B, buybacks are expected to resume aggressively at current historically depressed market multiples.
