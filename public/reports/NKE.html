01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market is attempting to digest a complex multi-year turnaround at Nike, Inc. as the stock sits near 12-year lows at $40.76. At a valuation of 41.1x Normalized Owner Earnings ($0.99/share) and 40.3x Enterprise Value to Owner Earnings, the market appears trapped between paying a historical legacy premium for an iconic global consumer platform and confronting deteriorating core operational fundamentals. Skeptics emphasize that headline earnings beats are being heavily distorted by non-operational tax and duty windfalls, masking underlying unit contraction, severe direct-to-consumer channel friction, and market share loss in high-margin categories.

The central disconnect lies in whether Nike's recent earnings collapse represents a temporary structural transition as new leadership under CEO Elliott Hill fixes legacy distribution missteps, or if it signals terminal decay in brand equity. With wholesale partners demanding margin concessions, direct digital sales dropping 12% YoY, and agile performance competitors like On Running and HOKA seizing market share in technical running, the market is pricing in prolonged top-line stagnation and structural gross margin erosion through FY28.

How Things Are Going Now

Nike's current operational reality reflects an unvarnished rebalancing act. Trailing twelve-month revenue stands at $46,398.0M, but the latest quarter annualized run-rate has slowed to $43,888.0M. Revenue trajectory shows clear channel divergence: Wholesale revenues grew 6.2% YoY to $27,500M as Nike re-engaged partners like Foot Locker and Dick's Sporting Goods to move product, while Direct revenues contracted 5.9% YoY to $17,700M due to digital softness and physical store foot traffic declines.

Gross margins for the latest quarter printed at 49.2%, but stripping out the non-operational $986M tariff recovery benefit reveals true core gross margins of 40.2%. The global workforce of 73,000 employees is undergoing restructuring, incurring significant cash severance charges ($230M in Q3 FY26 alone). Liquid cash and investments remain healthy at $9,027.0M against total debt of $7,942.0M, giving Nike a modest net cash cushion of $1,085M ($+0.73/share).
