01

What the Market is Pricing In

Mr. Market is currently pricing ASML at an aggressive premium of 58.3x Owner Earnings, reflecting an expectation of uninterrupted dominance in the semiconductor lithography space. However, beneath this optimistic surface, the market is beginning to grapple with a severe structural disconnect. Skeptics point to a looming air pocket in High-NA EUV adoption, where key customers like TSMC are actively pushing back against the prohibitive $400 million price tag per system, choosing instead to delay implementation until at least 2029. This creates a massive concentration risk, leaving ASML heavily reliant on a struggling Intel and Samsung to absorb early High-NA volumes.

Additionally, the geopolitical landscape presents an existential threat to ASML's highly profitable recurring revenue stream. The introduction of the MATCH Act threatens to completely sever ASML's ability to service and maintain its massive installed base of DUV immersion systems in China, which historically accounted for up to a third of total revenues. Combined with emerging domestic Chinese lithography alternatives and Canon's low-cost Nanoimprint Lithography (NIL) technology gaining traction in memory applications, the market's current valuation assumes peak earnings power that may be structurally unsustainable if these headwinds materialize simultaneously.

How Things Are Going Now

In stark contrast to the bear thesis, ASML's current operational performance demonstrates robust near-term momentum. In the latest quarter, the company sold exactly 91 total lithography systems, consisting of 86 new and 5 used units, representing a strong 19.7% year-over-year volume expansion. This operational volume, combined with an average net system revenue of €72.5M ($82.7M) per unit and €2,762M ($3,148.7M) in Installed Base Management sales, has propelled the company's annualized revenue run-rate to $42,526.6M.

Profitability remains exceptionally strong, with the latest quarter's gross margin reaching 54.0%, driven by a highly favorable software upgrade mix within the Installed Base Management segment. This high-margin service revenue acts as a resilient buffer against cyclicality. On the capital allocation front, ASML continues to return substantial cash to shareholders, backed by a liquid cash and investments position of $8,643.5M against total debt of $6,170.0M, leaving a healthy net cash cushion of $2,473M ($+6.44 per share).
