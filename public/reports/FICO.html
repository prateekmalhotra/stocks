01

What the Market is Pricing In

At today's market price of $1,172.67 (32.9x P/OE, 39.4x EV/OE, 3.0% Owner Yield), Mr. Market is pricing in significant structural friction following the pull-back from peak $2,000 levels. A chorus of sell-side bear notes from Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, MoffettNathanson, and Bernstein highlights that FICO's high-margin B2B Scores pricing expansion is confronting a regulatory ceiling. The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) and HUD mandates permitting VantageScore 4.0 alongside FICO 10T in GSE mortgage underwriting directly challenge FICO's 30-year single-score monopoly, granting originators score substitution optionality and inciting pushback from the Mortgage Bankers Association and Capitol Hill. Active antitrust scrutiny from state attorneys general and the Department of Justice (DOJ) further amplifies fears that federal regulators could cap per-score funding fees.

Concurrently, equity markets are discounting operational drag in legacy enterprise software, where non-platform software ARR contracted 17% YoY with net retention dropping to 82%. On the balance sheet, an aggressive $1.96 billion debt-funded share repurchase drive in Q3 FY2026 elevated net debt to $-5,334M ($-234.94 per share) and net leverage to ~3.7x EBITDA, forcing management to explicitly pause open-market share repurchases. With FICO's historical per-share cannibal compounding mechanism temporarily halted to service term loan debt, investors fear that moderating B2B pricing leverage combined with reverse operating leverage in legacy software will squeeze consolidated cash flow margins.

How Things Are Going Now

In operational reality, Fair Isaac Corporation continues to deliver solid top-line execution driven by core pricing leverage and cloud SaaS migration. Trailing twelve-month revenue reached $2,393.6M, with latest Q3 FY2026 revenue reporting $674.2M (+25.7% YoY), representing a real-time annualized revenue run-rate of $2,696.8M. Latest gross margin remained highly lucrative at 87.1%. Segment performance exhibits a bifurcated trajectory: B2B Scores revenue surged +41% YoY to $458.9M (with mortgage origination scores reaching $282M, up 97% YoY due to wholesale fee restructuring), while Software ARR reached $815.8M (+10.0% YoY).

Within Software, the enterprise transition to cloud infrastructure demonstrates stark divergence between modern and legacy units. Platform ARR reached $412.8M (+62.0% YoY)—surpassing non-platform ARR for the first time—with Platform Dollar-Based Net Retention Rate (DBNRR) reaching an exceptional 148%. Conversely, Non-Platform Software ARR declined 17% YoY to $402.8M with a depressed DBNRR of 82%, as enterprise clients either migrate to the FICO Platform or churn off legacy solutions, keeping overall Software DBNRR at 109%.

Capital allocation reflects balance sheet recalibration following aggressive buybacks. FICO holds $248.4M in cash and equivalents against $5,582.4M in total debt, resulting in $-5,334M in net balance sheet debt ($-234.94 per share). Management deployed $1.96 billion into share repurchases during Q3 FY2026, pushing net leverage to ~3.7x EBITDA. Consequently, management has paused open-market buybacks to channel operational free cash flow toward term loan reduction. FICO's Wide Moat remains intact due to deep regulatory integration into securitization markets and bank capital models, though pricing execution faces heightened public watchfulness.
