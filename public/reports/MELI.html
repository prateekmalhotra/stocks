Section 1: Company Overview & Audited Financial Baseline

MercadoLibre, Inc. (NASDAQ: MELI) operates the undisputed, vertically integrated e-commerce and financial technology monopoly across Latin America. Incorporated in Delaware in 1999 and headquartered in Montevideo, Uruguay, the enterprise functions as a self-reinforcing flywheel linking online retail, third-party marketplace logistics, digital payments, point-of-sale (POS) merchant processing, consumer and merchant lending, and high-margin digital advertising. Financial results and operational accounts are presented strictly in United States Dollars ($ USD), while local operational cash flows originate in Latin American foreign currencies, primarily the Brazilian Real (converted at an exchange rate of BRL 5.50 / $1.00 USD; 1 ADS = 1 Common Share), Mexican Peso (MXN 18.20 / $1.00 USD), and Argentine Peso (ARS 940.00 / $1.00 USD). The latest reported statutory period is FY 2025 / Q2 2026 LTM (ending June 30, 2026).

1. The Core Business Machine, Moat & Unit Economics

MercadoLibre operates a dual-engine architecture composed of Commerce (53.3% of FY 2025 Net Revenues) and Fintech / Mercado Pago (46.7% of FY 2025 Net Revenues). Reconciled across segment disclosures, these two operating pillars sum to exactly 100.0% of consolidated Net Revenues ($30,626M). The economic engine is defined by a primary Network Effects moat, tightly reinforced by structural Switching Costs and logistics Cost Advantage scale economics:

  • Commerce Engine (53.3% of Net Revenue; Mercado Libre Marketplace & Mercado Envios): The platform connects 121+ million active annual buyers and millions of merchant sellers across 18 countries. Monetization occurs via marketplace seller take rates (ranging from 10% to 19% depending on product category and shipping subsidies), 1P direct retail sales, fulfillment fees, and listing upgrades. The proprietary logistics infrastructure, Mercado Envios, processes over 2.4 billion shipments annually, with over 50% fulfilled directly through managed fulfillment centers. By driving down delivery times to same-day or next-day across major metropolitan centers (São Paulo, Mexico City, Buenos Aires), MercadoLibre creates an impenetrable cost and convenience barrier that prevents regional fragmentation.
  • Fintech Engine (46.7% of Net Revenue; Mercado Pago & Mercado Credito): Originally established as an online checkout gateway for the marketplace, Mercado Pago expanded off-platform into brick-and-mortar merchant acquiring (POS MPOS terminals, QR code payments) and consumer digital banking (interest-bearing wallet accounts, bill pay, credit cards). Off-platform Total Payment Volume (TPV) represents over 70% of total payment volume. Mercado Credito leverages real-time transaction data from marketplace sales and consumer wallet history to underwrite working capital loans to merchants and revolving credit lines/credit cards to consumers, producing high net interest margins (NIMAL exceeding 21%).
  • High-Margin AdTech Engine (Mercado Ads): Monetizing intent-driven search traffic across the e-commerce platform, Mercado Ads generates sponsored product listings and display media revenue. Operating with near-zero incremental marginal capital requirements, advertising revenues expand at >50% YoY, directly boosting consolidated gross and operating margins.

Buffett & Munger Pricing Power & Inflation Resistance Audit

Under the analytical framework of Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger, MercadoLibre is categorized as possessing Strong Pricing Power. The enterprise operates as an essential digital tollbridge for commerce and financial services in emerging markets.

The 'Prayer Session' Test: MercadoLibre routinely adjusts marketplace take rates, payment processing commission schedules, credit card interest rates, and flat handling fees without experiencing merchant churn or volume destruction. Because sellers depend on MercadoLibre for up to 80% of their total sales volume, and consumers rely on Mercado Pago as their primary financial account, management sets pricing unilaterally. In high-inflation environments such as Argentina, nominal transaction values automatically adjust upward with consumer prices, expanding take-rate dollar revenues in real-time without requiring renegotiation.

Customer Share of Wallet & Value Surplus: Merchant seller fees represent a modest cost of goods sold component relative to the buyer acquisition, logistics fulfillment, and payment collection capabilities provided by the platform. For consumers, Mercado Pago delivers instant financial inclusion—providing digital yield on unbanked wallet balances, instant zero-fee PIX transfers in Brazil, and micro-credit access.

  • AI Infrastructure Deployment: Integrated large language models (LLMs) across marketplace search, advertising placement algorithms, and customer support. Management reported a 1.1 percentage point increase in Brazil conversion rates and enhanced advertising monetization efficiency.

3. Owner Earnings Derivation & Cash Flow Waterfall

To establish the true recurring cash power of MercadoLibre, reported GAAP Operating Cash Flow (OCF) must be normalized. Because MercadoLibre operates an integrated fintech bank and payment gateway, GAAP OCF is heavily augmented by working capital float—specifically customer settlement deposits and merchant payables. Deducting non-operating corporate treasury interest yield, stock-based compensation (treated as a 100% cash charge), and calibrated Maintenance CapEx isolates true Core Baseline Owner Earnings (\(\text{OE}_0\)).

Disclosed Useful Lives & CapEx Calibration: Per Note 1 and Note 4 of Form 10-K, property and equipment useful lives are disclosed as: IT servers and networking hardware (3–5 years), POS terminals (3–5 years), fulfillment logistics equipment and vehicles (5–10 years), and buildings (up to 30–40 years). Total CapEx in FY 2025 was $1,343M (and $1,496M LTM Q2 2026). While Growth CapEx finances 16 new mega-fulfillment centers, air fleet acquisitions, and data center expansion, steady-state Maintenance CapEx (replacing depreciated servers, worn POS devices, and logistics vehicles) is calibrated at $470M for FY 2025 (~57% of $818M D&A) and $520M for LTM Q2 2026.

Section 2: The Three Forward-Looking Operating Stories

Based on MercadoLibre’s market footprint, operational execution track record, financial filings, and 4-quarter trajectory, we model three distinct, probable fundamental paths covering 90%–95% of operational outcomes over the FY 2025–FY 2030 horizon. All three operating stories anchor to an identical baseline: FY 2025 Net Revenue of $30,626M, an FY 2025 Operating Margin of 10.5% ($3,211M Operating Income), and a Starting Core Baseline Owner Earnings (\(\text{OE}_0\)) of EXACTLY $5,077M ($100.14/ADS) across 50.70M diluted ADSs.

Core Baseline Owner Earnings (\(\text{OE}_0\)) Bridge: Calculated as GAAP Operating Cash Flow of $12,116M less $5,946M in Fintech float and non-operational working capital normalization, less $320M in non-operating treasury yield on regulatory deposits, less $470M in Maintenance CapEx (IT infrastructure, fulfillment asset replacement, and software capitalization), and less $303M in Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) = $5,077M ($100.14/ADS).

📖 Story 1: Base Case — Strategic Reinvestment Cycle Yields Operating Leverage & Credit Maturity (16.9% OE CAGR)

Narrative & Reinvestment Framing: Under this baseline operational trajectory, MercadoLibre’s near-term margin compression—punctuated by a temporary trough in Q2 2026 where GAAP Operating Margin drops to 6.7%—is framed as a deliberate, high-ROIC growth reinvestment strategy rather than operational degradation. Management intentionally deploys capital into two powerful growth vectors: (1) lowering free shipping thresholds across Brazil and Mexico to capture higher buyer purchase frequency and non-discretionary basket share, and (2) accelerating credit card issuance, which under Expected Credit Loss (ECL) accounting rules requires 100% upfront bad debt provisioning prior to recognizing multi-year interest and merchant processing revenues. As these credit card cohorts age past 6–9 months and fulfillment delivery density reaches critical mass in major metropolitan hubs, these upfront investments yield expanding operating leverage.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Active annual buyers expand at a 7.5% CAGR from 121 million to ~165 million by FY 2030. Commerce GMV expands at an 18.0% USD CAGR (reflecting 23.5% FX-neutral growth offset by a 5.0% average annual LatAm currency depreciation against the USD), scaling from ~$50B to ~$115B. Total Payment Volume (TPV) expands at a 24.0% USD CAGR to reach $550B annually, propelled by off-platform QR code adoption and MPOS terminal expansion in Brazil and Mexico. The credit portfolio grows from $16.4B to $28.0B while Net Interest Margin After Losses (NIMAL) stabilizes at 19.5%–21.0% as proprietary machine learning scoring algorithms optimize default rates. Mercado Ads maintains high momentum, increasing platform monetization from ~1.3% of GMV to 2.2% of GMV. Consolidated Net Revenue grows from $30,626M in FY 2025 to $72,500M by FY 2030 (a 18.8% USD CAGR).

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Consolidated GAAP Operating Margin recovers steadily from the Q2 2026 low of 6.7% back to 11.5% by FY 2030 as upfront bad debt provisioning normalizes relative to maturing credit card balance run-rates and fulfillment route optimization lowers per-unit shipping costs. Maintenance CapEx scales linearly with fulfillment center additions and IT server capacity to $950M annually (~1.3% of net revenue). Stock-Based Compensation is capped at ~1.0% of revenue ($725M). Core Owner Earnings expand from baseline \(\text{OE}_0\) of $5,077M ($100.14/ADS) in FY 2025 to $11,100M ($218.93/ADS) by FY 2030, representing a robust 16.9% OE CAGR.

Key Milestones to Watch:

  • Sequential stabilization of GAAP Operating Margins above 8.5% in H2 2026 as upfront credit card provisioning normalizes.
  • Items sold per active buyer increasing from 7.2 to >8.5 annually across primary tier-1 metropolitan markets.
  • AdTech revenue growth maintaining a >35.0% YoY USD pace, contributing high-margin revenues that offset logistics fulfillment costs.

📖 Story 2: Bull Case — Ecosystem Dominance, AdTech Acceleration & Credit Scoring Monopoly (26.1% OE CAGR)

Narrative & Reinvestment Framing: In this high-upside operational scenario, MercadoLibre solidifies its status as the regional digital tollbridge for Latin American commerce and banking. The Q2 2026 margin compression (GAAP Operating Margin dipping to 6.7%) represents a decisive strategic offensive. By lowering free shipping thresholds and expanding credit card access, MELI starves regional competitors (Shopee, Amazon LatAm, local department stores) of scale and liquidity. The high-ROIC reinvestment cycle accelerates network effects: lower shipping thresholds trigger unprecedented order frequency, while upfront credit card provisioning creates an vast consumer wallet-share lock-in. As AI-driven underwriting slashes NPLs and AdTech adoption accelerates, MELI transforms into an ultra-high-margin digital platform compounder.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Active annual buyers accelerate to 195 million by FY 2030. GMV expands at a 25.0% USD CAGR to $155B, driven by dominant category position in electronics, apparel, and non-discretionary consumer packaged goods. Mercado Ads breaches 3.2% of GMV as brand advertisers shift major media budgets to intent-based marketplace search. Consolidated Net Revenue reaches $98,000M by FY 2030 (a 26.2% USD CAGR).

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: Operating margins expand dramatically from mid-2026 low levels to reach 15.5% by FY 2030, driven by the near-100% incremental margin of AdTech, merchant processing scale, and automated customer service workflows. 📖 Story 3: Bear Case — Macro Currency Collapse, Credit Stagflation & Tariff Friction (-5.6% OE CAGR)

Narrative & Reinvestment Framing: This stress scenario models a severe Latin American macroeconomic downturn characterized by aggressive currency devaluation (BRL depreciating 20%+, MXN depreciating 15%+ against the USD), combined with persistent local inflation and high interest rates. In this environment, the Q2 2026 margin compression down to 6.7% fails to generate expected operational leverage. Lowering free shipping thresholds increases logistics cash burn without driving incremental unit volume due to squeezed consumer purchasing power. Simultaneously, upfront credit card provisioning is compounded by rising consumer default rates, forcing heavy write-offs rather than yielding high-LTV customer cohorts.

Operational Metric Drivers & Revenue: Active annual buyers stagnate at ~130 million as macro pressures constrain consumer spending. While FX-neutral GMV grows 12.0% annually, severe currency headwinds reduce USD GMV growth to a 2.0% CAGR, resulting in $55B GMV by FY 2030. TPV growth slows to a 10.0% USD CAGR ($160B). In the credit book, weakening balance sheets force 15-to-90 day NPLs above 11.0%, depressing NIMAL to 11.0%. To mitigate solvency risk, management contracts the credit portfolio from $16.4B down to $11.0B. Consolidated Net Revenue reaches $42,000M by FY 2030 (a 6.5% USD CAGR).

Cost Dynamics, CapEx & Owner Earnings: High fixed operating costs in fulfillment infrastructure, elevated aircraft lease expenses, and elevated bad debt provisioning depress GAAP Operating Margins to 5.5%. Maintenance CapEx absorbs $650M annually to replace physical fulfillment equipment and hardware under high local inflation. Core Owner Earnings drop from baseline \(\text{OE}_0\) of $5,077M ($100.14/ADS) down to $3,800M ($74.95/ADS) by FY 2030, representing a -5.6% OE CAGR.

Key Milestones to Watch:

  • Consolidated NPLs (>90 days) exceeding 8.5% or NIMAL dropping below 14.0%.
  • USD-translated Net Revenues decelerating below 10.0% YoY.
  • FX depreciation in Brazil (BRL) or Mexico (MXN) exceeding 15.0% in a single fiscal year without proportional local price increases.
